## Q1. What do the current CRI Guidelines say on technical effect, technical contribution, novel hardware and AI/ML, and what did the 2025 revision add?

### Takeaway
The **CRI Guidelines 2025 (final, issued 29 July 2025)** replace the 2017 Guidelines and are now the operative examiner manual. They (a) apply a stepwise analysis to each 3(k) limb; (b) expressly drop "novel hardware" as a requirement, following *Raytheon* and *Microsoft*; (c) ask for a technical solution to a technical problem by technical means, giving a non-exhaustive list of recognised technical effects that includes **improved transmission/reception of communication signals, higher processing speed, reduced memory access, real-time monitoring/control of a device and enhanced network security**; and (d) add an AI/ML/DL chapter that ties eligibility to technical application and **sufficiency** to disclosure of model, training methodology and training-data characteristics. The guidelines are not binding law, and courts still apply the case-law tests directly.

### Cited Findings
- Final "Guidelines for Examination of Computer Related Inventions (CRIs) – 2025" listed on the IPO Examination Guidelines page with **Annexure I (additional illustrative examples)** and **Annexure II (non-exhaustive case law)**; public notice dated 29 July 2025; replaces 2017 version; drafts published March 2025 (v1) and June 2025 (v2) — [IP India guidelines page](https://www.ipindia.gov.in/Patents/guidelines_patents); [IP India public notice](https://ipindia.gov.in/uploads/52c49fb5-31ee-4630-bb7d-52bddc4fb827_Public%20Notice.pdf); [IAM](https://www.iam-media.com/article/indian-patent-office-releases-highly-anticipated-examination-guidelines-computer-related-inventions). Official PDF (not read): [CRI Guidelines 2025 PDF](https://ipindia.gov.in/frontend/pdf/patents/guidelines/GUIDELINES%20FOR%20EXAMINATION%20OF%20COMPUTER%20RELATED%20INVENTIONS%20(CRIs)%20-%202025.pdf).
- Structure (secondary): stepwise framework for each 3(k) limb (mathematical method, business method, computer programme per se, algorithm) with worked examples; a jurisprudence chapter; a chapter on AI, ML, DL, blockchain and quantum computing — [IP India search summary / Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=3c84cc89-5947-4dc8-be2b-762d2d6c9603); [ThePALaw](https://thepalaw.com/patent/whats-new-in-the-revised-2025-cri-guidelines-part-1/).
- **Novel hardware:** "Allowability does not require 'Novel Hardware', but a technical solution to a technical problem via technical means, achieving technical effects"; the guidelines confirm novel hardware is not mandatory, tracing this to *Raytheon* (2023), which held the requirement has no basis in law — [Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=3c84cc89-5947-4dc8-be2b-762d2d6c9603); [Intepat](https://www.intepat.com/blog/cri-guidelines-2025-patent-india-3k); [BananaIP (draft v2)](https://www.bananaip.com/intellepedia/cri-guidelines-2025-version-2/). Background: the **2016** Guidelines had required novel hardware; the **2017** Guidelines removed the three-step test that included it; the IPO nonetheless kept applying it in some refusals, leading to *Raytheon* and *Microsoft* reversals — [IAM (clash over novel hardware)](https://www.iam-media.com/article/delhi-high-court-and-indian-patent-office-clash-over-novel-hardware-features-requirement); [Depenning](https://depenning.com/news-and-insights/raytheon-vs-controller/).
- **Technical-effect list (non-exhaustive, "based on the interpretations of the Hon'ble Courts")**: higher processing speed; reduced hard-disk/memory access time; better control of a robotic arm; improved reception/transmission of a signal; real-time monitoring and control of a device; enhanced security in a computer network; efficient image/signal processing — [Bar & Bench](https://www.barandbench.com/law-firms/view-point/revised-cri-guidelines-2025); [Mondaq "3Ts of CRIs"](https://www.mondaq.com/india/patent/1730010/3ts-of-computer-related-inventions-and-beyond-analysis-of-section-3k-of-the-patents-act-1970). Another summary describes the examples as "improved hardware performance and reduced use of computational resources" — [Khurana & Khurana](https://www.khuranaandkhurana.com/patentability-of-cri-s-in-india-assessing-the-2025-ipo-guidelines). (One of these sources was summarising the draft; whether the final list is identical is unconfirmed.)
- The 2025 Guidelines quote *Ab Initio* (Delhi HC) for the idea that technical effect concerns the interaction between input and the processor/system — [search summary of Mycrave/commentary](https://www.mycrave.co.in/ip-knowledge-bank/software-patent-india-section-3k/) (secondary, weak).
- **AI/ML chapter (draft-era descriptions; final wording unverified):** for a trained model, the input-output correlation is "fully described" when (1) training data is explicitly identified, (2) a link between training-data characteristics and the technical problem is made, (3) the learning model and training methodology are comprehensively described, and (4) the trained model is shown to address the technical problem with predictable results — [Kan & Krishme (sufficiency, draft)](https://kankrishme.com/analyzing-the-draft-computer-related-inventions-cri-guidelines-2025-part-4-sufficiency-of-disclosure/); [Section3k](https://www.section3k.com/post/india-s-draft-cri-guidelines-2-0-2025-a-sharper-blueprint-for-patentability-in-the-age-of-ai). Stakeholders (BananaIP, NASSCOM) argued for disclosure of data *characteristics* rather than the datasets — [BananaIP](https://www.bananaip.com/intellepedia/comments-on-draft-cri-guidelines-ai-ml-blockchain/).
- Annexure I in the draft carried 20+ eligible/ineligible examples, e.g., ineligible: new pricing method or business model even if coded; eligible: network-security system preventing cookie theft in authentication — [Cyril Amarchand (draft)](https://corporate.cyrilamarchandblogs.com/2025/04/draft-guidelines-for-examination-of-computer-related-inventions-2025/); [Depenning (draft)](https://depenning.com/blog/draft-cri-guidelines/).
- AI-assisted (not AI-generated) inventions are not categorically excluded under 3(k) if they show technical effect through a tangible application — [K&S Partners](https://ksandk.com/corporate/why-ai-cannot-be-patent-inventor-india/).
- Guidelines are not legally binding; commentators flag risk of inconsistent application — [Khurana & Khurana](https://www.khuranaandkhurana.com/patentability-of-cri-s-in-india-assessing-the-2025-ipo-guidelines). Courts have nonetheless used the CRI Guidelines as a source of definitions of 3(k) terms — [SpicyIP (ANN Part II)](https://spicyip.com/2024/09/part-ii-artificial-neural-networks-are-they-mathematical-methods-or-computer-programmes-or-does-it-even-matter.html).
- Transitional point: in *Google LLC v Controller* (reported as **Calcutta HC, 6 Aug 2025**) the court upheld a 3(k) refusal on a 2014 application decided in 2020 under the **2017** Guidelines, without reference to the 2025 Guidelines — [SpicyIP](https://spicyip.com/2025/08/figuring-out-the-correct-cri-guidelines-by-looking-at-google-llc-v-the-controller-of-patents.html). (Court identity came from one commentary only.)

### Inferences
- The invention's core effects map directly onto items on the IPO's own list: ~88% less uplink per round is "improved transmission of signals / reduced use of computational and network resources"; no raw-data egress plus DP/secure aggregation is "enhanced security in a computer network"; link-stability-based selection under a bandwidth budget is "real-time monitoring and control of a device/network". The specification should use these phrases explicitly, because examiners work from the list.
- Since novel hardware is not required, the RSU and on-vehicle units can be conventional, but the claims should still recite them as the *technical means* that carry out the claimed steps and on which the effect is measured.
- Expect a sufficiency check alongside 3(k): describe the perception/trajectory model architecture, local training procedure, the aggregation weights formula, selection rule, sparsification/error-feedback operator, fp16+gzip pipeline, DP calibration, and data characteristics (sensor modalities, non-IID split across vehicles, data volumes).

## Q2. Key case law and the tests the courts apply

### Takeaway
The Delhi and Madras High Courts apply a **technical-effect / technical-contribution** test: a CRI is not excluded merely because it uses algorithms or runs on a general-purpose computer, provided it produces a technical effect or improves the functioning of a system or hardware; it is excluded when it is "a mere sequence of instructions" on conventional hardware with no demonstrable technical advancement, and business methods are excluded absolutely. Outcomes split on facts (*Kroll* and *Google* refusals upheld; *Raytheon*, *Microsoft*, *Comviva*, *Ab Initio*, *Priya Randolph* refusals set aside). No reported 2025–2026 Indian High Court judgment specifically on a neural-network or ML claim was found.

### Cited Findings
- ***Ferid Allani v Union of India*** (Delhi HC, Dec 2019): foundational; software-implemented inventions showing technical effect/contribution cannot be refused merely for being implemented in software; "per se" limits the exclusion — [SCC Online](https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2019/12/31/del-hc-technical-contribution-is-the-test-of-patentability-for-computer-programs-law-on-s-3k-of-patents-act-clarified/) (via background file); [search summary](https://www.mycrave.co.in/ip-knowledge-bank/software-patent-india-section-3k/).
- ***Raytheon Co v Controller General*** (Delhi HC, 15 Sept 2023, Prathiba M. Singh J., 2023:DHC:6673): HPC scheduling; refusal set aside because the Controller relied on the 2016 Guidelines' novel-hardware requirement, which is a higher standard without basis in law; examiner must test technical contribution/effect, and ask whether the invention goes beyond a series of instructions or "if-then-else iterations" — [SpicyIP](https://spicyip.com/2023/10/raytheon-company-v-controller-general-of-patents-and-designs-the-question-of-cri-and-3k.html); [Depenning](https://depenning.com/news-and-insights/raytheon-vs-controller/); [Intepat](https://www.intepat.com/blog/navigating-cris-insights-from-the-raytheon-case).
- ***Microsoft Technology Licensing v Assistant Controller*** — two separate decisions: (i) **Delhi HC 2023** (2023 SCC OnLine Del 2772): use of algorithms does not make an invention a programme per se; assess technical advancement and practical application (network-authentication security) — [SCC Online](https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2023/05/16/invention-not-to-be-deemed-a-computer-programme-per-se-solely-due-to-use-of-algorithms-legal-news/); [AIPPI](https://www.aippi.org/news/analysis-of-microsoft-technology-licensing-llc-vs-assistant-controller-of-patents-and-designs/); (ii) **Madras HC 2024**: 3(k) does not exclude all CRIs; relying on the parliamentary committee's "per se" reading, a CRI escapes if it produces a technical effect improving the system or solves a technical problem; criticised IPO use of outdated guidelines/novel hardware; the patent was later granted — [SpicyIP](https://spicyip.com/2024/07/madras-hc-on-computer-program-and-3k-following-the-dhc-way.html); [IAM](https://www.iam-media.com/article/microsoft-granted-computer-program-patent-following-madras-high-court-eligibility-ruling); [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/india/patent/1722104/use-of-algorithms-and-computer-programmes-with-technical-contribution-patentable-madras-high-court-rules).
- ***OpenTV Inc v Controller*** (Delhi HC, 2023 LiveLaw (Del) 400): "System and method to provide gift media" held a **business method**; appeal dismissed; the business-method bar is absolute (not qualified by "per se"), applied without technical-effect analysis; court called for Parliament to re-examine 3(k) exclusions — [AsiaIPLaw](https://asiaiplaw.com/sector/patents/india-the-delhi-high-courts-decision-in-opentv); [LiveLaw](https://www.livelaw.in/amp/high-court/delhi-high-court/delhi-high-court-patents-act-exclusions-innovations-228666); [Verdictum](https://www.verdictum.in/court-updates/high-courts/delhi-hc-patent-act-relook-at-exclusions-1476647).
- ***Priya Randolph v Deputy Controller*** (Madras HC, 20 Dec 2023): refusal set aside; a business-method element does not by itself bar patentability; invention combined hardware, software and firmware improving **data privacy and protection** — [Conventus Law / Cyril Amarchand](https://conventuslaw.com/report/india-scope-of-business-method-inventions-under-section-3k/).
- ***Telefonaktiebolaget LM Ericsson v Lava International*** (Delhi HC, 28 Mar 2024): 3(k) revocation counterclaim failed; question is whether algorithms are directed at enhancing the functionality of a system or hardware component; use of algorithms or computer-executable instructions does not by itself exclude; a general-purpose computer implementation with a further technical effect can be patentable — [BananaIP](https://bananaip.com/?p=101685); [AsiaIPLaw (seven decisions of 2024)](https://asiaiplaw.com/sector/patents/seven-key-decisions-of-2024-on-computer-related-inventions-and-their-impact-on-high-tech-innovations-in-india).
- ***Ab Initio Technology v Controller*** (Delhi HC, July/Aug 2024): data-lineage invention showed technical effect and structural features beyond a programme per se; refusal unsustainable — [Mondaq/SS Rana](https://mondaq.com/india/patent/1353616/patent-act-needs-to-be-re-examined-says-delhi-high-court); [SpicyIP Aug 2024](https://spicyip.com/2024/08/dhc-and-3k-this-time-using-the-technical-effect-test.html).
- ***BlackBerry Ltd v Controller*** ("auto-selection of media files", Delhi HC, 30 Aug 2024): exclusion applies only to inventions lacking technical effect/contribution; the court noted a technical implementation and impact on the user device — [Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=f858ad5c-fb7e-48e9-a234-37f2df3e72d2); **conflict:** the background file and Cyril Amarchand describe a same-period BlackBerry/Malikie decision holding that an invention that is "a series of instructions" managing data flow does not meet the threshold (refusal upheld) — [Cyril Amarchand (Sept 2024)](https://corporate.cyrilamarchandblogs.com/2024/09/invention-that-is-a-series-of-instructions-does-not-meet-the-criteria-for-patent-protection-under-section-3k-of-the-patents-act/). There may be two BlackBerry matters; check the orders.
- ***Comviva Technologies v Assistant Controller*** (Delhi HC, 12 Nov 2024): refusal under 3(k) (programme and business method) set aside; payment-authentication security improvement; transaction vocabulary does not make it a business method — [Verdictum](https://www.verdictum.in/columns/comviva-technologies-limited-vs-assistant-controller-of-patent-design-1562082) (via background file); [Lexology search summary](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=f858ad5c-fb7e-48e9-a234-37f2df3e72d2).
- ***Kroll Information Assurance v Controller*** (Delhi HC, 1 July 2025, 2025:DHC:5096): system for locating users on **P2P networks** refused; claims were "computer programme per se" and "algorithms" without "demonstrable technical advancement", employing standard computing components; software must be "more than a mere sequence of instructions"; s.59 amendment valid but did not cure 3(k) — [Verdictum](https://www.verdictum.in/court-updates/high-courts/delhi-high-court/kroll-information-assurance-llc-v-the-controller-general-of-patents-designs-and-trademarks-2025-dhc-5096-1583129); [BananaIP](https://www.bananaip.com/intellepedia/delhi-high-court-rejects-kroll-p2p-patent/); [Anand & Anand](https://patentsrewind.anandandanand.com/2025/11/13/delhi-high-court-on-section-59-and-section-3k-kroll-information-assurance-llc-vs-controller-of-patents/).
- ***Google LLC v Controller*** (reported Calcutta HC, 6 Aug 2025): refusal under 3(k) upheld under 2017 Guidelines — [SpicyIP](https://spicyip.com/2025/08/figuring-out-the-correct-cri-guidelines-by-looking-at-google-llc-v-the-controller-of-patents.html).
- 2026: Delhi HC set aside a 23 Jan 2026 refusal on natural-justice grounds (inventive-step objection raised without notice) without deciding merits; another Delhi HC ruling remanded for re-analysis of inventive step under the five-step *Hoffmann/Tapas* test — [Abhishek Gandhi](https://advocategandhi.com/delhi-high-court-sets-aside-patent-refusal-for-violating-natural-justice-by-raising-inventive-step-objection-without-notice/); [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/india/patent/1751324/order-for-refusal-of-patent-set-aside-delhi-high-court-directs-re-consideration-of-inventive-step-using-hoffman-test). Neither is reported as an AI case.
- Neural networks: SpicyIP's 2024 analysis treats ANN claims as a classification question (mathematical method vs computer programme) and notes the uncertainty — [SpicyIP Part II](https://spicyip.com/2024/09/part-ii-artificial-neural-networks-are-they-mathematical-methods-or-computer-programmes-or-does-it-even-matter.html).

### Inferences
- Consolidated test an Indian examiner/court will apply: (1) Is it a business method? (absolute bar — avoid framing the invention as fleet data monetisation or service pricing). (2) Is it a mathematical method/algorithm as such? (3) Does the claimed subject matter, read as a whole, produce a technical effect or contribution (improve functioning of the system, network or hardware; solve a technical problem) beyond normal execution of instructions on a computer? (4) Is that effect demonstrable rather than asserted?
- The *Kroll* contrast is the closest risk for this invention: Kroll was a **network** (P2P) data-processing scheme on standard components and still failed because no demonstrable technical advancement was shown. The FL framework must therefore present measured network-level effects (uplink bytes, rounds, link utilisation) as the contribution, not merely "a better way to aggregate".
- *Ericsson v Lava* and *Microsoft* support the framing that algorithms directed at enhancing the functioning of a communications system (here the V2I uplink and RSU) are patentable.
- *Priya Randolph* and *Comviva* support privacy/security of data transmission as a technical effect.

- **Avery Dennison** (cited in the brief): no search result located a 3(k) decision under that name; unverified.
- No judgment text read; no 2025–2026 Indian HC decision specifically on ML/neural networks/FL found.
- The BlackBerry outcome conflict (above) is unresolved.

## Q4. Which technical effects are recognised and what evidence helps establish them

### Takeaway
Recognised: improved signal transmission/reception, faster processing, reduced memory/resource use, real-time device/network control, enhanced network security and efficient signal/image processing (CRI 2025 list); courts additionally accepted improved functioning of a system/hardware (*Ericsson v Lava*), authentication/network security (*Microsoft*, *Comviva*), data privacy via hardware/software combination (*Priya Randolph*), and HPC scheduling efficiency (*Raytheon*). Effects must be **demonstrable**, not asserted (*Kroll*).

### Cited Findings
- CRI 2025 technical-effect list — [Bar & Bench](https://www.barandbench.com/law-firms/view-point/revised-cri-guidelines-2025); [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/india/patent/1730010/3ts-of-computer-related-inventions-and-beyond-analysis-of-section-3k-of-the-patents-act-1970).
- Effect demonstrated only by "demonstrable technical advancement", and standard components plus instructions are not enough — [Verdictum (Kroll)](https://www.verdictum.in/court-updates/high-courts/delhi-high-court/kroll-information-assurance-llc-v-the-controller-general-of-patents-designs-and-trademarks-2025-dhc-5096-1583129).
- Draft AI chapter: trained model must be shown to address the technical problem with predictable results — [Kan & Krishme](https://kankrishme.com/analyzing-the-draft-computer-related-inventions-cri-guidelines-2025-part-4-sufficiency-of-disclosure/).
- Practitioner guidance: identify the technical effect and argue/amend claims in terms of technical contribution at FER/hearing stage — [PatenEvo](https://patenevo.in/understanding-the-first-examination-report-fer-and-how-to-respond/); [Patent Attorney Worldwide](https://patentattorneyworldwide.com/in/understanding-section-3k-of-the-indian-patent-act-and-strategies-to-overcome-it-in-patent-drafting-and-prosecution/).
- Commentators differ on whether a *measurable* effect is demanded by the 2025 guidelines (background file, Q1 India).

### Inferences
- Map each measured result to a listed effect and put the numbers in the specification as worked examples with baseline: (a) uplink bytes/round vs FedAvg (~88% reduction) at equal accuracy target, and rounds-to-target; (b) per-round bandwidth-budget compliance and link utilisation on the V2I channel (dropped/failed uploads vs link-stability selection); (c) worst-vehicle accuracy and robustness when sensors are degraded (sensor-quality-aware weights) — an "efficient signal/image processing" and reliability effect; (d) no raw-data egress plus DP epsilon / secure aggregation — "enhanced security in a computer network"; (e) on-vehicle compute/energy and RSU aggregation latency if measured.
- Effects that are *only* model accuracy (a better number on a benchmark) are weaker in India, as elsewhere; tie them to system behaviour (e.g., perception reliability of the vehicle).
- Evidence can be added later: Indian practice allows affidavits/data at FER response and hearing (see AIPPI note on evidence at different stages) — [AIPPI](https://www.aippi.org/news/evidence-at-different-stages-under-the-indian-patents-act/); but claimed effects should be present in the specification at filing to avoid s.59 problems with later amendments (*Kroll*, *OpenTV* both discussed s.59).

### Gaps
- No Indian decision found on whether post-filing experimental data can establish a technical effect not described in the specification.
- Energy-use and faulty-sensor robustness were not found as named examples in Indian sources.

## Q5. Practical drafting advice for this invention

### Takeaway
Draft so that every independent claim (method, system, CRM) is anchored in the vehicle on-board unit, the V2I radio link and the RSU, recites the specific mechanism that produces the bandwidth/robustness effect, and is supported by measured results against FedAvg in the specification. The 2025 Guidelines warn that if *any* claim category falls within the exclusion the subject matter fails, so the CRM claim must be as technically anchored as the method.

### Cited Findings
- If any claim form (method/apparatus/CRM) falls in the exclusion, the subject matter is not patentable; algorithms must be technically enabled; business methods remain excluded — [Intepat](https://www.intepat.com/blog/cri-guidelines-2025-patent-india-3k); [Fox Mandal](https://foxmandal.in/patenting-ai-in-india-patent-office-revises-its-cri-guidelines/) (via background file).
- Applicants should state the technical problem and solution and show a contribution beyond normal computer functioning — [Intepat AI/ML](https://www.intepat.com/blog/ai-machine-learning-patents-patentable-india) (via background file).
- Disclosure of model architecture details (network type, layers, activation functions) recommended by commentators — [Intepat (AI IP)](https://www.intepat.com/blog/intellectual-property/artificial-intelligence-ipr/); weak LinkedIn sources say the same.
- Claim amendments during prosecution are limited by s.59 (must be within the scope of the original claims/disclosure) — [AsiaIPLaw (OpenTV)](https://asiaiplaw.com/sector/patents/india-the-delhi-high-courts-decision-in-opentv); [Anand & Anand (Kroll)](https://patentsrewind.anandandanand.com/2025/11/13/delhi-high-court-on-section-59-and-section-3k-kroll-information-assurance-llc-vs-controller-of-patents/).

### Inferences (drafting recommendations synthesised from the above)
- **Title/abstract/field:** "System and method for bandwidth-constrained federated training of vehicle perception models over vehicle-to-infrastructure links" — field: vehicular communication networks and autonomous-vehicle perception. Avoid titles like "federated learning algorithm" or "method for aggregating model weights".
- **Technical problem statement:** limited and time-varying V2I uplink capacity and RSU contact windows; heterogeneous and degraded vehicle sensors; prohibition/risk of raw sensor-data egress. Solution: the specific combination of link-stability/coverage-based selection, sensor-quality-aware aggregation and budgeted top-k + error-feedback + fp16 + gzip encoding.
- **Independent claims (suggested set):**
  1. *RSU-side method*: receiving, via a V2I transceiver, link-quality reports and trajectory/region metadata from vehicles in range; selecting a subset based on predicted link stability over the round and region coverage; allocating a per-round uplink bandwidth budget; receiving compressed sparse model deltas; decoding; aggregating with weights computed from reported sensor-quality indicators; transmitting the updated global model to vehicles. 
  2. *Vehicle on-board-unit method*: capturing sensor data with on-board camera/LiDAR/radar; training the perception/trajectory model locally; computing a delta; applying top-k sparsification with an error-feedback residual stored in vehicle memory; choosing k so the encoded payload fits the allocated budget; quantising to fp16 and entropy-compressing; transmitting over the V2I link without transmitting raw sensor data; updating the vehicle's perception module with the received model.
  3. *System claim*: RSU (processor, memory, V2I transceiver, aggregation module) + plural vehicles (sensors, OBU processor, memory storing residual, transceiver) configured as above.
  4. *CRM claim*: non-transitory medium with instructions that, when executed by a processor of an RSU/OBU, cause the steps above — recite the same hardware hooks.
- **Dependent claims:** DP noise calibration and clipping; secure aggregation masks; sensor-quality indicator definitions (e.g., calibration residuals, occlusion rate, detection confidence); link-stability prediction from RSSI/SINR, relative velocity and dwell time; region-coverage criterion; fallback when a vehicle exits coverage mid-upload; specific compression ratios and budget values supported by the experiments.
- **Avoid** claims that are only equations (aggregation formula alone), "a neural network trained to…", or generic "server/client" language; keep formulas in the description and dependent claims, with the effect stated in the independent claim's operational steps (e.g., "such that the encoded payload does not exceed the uplink budget allocated to the vehicle").
- **Describe the ML model concretely:** architecture of the perception/trajectory model (backbone, heads, parameter count), local optimiser and hyperparameter ranges, number of local steps, data characteristics (sensor modality, number of vehicles, non-IID split, data volume per vehicle; synthetic/simulated or real), and say the scheme is model-agnostic only if experiments support it.
- **Evidence section:** a results table vs FedAvg (and ideally vs plain top-k and random selection as ablations) with uplink bytes/round, total bytes to target accuracy, mean and worst-vehicle accuracy, effect of degraded-sensor vehicles, wall-clock/round time on the RSU, and privacy parameters; describe the prototype hardware/simulator.
- **Avoid business framing** (OpenTV absolute bar): do not describe the benefit as fleet operator cost savings or a data-sharing service model.
