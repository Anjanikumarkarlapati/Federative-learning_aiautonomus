## 1. Which forms are required, what each contains, when each is due, and what the Patents (Amendment) Rules 2024 changed

### Takeaway
A minimum Indian filing by individual applicants consists of Form 1 (application), Form 2 (provisional or complete specification), Form 3 (foreign-filing statement and undertaking), Form 5 (inventorship declaration, with the complete specification) and Form 18 (request for examination, within 31 months). Add Form 26 if an agent files, Form 9 for early publication, Form 18A for expedited examination (only if eligible), Form 28 only for startup/small-entity/educational-institution fee status, and Form 31 if a s.31 grace-period disclosure has already happened. The 2024 Rules (in force 15 Mar 2024) cut the request-for-examination window from 48 to 31 months, changed Form 3 timing, created Form 31 (Rule 29A) and Form 8A (Rule 70A, certificate of inventorship), widened divisional filing (Rule 13(2A)), made Form 27 working statements triennial, and allowed late requests for extension (Form 4).

### Cited Findings
**Core forms and their timing**
- Form 1 = application for grant; Form 2 = specification (Rule 13(1): "every specification, whether provisional or complete, shall be made in Form 2") — [IP India Rule 13](https://ipindia.gov.in/writereaddata/Portal/ev/rules/pr13.html); [Banana IP abstract guidelines](https://www.bananaip.com/intellepedia/indian-patent-office-abstract-writing-guidelines-technical-requirements/)
- Form 5 names the "true and first inventors"; an application may be filed by the true and first inventor(s) or by an assignee who has acquired the right from them — [Banana IP filing requirements](https://www.bananaip.com/intellepedia/?p=1336) (as cited in background notes)
- Form 3 (2024 change): filed with the application or within six months of it, then an updated Form 3 within three months of the First Examination Report (FER); the Controller may require a fresh Form 3 within two months of a communication, extendable/condonable by up to three months via Form 4 — [Mondaq 1445504 summary](https://www.mondaq.com/india/patent/1445504/patent-amendment-rules-2024) (via background notes); [Conventus/Mondaq on 2024 Rules](https://conventuslaw.com/report/india-announces-progressive-changes-in-patents-rules-2003/)
- Form 18 (request for examination) deadline is 31 months from the earlier of priority date or filing date (cut from 48 months), effective 15 Mar 2024 — [Banana IP 2024 Rules](https://www.bananaip.com/intellepedia/patent-amendment-rules-2024-key-changes-india/); [Mondaq 1442682](https://www.mondaq.com/india/patent/1442682/patent-amendment-rules-2024-key-changes). Conflict: one search summary said some sources limit the 31-month rule to PCT national-phase applications with priority from 15 Mar 2024; the majority view is that it applies to all applications — [Intepat e-filing guide](https://www.intepat.com/blog/patent-e-filing-india-step-by-step-guide); [incorpx 2026 fee comparison](https://www.incorpx.io/blog/patent-filing-fees-india-2026-startup-vs-company)
- Missing the Form 18 deadline makes the application "deemed withdrawn" under s.11B(4); examination starts only after publication even if Form 18 is filed earlier; Form 9 (early publication) leads to publication within about one month — [S.S. Rana on examination request](https://ssrana.in/ufaqs/request-for-examination-in-india/) (via background notes)
- Form 26 = authorisation of a patent agent or any person in a proceeding under the Act; Form 6 = claim or request regarding a change in applicant; Form 13 = amendment of the application or complete specification — [IP India forms & official fees page](https://www.ipindia.gov.in/patents-before-you-apply-forms-official-fees); [sample Form 26 (R.K. Dewan)](https://www.rkdewan.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/FORM-26.pdf)
- Form 28 is the vehicle for claiming startup / small-entity / educational-institution status, with proof (DPIIT recognition for startups, MSME registration for small entities) — [Intepat on startup/small entity](https://www.intepat.com/blog/foreign-applicant-startup-small-entity-indian-patent) (via background notes)

**2024 amendments (Patents (Amendment) Rules 2024, notified and effective 15 Mar 2024)**
- Effective from publication, 15 Mar 2024 — [Banana IP](https://www.bananaip.com/intellepedia/patent-amendment-rules-2024-key-changes-india/); Gazette text: [Gazette of India Part II Sec 3(i), hosted by Sanda Law](https://sandalawoffices.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/Patent_Amendment_Rule_2024_Gazette_.pdf)
- New Rule 29A / Form 31: procedure to claim the s.31 12-month grace period after a qualifying disclosure; fee INR 500 (e-filing, natural persons/startups/small entities/educational institutions) and INR 2,500 for others; physical INR 550 / 2,750 — [LiveLaw (S.S. Rana) on 2024 Rules](https://www.livelaw.in/law-firms/law-firm-articles-/patent-rules-ipr-ss-rana-co-253592); [Abhijit Bhand on Form 31](https://abhijitbhand.com/blog/form-31-grace-period); [IP India First Schedule PDF](https://ipindia.gov.in/frontend/pdf/forms_and_official_fees/Schedule_1.pdf)
- New Rule 70A / Form 8A: an inventor may request a certificate of inventorship (after grant); fee INR 900 e-filing / INR 1,000 physical; it is declaratory only and does not affect ownership — [Abhijit Bhand on Form 8A](https://abhijitbhand.com/blog/form-8a-certificate-of-inventorship); [IP India First Schedule PDF](https://ipindia.gov.in/frontend/pdf/forms_and_official_fees/Schedule_1.pdf). One blog lists INR 900 for small applicants only and the schedule for others (conflict, minor).
- Divisionals: new Rule 13(2A) lets the applicant file one or more divisional applications based on an invention disclosed in the provisional, the complete specification, or an earlier divisional — [Conventus/Mondaq on 2024 Rules](https://conventuslaw.com/report/india-announces-progressive-changes-in-patents-rules-2003/); [De Penning 2024 highlights](https://depenning.com/news-and-insights/key-highlights-the-patent-amendment-rules-2024/)
- Extension of time (Form 4): the request may now be filed after the original deadline but before the extended period ends (e.g., FER reply 6 months + up to 3 months, request can be made up to month 9) — [Conventus/Mondaq on 2024 Rules](https://conventuslaw.com/report/india-announces-progressive-changes-in-patents-rules-2003/); [De Penning](https://depenning.com/?p=3845)
- Form 27 (statement of working) is now filed once every three financial years (Rule 131(2)), within six months of the end of each period, extendable by three months on Form 4; it now asks whether the patent is worked and whether it is available for licensing — [Mondaq 1442682](https://www.mondaq.com/india/patent/1442682/patent-amendment-rules-2024-key-changes); [Banana IP](https://www.bananaip.com/intellepedia/patent-amendment-rules-2024-key-changes-india/)
- 10% renewal-fee reduction where renewals are paid at least four years in advance, electronically (sources conflict on who qualifies) — background notes, [Mondaq 1445504](https://www.mondaq.com/india/patent/1445504/patent-amendment-rules-2024)

### Inferences
- Deliverable list for this FL-AV invention, filed by student inventors as applicants with no agent: Form 1 + Form 2 (provisional) now; then within 12 months Form 2 (complete) + Form 5 (+ Form 3 if not filed earlier, inside six months of filing) + optionally Form 9 and Form 18 together with the complete specification. Adding Form 18 at the complete-specification stage avoids the 31-month trap.
- If an agent or attorney files, add Form 26 (a general authorisation can be reused). If a university is an applicant, it qualifies for the reduced "educational institution" fee only via Form 28 with proof, and mixed applicant sets (e.g., a student plus a company) lose the reduction (see background notes).
- Form 8A is post-grant and optional: useful for students' CVs, as it gives formal recognition of inventorship separate from ownership.

### Gaps
- Exact Form 4 extension fees conflict (INR 2,000 small applicants / INR 10,000 others vs "per month" figures); not verified against the First Schedule.
- Whether Rule 13(2A) also allows a divisional to be "out of a divisional" was described only by secondary summaries, which agree that it does.
- Whether Form 3 must be refiled at each foreign filing after FER beyond the three-month window was not confirmed.

## 2. Provisional vs complete specification: Form 2 structure, 12-month deadline, s.10(4)

### Takeaway
A provisional Form 2 needs only a title and a description, with drawings optional. A complete Form 2 must have the title, field, background (prior art), objects/summary, brief description of drawings, a detailed description including the best method, claims ("We claim:") and a separate abstract of at most 150 words. The complete specification is due within 12 months of the provisional, or the application is deemed abandoned. Section 10(4) requirements (full and particular description, best method, claims fairly based, abstract) are mandatory.

### Cited Findings
- s.9(1): complete specification within 12 months of the provisional, or the application is deemed abandoned. s.9(2) lets one complete specification cover cognate provisionals; s.9(4) allows post-dating to the complete. A reported 15-month proviso is disputed (one source has it, the IP India text retrieved omits it) — background notes citing [IP India s.9](https://ipindia.gov.in/writereaddata/Portal/ev/sections/ps9.html); [Intepat](https://www.intepat.com/blog/patent/can-draft-patent-specification/)
- s.10(4)-(5): full and particular description of the invention and its operation, the best method known to the applicant, claims defining the scope of a single invention that are clear, succinct and fairly based on the disclosure, and an abstract — [IP India s.10](https://ipindia.gov.in/writereaddata/Portal/ev/sections/ps10.html)
- The Delhi HC reads "shall" in s.10 as making every s.10(4)/(5) requirement mandatory. The IPAB held sufficiency is met if at least one way of working the invention is clearly described, together with the best method known; not every conceivable mode is needed — [Mondaq on sufficiency](https://webiis08.mondaq.com/india/patent/1139820/meeting-sufficiency-of-disclosure-requirements-under-the-indian-patent-act-1970); [Asia IP on sufficiency](https://asiaiplaw.com/section/ip-analysts/sufficient-disclosure-in-patent-specifications-the-india-perspective)
- Where ranges or multiple embodiments are claimed, working examples covering the breadth of the claims may be required (mostly chemistry/biotech) — [Managing IP on experimental data](https://managingip.com/article/b1tq4vvgjjgph0/too-much-information-or-not-quite-enough-experimental-data-in-indian-patent-applications); [Mondaq on sufficiency](https://webiis08.mondaq.com/india/patent/1139820/meeting-sufficiency-of-disclosure-requirements-under-the-indian-patent-act-1970)
- Abstract (Rule 13(7)): begins with the title; states the technical field, the technical problem, the solution and its principal use; max 150 words including the figure reference; it must indicate a reference figure, and features in that figure carry reference numerals in the abstract. A defective abstract gets three months for amendment and holds up publication — [Banana IP abstract guidelines (IPO 2009 procedure)](https://www.bananaip.com/intellepedia/indian-patent-office-abstract-writing-guidelines-technical-requirements/); [IP India Rule 13](https://ipindia.gov.in/writereaddata/Portal/ev/rules/pr13.html)
- Title: on the first page, ideally under about 15 words, without inventor names or the word "patent"; convention, PCT national-phase and divisional applications cannot use the provisional route — [Intepat on drafting](https://www.intepat.com/blog/patent/can-draft-patent-specification/) (via background notes)
- The 2025 CRI Guidelines (final, reported effective 29 July 2025, superseding 2017) expect the specification to tie the claimed subject matter to the technical problem and a concrete technical effect (e.g., reduced computational load, faster processing, lower memory) — [Conventus analysis of CRI 2025](https://conventuslaw.com/report/india-a-detailed-analysis-of-indias-new-cri-guidelines-2025/); [Banana IP CRI 2025](https://www.bananaip.com/intellepedia/cri-patent-guidelines-2025-ai-algorithms/). Conflict: some sources still describe the March 2025 draft or a "Version 2.0" draft — [Banana IP draft v2.0](https://www.bananaip.com/intellepedia/?p=141588)

### Inferences
- Recommended Form 2 (complete) heading order for this invention: Title -> Field of the invention -> Background/prior art (FedAvg, FedProx, client selection, gradient compression, DP-FL, and their limits for V2I) -> Objects of the invention -> Summary -> Brief description of drawings -> Detailed description with the best method (RSU aggregation loop; quality-aware weighting formula; connectivity/trajectory-aware selection with dwell-time prediction; top-k with error-feedback residual; DP clipping/noise + secure aggregation; concrete parameter values) -> Experimental results -> Claims ("We claim:" on a new page) -> Abstract (separate page, ≤150 words, "Fig. 1"). The end of the claims carries date and signature.
- For a CRI, the "best method" should give concrete hyperparameters (k fraction, staleness/quality weight formula, selection threshold on predicted RSU dwell time, DP epsilon/delta, clipping norm) and pseudo-code. That satisfies s.10(4) without filing source code.
- Write the provisional as fully as the complete, because claims not fairly based on it take the later date.

### Gaps
- The Manual of Patent Office Practice and Procedure chapter on the specification was not retrieved. The Form 2 heading list above is drawn from practice and secondary sources, not a mandatory rule text, apart from Rule 13(7) for the abstract.
- The s.9(1) 15-month extension conflict is unresolved.

## 3. Drawings rules (Rule 15) for software/flowcharts

### Takeaway
Drawings go on A4 with clear margins of at least 4 cm top and left and 3 cm bottom and right, with no dimensions, sheets numbered (e.g., "1/6"), applicant name at top left, and signature at bottom right. No descriptive matter is allowed except in flow diagrams, and reference numerals tie each element to the text. Black-and-white line drawings, block diagrams and flowcharts are the norm for a CRI.

### Cited Findings
- Rule 15: standard A4 sheets with a clear margin of at least 4 cm on the top and left and 3 cm at the bottom and right; dimensions not marked; scale large enough to show the invention clearly; at least one copy neatly drawn on durable paper; sheets numbered consecutively; applicant's name at top left — [IP India Rule 15](https://ipindia.gov.in/writereaddata/portal/ev/rules/pr15.html); [IP India Rule 15 (new site)](https://www.ipindia.gov.in/acts/patent-rules-2003/rule-15)
- No descriptive matter on drawing sheets except flow diagrams where required (secondary source; the official text was truncated in retrieval) — [iPleaders on patent drawings](https://blog.ipleaders.in/patent-drawing-importance/)
- Each element should carry a reference numeral, with a corresponding description in the specification — [Kanakkupillai patent drawing overview](https://www.kanakkupillai.com/patent-drawing)
- The abstract must name a reference figure, with reference signs — [Banana IP](https://www.bananaip.com/intellepedia/indian-patent-office-abstract-writing-guidelines-technical-requirements/)

### Inferences
- Suggested drawing set (6–8 sheets): Fig.1 system architecture (vehicles 100a-n, OBU, V2I link, RSU 200 with aggregator, selector, compressor/decoder, DP/secure-agg module, optional cloud); Fig.2 per-round sequence/flow; Fig.3 client-selection flowchart (connectivity + trajectory/dwell-time prediction); Fig.4 quality-aware weighting flowchart; Fig.5 top-k + error-feedback flowchart on the vehicle; Fig.6 DP clipping/noise + secure-aggregation masking; Fig.7 hardware block diagram (processor, memory, transceiver) to support "system" claims; Figs. 8+ experimental results (accuracy vs rounds, bytes per round), as plain B/W line graphs.
- Use black lines on white, no colour, no shading that will not reproduce, and text in boxes kept short (allowed for flow diagrams).

### Gaps
- Rule 15's full text on signatures/sheet marking and the Manual's drawing chapter were not retrieved; whether IP India accepts greyscale plots of experimental results was not confirmed (practice suggests line graphs are accepted).

## 4. Claims drafting conventions; claim/page fees

### Takeaway
India accepts independent method and system claims for a CRI with a demonstrated technical effect. "Computer-readable medium" and "computer program product" claims are reported as still objectionable under the 2025 CRI Guidelines as programs per se, so put them last or omit them. The two-part "characterised in that" form is optional. The basic Form 1 fee covers 30 pages and 10 claims, with surcharges beyond that.

### Cited Findings
- s.3(k) excludes "a mathematical or business method or a computer programme per se or algorithms"; claims survive with a concrete, reproducible technical effect on a technical system — [Conventus CRI 2025](https://conventuslaw.com/report/india-a-detailed-analysis-of-indias-new-cri-guidelines-2025/); [Intepat on AI/ML patentability](https://www.intepat.com/blog/ai-machine-learning-patents-patentable-india)
- CRM and computer-program-product claims are reported as remaining non-patentable or caught by "computer programme per se" — [Conventus CRI 2025](https://conventuslaw.com/report/india-a-detailed-analysis-of-indias-new-cri-guidelines-2025/); [Mondaq CRI 2025 overview](https://webiis10.mondaq.com/india/patent/1640462/computer-related-inventions-guidelines-2025-overview)
- Means-plus-function claims need corresponding structure disclosed in the specification; claims must satisfy unity (s.10(5)) and be fairly based — [Conventus CRI 2025](https://conventuslaw.com/report/india-a-detailed-analysis-of-indias-new-cri-guidelines-2025/)
- Both independent method and system claims are allowed if supported — background notes citing Conventus
- Fee for Form 1 covers up to 30 pages and 10 claims; extra page INR 160 (small applicants) / INR 800 (others); extra claim INR 320 / INR 1,600 (e-filing) — background notes (practitioner fee tables)

### Inferences
- Suggested claim set within 10 claims (no excess fee): claim 1 method at the RSU (receive compressed updates from selected vehicles over V2I; reconstruct; compute quality-aware weights; aggregate; broadcast), with selection based on predicted link quality/dwell time; claims 2–6 dependents (error-feedback top-k; DP clipping + Gaussian noise; secure-aggregation masks; weight formula; staleness handling); claim 7 RSU system (processor, memory, V2I transceiver configured to...); claim 8 vehicle-side method/OBU; claims 9–10 system dependents. Stay at or under 30 pages to avoid page surcharges, or budget INR 160/page.
- Draft the effect into claim 1 (e.g., bandwidth per round reduced by transmitting only the k largest coordinates with a residual memory; robustness to dropout via dwell-time-aware selection), since "applying ML to driving data" alone will draw a s.3(k) objection.
- "Characterised in that" is a European style; Indian examiners accept it but do not require it (no source found requiring it).

### Gaps
- No source specifically addressed "characterised in that" in Indian CRI practice; the official CRI 2025 guideline PDF was not opened.

## 5. Official fees, expedited examination and timelines

### Takeaway
For natural persons (e-filing, 2024 schedule as reported): Form 1 INR 1,600; Form 9 INR 2,500; Form 18 INR 4,000; Form 18A INR 8,000; Form 31 INR 500; Form 8A INR 900; Form 13 INR 800; renewal years 3–6 INR 800/yr. Physical filing costs about 10% more. Expedited examination is available only on Rule 24C grounds (startup, small entity, at least one female natural-person applicant, government entity, India as ISA/IPEA, etc.), so an all-male team of student individuals does not qualify unless a co-applicant is a woman or the team files through a DPIIT startup. Actual average time to FER is about 5.7 months expedited vs about 24.7 months ordinary; the FER reply is due within 6 months, extendable by 3 months.

## 6. Is a working model, prototype or source code required? What evidence can be submitted?

### Takeaway
No. A model or sample is furnished only if the Controller requires it (s.10(3), Rule 16), and it never forms part of the specification. Source code is not filed. Experimental results, pseudo-code and parameter tables can and should go into the description to support sufficiency and technical effect.

### Cited Findings
- s.10(3): the Controller may require a model or sample to supplement the application, to be furnished before the application is in order for grant, but it "shall not be deemed to form part of the specification"; Rule 16: models or samples are furnished only when required by the Controller — [IP India s.10](https://ipindia.gov.in/writereaddata/Portal/ev/sections/ps10.html); [Mondaq on models and samples](https://webiis08.mondaq.com/india/patent/1136566/explaining-inventions-better-models-and-samples-in-indian-patent-applications)
- The Delhi HC (Titan Umreifungstechnik v. Assistant Controller) held that the absence of working examples alone does not justify rejection, though the Controller may ask for workings supporting the claims — search summary citing [Conventus on enablement (Delhi HC)](https://conventuslaw.com/report/india-enablement-requirements-redefined-by-the-delhi-high-court/)
- Best mode can be shown through working examples — [Mondaq on sufficiency](https://webiis08.mondaq.com/india/patent/1139820/meeting-sufficiency-of-disclosure-requirements-under-the-indian-patent-act-1970)
- The 2025 CRI guidelines look for demonstrable technical effect (e.g., reduced computation or bandwidth) — [Conventus CRI 2025](https://conventuslaw.com/report/india-a-detailed-analysis-of-indias-new-cri-guidelines-2025/)

### Inferences
- Include in the specification: a simulation setup (CARLA/SUMO-type or dataset, number of vehicles/RSUs), baselines (FedAvg, random selection, no compression), and metrics (accuracy, rounds-to-target, uplink bytes, dropout rate, privacy budget). These directly evidence the technical effect for s.3(k), and can be cited in FER replies. Keep a dated, unpublished copy of the code and logs as private evidence; there is no need to file it.
- Results added later than the provisional cannot support new claims retroactively, so put at least preliminary results in the provisional.

### Gaps
- No Indian source specific to software sufficiency (pseudo-code vs flowchart) was found.

## 7. Inventorship, applicant vs inventor, assignment, AI assistance, university ownership

### Takeaway
Inventors must be natural persons. The Indian Patent Office refused the DABUS application on 15 Apr 2026 because an AI cannot be an inventor. Human inventors who used AI tools should be named for their own conceptual contribution to the claims. Applicants may be the inventors themselves or assignees (a university or company) with proof of right. Form 1 has a declaration by inventors that can serve as consent/assignment, Form 5 declares inventorship, and Form 6 records a later change in applicant. University/funder policies typically decide ownership; India has no default employer-ownership statute.

### Cited Findings
- 15 Apr 2026: the IPO refused Application No. 202017019068 (DABUS, Thaler) under s.15; inventors must be natural or legally recognised persons; owning the AI system does not make one an inventor; the invention was also held obvious. It is a Controller's order, not a court judgment, and leaves open how much human contribution suffices for AI-assisted inventions — [SpicyIP on DABUS refusal](https://spicyip.com/2026/04/the-inventor-is-still-human-indian-patent-offices-dabus-refusal.html); [K&S on DABUS](https://ksandk.com/intellectual-property-rights/ai-cannot-be-inventor-dabus-patent-application-india/); [EU IP Helpdesk news](https://intellectual-property-helpdesk.ec.europa.eu/news-events/news/indias-patent-office-rejects-ai-inventor-landmark-dabus-decision-2026-07-27_en); [Intepat USPTO vs India](https://www.intepat.com/blog/uspto-ai-assisted-inventorship-guidance-vs-india-dabus-position)
- Applicant may be the true and first inventor or an assignee; Form 5 names the inventors — [Banana IP](https://www.bananaip.com/intellepedia/?p=1336) (via background notes)
- Form 6 = change in applicant (e.g., after assignment) — [IP India forms page](https://www.ipindia.gov.in/patents-before-you-apply-forms-official-fees)
- Form 8A certificate of inventorship is declaratory and does not affect ownership — [Abhijit Bhand](https://abhijitbhand.com/blog/form-8a-certificate-of-inventorship)
- India has no default employer/employee allocation; a written assignment is needed; ownership follows funding agreement and institute policy (e.g., DBT requires assignment to the institution/DBT; IIT royalty-sharing schemes) — background notes citing [Khurana & Khurana](https://www.khuranaandkhurana.com/patent-ownership-in-employee-inven) and [SpicyIP](https://spicyip.com)

### Inferences
- Before filing, students should check their institution's IPR policy (many claim ownership where institute resources or funding were used). If the institution owns the invention, it is applicant (Form 28 educational-institution fee reduction), with students as inventors in Form 5. Otherwise the students are applicants in their own name (natural-person fees).
- Keep an inventorship record mapping each claim element to its human contributor; record AI-tool use (e.g., code assistants) as tools, not inventors.

## 8. Pre-filing disclosure (s.29–31) and foreign filing (s.39, Form 25)

### Takeaway
India has no general grace period. Publishing a paper, preprint (arXiv), GitHub repo or demo before the Indian filing date anticipates the invention (s.29), except for the narrow s.31 cases (notified exhibition, a paper read before a learned society, unauthorised disclosure), claimed with Form 31 within 12 months. File first, then publish. Indian residents must not file abroad (or PCT at a foreign RO) without first filing in India and waiting six weeks with no secrecy direction, or obtaining Form 25 permission.

### Cited Findings
- s.31 covers only a notified exhibition (and publications arising from it), unauthorised use, and a paper read before / published in the transactions of a learned society, each with a 12-month window; ordinary journal/conference papers and preprints are not covered — background notes ([patent_timing_disclosure.md](../Patent%20ready%20research%20paper%202026/patent_timing_disclosure.md))
- Form 31 procedure under Rule 29A, INR 500 for natural persons — [LiveLaw (S.S. Rana)](https://www.livelaw.in/law-firms/law-firm-articles-/patent-rules-ipr-ss-rana-co-253592); [Abhijit Bhand](https://abhijitbhand.com/blog/form-31-grace-period)
- s.39: Indian residents need either a first Indian filing plus six weeks without a s.35 secrecy direction, or Form 25 permission (Controller to dispose within 21 days); breach risks revocation (s.40) and penalties up to two years' imprisonment or fine (s.118) — background notes

### Inferences
- An AV/V2I federated-learning invention could plausibly touch defence-relevant communication, but secrecy directions are rare for civilian ML; the six-week wait after the Indian provisional is the simplest route to PCT/US later.
- Sequence: (1) inventorship/ownership sign-off; (2) Indian provisional (Form 1 + 2 [+ 3 + 26]) before any arXiv/GitHub/conference submission; (3) publish after the filing date; (4) within 12 months, complete specification (Form 2 + 5 + 3, with Form 9 + 18/18A) and/or a PCT with India as RO/ISA (which also opens the Rule 24C expedited ground); (5) FER reply within 6 (+3) months, along with an updated Form 3 within 3 months of the FER; (6) after grant, renewals and Form 27 every three financial years; optional Form 8A.

### Gaps
- No 2025–26 change to s.39/Form 25 practice was searched; the background notes' figures are carried forward unverified.
