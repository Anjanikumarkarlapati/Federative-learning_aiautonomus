## Q1. What are the 2026 submission requirements, checklists and policy changes at the main ML and medical-AI venues?

### Takeaway
The conference core is converging: 8-9 page double-blind main text (7 at AAAI), unlimited references/appendix, a mandatory checklist (NeurIPS, AAAI), stricter LLM/AI-use disclosure (ICLR 2027 now requires an AI-use section), reciprocal-reviewing obligations with desk rejection for violations, and arXiv posting allowed everywhere I could verify. Medical venues add reporting-guideline expectations (TRIPOD+AI, CLAIM 2024, FUTURE-AI) and conflict/disclosure statements; journal-specific numbers (NMI, npj Digital Medicine, Lancet Digital Health, TNNLS/TPAMI/JBHI) could mostly not be verified from official pages.

### Inferences
- **Strictest-common-denominator drafting plan:** write a 7-page core (AAAI) that expands to 8 (ICML/MICCAI) or 9 (NeurIPS/ICLR); keep all proofs, extra baselines, full hyperparameter tables, per-client plots and the privacy-attack details in an appendix, because references/appendix are unlimited at NeurIPS, ICML, ICLR and AAAI (MICCAI has a 2-page references cap and no unlimited appendix I could confirm).
- **Treat the NeurIPS 16-question checklist as the universal rigor template.** Answer it even for venues that do not require it (journals, MICCAI); AAAI-27 requires its own reproducibility checklist and MICCAI's checklist descends from Pineau et al.
- **AI-use disclosure is now a cross-venue requirement:** NeurIPS (non-standard method use), ICLR 2027 (mandatory section), IEEE (acknowledgments), Nature (Methods). Keep a log of every AI-assisted writing/coding/figure step from now on so one disclosure paragraph can be adapted per venue. Do not use generative-AI-created figures for Nature-family journals.
- **Medical-AI journals** (npj Digital Medicine, Lancet Digital Health, NMI, JBHI) will likely expect: a named reporting guideline (TRIPOD+AI or CLAIM 2024), external/held-out-site validation, calibration, subgroup fairness analysis, a code/data availability statement and a competing-interests statement. This is an inference from third-party guides, not from official pages.
- Because ICLR has a 20-submission cap, reciprocal-reviewing duties, and ICML/ICLR desk-reject for reviewer LLM violations, a team should name a reviewer-eligible author early.

## Q2. What section-by-section anatomy and what experimental rigor does a top-tier federated learning paper have in 2026?

### Takeaway
A top FL paper states its FL setting precisely (cross-device vs cross-silo, partitioning, participation), shows reproducible simulation details, compares against tuned standard baselines (FedAvg, FedProx, SCAFFOLD, FedOpt family, MOON, FedNova, FedDyn, etc.) on standard benchmarks with controlled non-IID Dirichlet partitions and multiple seeds with uncertainty, and reports communication, computation and privacy costs with explicit conventions. Medical FL papers additionally use natural-silo datasets (FLamby, FeTS/BraTS) and clinical reporting items.

### Cited Findings

**Setting definition and simulation reporting**
- Kairouz et al. distinguish cross-device (mobile/edge) from cross-silo (organizations); say most research is in simulation, so papers must precisely describe the FL setting, give full simulation details so work is reproducible, and tie claims to metrics measurable in simulation. — [Advances and Open Problems in FL (arXiv 1912.04977)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1912.04977) [PRIMARY-paper, via excerpt]
- "A Field Guide to Federated Optimization" gives recommendations for formulating, designing, evaluating and analyzing FL algorithms with simulations that infer real-world performance. — [Google Research listing](https://research.google/pubs/a-field-guide-to-federated-optimization/) [PRIMARY-paper listing]
- Simulation has limits: overlooks network variability, system heterogeneity and energy costs in geo-distributed settings (2026 preprint); few real-world case studies exist (systematic review). — [arXiv 2007.11354](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2007.11354); [2026 HAL preprint](https://hal.univ-lorraine.fr/AMIAD/hal-05477787v1)

**Reporting-rigor items tied to venue checklists (usable as section requirements)**
- Claims: abstract/intro must state contributions, scope, important assumptions and limitations; theory needs stated assumptions and complete proofs (main text or supplement); open data/code with exact commands and environment; experimental details (hyperparameters, splits, selection); statistical significance; compute resources. — [NeurIPS 2022 checklist guidelines](https://neurips.cc/Conferences/2022/PaperInformation/PaperChecklist) [OFFICIAL-excerpt]; [3rd-party 2026 mapping](https://github.com/serre-ai/research/blob/main/docs/submission-guides/neurips-2026.md)
- ICLR strongly encourages a paragraph-long Reproducibility Statement and allows a 1-page ethics statement outside the page limit. — [ICLR 2027 Author Guidelines](https://www.iclr.cc/Conferences/2027/AuthorGuidelines) [OFFICIAL-excerpt]
- ICML main-track papers carry an impact statement outside the page limit (position papers exempt). — [ICML 2026 position call](https://icml.cc/Conferences/2026/CallForPositionPapers) [OFFICIAL-excerpt]
- NeurIPS 2019 reproducibility program: code-submission policy, reproducibility challenge, and a checklist covering the minimal information needed (not exhaustive). — [Pineau et al., JMLR 2021](https://jmlr.org/papers/v22/20-303.html) [PRIMARY-paper]

**Seeds, uncertainty, statistics**
- Report interval estimates (stratified bootstrap CIs), score distributions/performance profiles, and robust aggregates (interquartile mean, optimality gap, probability of improvement) rather than point estimates; the `rliable` library implements them for "even a handful of runs". — [rliable README](https://github.com/google-research/rliable) [README]; [Agarwal et al., NeurIPS 2021 (arXiv 2108.13264)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.13264v4) [PRIMARY-paper]
- Variance sources to randomize/report: data sampling, initialization, hyperparameter choice; a biased estimator covering more variation sources can approximate the ideal at 51x lower compute. — [Bouthillier et al., MLSys 2021](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2103.03098) [PRIMARY-paper]
- NeurIPS guidance on error bars: state which factors vary (split, init, seed), SD vs SE, the calculation (bootstrap/analytic), assumptions (e.g., normality); 1-sigma allowed if stated, 2-sigma preferred when normality unverified; avoid symmetric bars where they cross impossible values. — [NeurIPS 2022 checklist guidelines](https://neurips.cc/Conferences/2022/PaperInformation/PaperChecklist) [OFFICIAL-excerpt]
- Seed practice in FL benchmarks: FLamby reruns each benchmark with 5 seeds (42-46) and notes results vary across machines due to GPU nondeterminism; NIID-Bench uses `init_seed` 0 and repeats with seeds 1 and 2, leaderboard accuracy is the mean of 3 runs; Seo et al. (2025) average 3 independent runs and draw the min-max range as a shaded band. — [FLamby README](https://github.com/owkin/FLamby); [NIID-Bench README](https://github.com/Xtra-Computing/NIID-Bench); [Seo et al., arXiv 2502.00182](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.00182)

**Non-IID partitioning protocols**
- NIID-Bench taxonomy: label-distribution skew (quantity-based: each party holds k labels; distribution-based: Dirichlet with concentration beta, typically 0.5 or 0.1), feature-distribution skew (noise-based, synthetic, real-world e.g. FEMNIST by writer), quantity skew (Dirichlet sizes), mixed skew; 4 algorithms (FedAvg, FedProx, SCAFFOLD, FedNova) plus MOON; 9 datasets (MNIST, CIFAR-10, Fashion-MNIST, SVHN, generated 3D, FEMNIST, adult, rcv1, covtype), later CIFAR-100 and Tiny-ImageNet; defaults 10 parties, 50 rounds; partition map saved via `get_partition_dict()`. — [NIID-Bench README](https://github.com/Xtra-Computing/NIID-Bench); [Li et al. (arXiv 2102.02079)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2102.02079.pdf)
- Dirichlet notation is inconsistent across papers (alpha vs beta vs x; beta=30 behaves near-IID; alpha ~0.1 = strongly non-IID), so papers must state exact partitioner, parameter, client count and seeds. Lower alpha lowered accuracy from ~69% to ~55% and slowed convergence in one study; at alpha=0.1 partial participation further harmed accuracy. — [Seo et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.00182); [DP-Hype](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.04902); [LIA](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.11518); [FedPop](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.08634) (as summarized by search tool)

**Benchmarks and frameworks**
- LEAF: FEMNIST (62 classes, 28x28, ~3,500 users in README; 3,550 devices / 805,263 samples in paper table), Sentiment140 (660,120 users), Shakespeare (1,129 users, 660 with chosen sequence length), CelebA (9,343 users), Reddit (1,660,820 users, 56,587,343 comments), synthetic; provides systems metrics (bytes, FLOPs) logging. Published results often use subsets or filters (e.g., drop clients with fewer than k records), so state the subset. — [LEAF README](https://github.com/TalwalkarLab/leaf); [LEAF paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1812.01097)
- FedScale (ICML 2022): 20+ large-scale heterogeneous FL datasets, 70+ models, runtime emulating thousands of clients per round, Android deployment path, leaderboard. — [FedScale README](https://github.com/SymbioticLab/FedScale); [FedScale ICML 2022](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v162/lai22a/lai22a.pdf)
- Flower: community baselines for FedProx, FedNova, FedAvgM, FedOpt, FedPer, FedRep, FedBN, MOON, NIID-Bench, DASHA, DepthFL, HeteroFL, FedMeta, FedMLB, FedStar, FedWav2vec2, FjORD, TAMUNA, FedVSSL, FedXGBoost, FedPara; quickstarts for PyTorch/TensorFlow/JAX/MONAI/XGBoost etc.; Opacus sample-level DP example; FlowerTune LLM and ViT federated fine-tuning examples. — [Flower README](https://github.com/adap/flower); [Flower baselines README](https://github.com/adap/flower/blob/main/baselines/README.md)
- FedML (TensorOpera): open-source library plus FLOps platform for FL on devices and cross-cloud GPUs. — [FedML README](https://github.com/FedML-AI/FedML)
- FL-bench bundles most methods (FedAvg AISTATS 2017; FedProx MLSys 2020; SCAFFOLD ICML 2020; FedOpt/FedAdam ICLR 2021; MOON CVPR 2021; FedDyn ICLR 2021; plus FedBN, FedRep, Ditto). NVIDIA FLARE ships FedProx, FedOpt, SCAFFOLD. — [FL-bench README](https://github.com/KarhouTam/FL-bench/blob/master/README.md); [NVFlare paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.13291); [SCAFFOLD ICML 2020](https://icml.cc/virtual/2020/poster/53058); [FedProx](https://mlanthology.org/icmlw/2019/li2019icmlw-federated)
- Baseline caveats: pfl-research found SCAFFOLD no better than FedAvg on any of their benchmarks and warned that individually tuned hyperparameters make direct comparison inconclusive; another study found no single algorithm best on all metrics and FedDyn/SCAFFOLD prone to catastrophic failures without gradient clipping; NVFlare found FedOpt/SCAFFOLD converge better under high heterogeneity. — [pfl-research](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.06430); [NVFlare](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.13291) (as summarized)

**Medical FL datasets**
- FLamby (NeurIPS 2022 D&B): cross-silo FL with *natural* partitions; seven datasets: Fed-Camelyon16 (2 clients), Fed-LIDC-IDRI (4 clients: GE 661, Siemens 205, Toshiba 69, Philips 74), Fed-IXI (3 clients, 311/181/74), Fed-TCGA-BRCA (6 clients, 1,088 total), Fed-KITS2019 (6 clients, 96 total), Fed-ISIC2019 (6 clients, 23,247 images), Fed-Heart-Disease (4 clients, 740 total); includes data loaders, evaluation functions, benchmark code, Docker for float-level reproducibility, plotting scripts for heterogeneity figures; integrations with Fed-BioMed, FedML, Substra; BenchOpt integration; dataset licenses must be accepted at the source. — [FLamby README](https://github.com/owkin/FLamby); [FLamby paper (arXiv 2210.04620)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.04620v2); client counts via search summary of [NeurIPS paper](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2022/file/232eee8ef411a0a316efa298d7be3c2b-Paper-Datasets_and_Benchmarks.pdf)
- FeTS/BraTS: institution-partitioned BraTS2021 (~1,200 multi-modal MRI from 23 institutions); FeTS challenge was the first FL challenge and MedPerf ran 41 models at 32 sites; a 2024 Medical Image Analysis benchmark ("Federated brain tumor segmentation: an extensive benchmark"). — [FeTS benchmarking listing](https://wsai.iitm.ac.in/publications/towards-fair-decentralized-benchmarking-of-healthcare-ai-algorithms-with-the-federated-tumor-segmentation-fets-challe-83/); [Reward systems for trustworthy medical FL](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.00470)
- Chest X-ray FL combos: NIH ChestX-ray8 + CheXpert + MIMIC-CXR on the overlapping 8-label set; multimodal medical FL benchmarks remain scarce (Med-MMFL, 2026 preprint). — [arXiv 2205.00470](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.00470); [Med-MMFL](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2602.04416) (via search summary)
- OpenFL authors note challenge data are acquired in controlled settings at few institutions, limiting clinical relevance. — [OpenFL](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2105.06413) (via summary)

**Hyperparameter budgets and fair baselines**
- Noisy client-subsampled evaluation can severely degrade federated hyperparameter tuning; simple baselines are robust in high-noise settings; a private-tuning benchmark ran 4,067 (IID) and 6,912 (non-IID) experiments and found simple server-side aggregation rules fail to recover near-optimal hyperparameters under non-IID. — [Kuo et al., MLSys 2023](https://proceedings.mlsys.org/paper_files/paper/2023/file/3796d87d44d7ee1d9fa2dc38377afa75-Paper-mlsys2023.pdf); [arXiv 2402.16087](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.16087)
- Baseline drift: some works use weighted FedAvg, others unweighted; implementation bugs (e.g., trigger applied after normalization) change attack results; benchmark surveys flag inconsistent settings and missing time/memory cost; experiments can "run successfully" yet use unintended client partitions or mis-aggregated metrics. — [BackFed](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.04903); [FL generalization/robustness/fairness benchmark](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.06750); [ATR-Bench](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.16850); [Developer pain points 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.19621)
- Wall-clock and communication rounds are not linearly related (FedEval). Threshold-dependent metrics (e.g., anomaly detection) can be gamed. — [FedEval](https://arxiv.org/html/2011.09655v1); [FedAD-Bench](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.04442)
- Flower Hub proposes packaging benchmarks as versioned executable apps with pinned dependencies and explicit evaluation workflows. — [Flower Hub](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.25114)

**Communication / computation cost reporting**
- Total communication = rounds x per-round payload; state whether uplink only or uplink+downlink (round cost ~2x model size when both counted), per-client vs total across clients, and define rounds-to-target (first round reaching and holding a target; target chosen e.g. as 90% of best accuracy of any method or 95% of a reported number); say how non-converging runs are treated; fewer rounds can cost more bytes when participation grows; wall-clock needs per-round time. — [apxml FL evaluation primer](https://apxml.com/courses/federated-learning/chapter-1-federated-learning-foundations-revisited/evaluating-fl-systems) [3RD-PARTY]; [Fed-BAC](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.11815); [HDAFL](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2011.06393); [FedEval](https://arxiv.org/html/2011.09655v1) (as summarized)
- LEAF logs systems metrics (communicated bytes, FLOPs) alongside statistical metrics. — [LEAF paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1812.01097) (as summarized)

**Privacy / security analysis conventions**
- DP reporting: report epsilon and delta (a NeurIPS 2020 meta-review asked authors to do so), accountant used (e.g., RDP with subsampling), per-round vs final epsilon, sampling rate = participating/total clients, and the protected unit (record, user, device). — [NeurIPS 2020 meta-review](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper/2020/file/4311359ed4969e8401880e3c1836fbe1-MetaReview.html); [CANIFE](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.02912); [CIO article](https://www.cio.com/article/4225087/ai-privacy-budgets-ask-for-the-calculation-not-the-claim.html)
- Empirical privacy audits complement theoretical bounds (DP threat model can be conservative): canary-based (CANIFE) and one-shot estimation in a single training run (Andrew et al.); membership-inference and gradient-inversion attacks are the standard empirical checks; report precision and recall separately; secure aggregation does not stop all inference or active attacks. — [CANIFE](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.02912); [One-shot empirical privacy estimation](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2302.03098); [Eluding secure aggregation](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2111.07380)

### Inferences
(Synthesis from the cited requirements; section anatomy is conventional ML-paper practice, not taken from a single source.)

**Recommended anatomy for a federated-learning methods paper**
1. **Title / abstract (150-250 words):** problem, gap, method name, 1-2 headline numbers with uncertainty, privacy guarantee (epsilon, delta) if any, and a data/code statement. Claims must match the checklist's "Claims" item exactly.
2. **Introduction:** motivating setting (cross-silo hospitals vs cross-device), the specific failure mode (client drift, heterogeneity, communication, privacy leakage), why existing methods fail (with a preview number), and a bulleted contribution list of 3-4 items each testable in the experiments; point to Figure 1 (teaser) and state scope/limitations up front.
3. **Related work:** FL optimization (FedAvg to FedProx/SCAFFOLD/FedOpt/FedNova/FedDyn/MOON), personalization, privacy (DP, secure aggregation), communication efficiency, and medical FL; position against the closest 3-5 papers and cite concurrent work in the third person for double-blind venues.
4. **Problem formulation / preliminaries:** notation table; objective F(w)=sum_k p_k F_k(w); client set, participation model, heterogeneity measure; threat model (honest-but-curious server, malicious clients) and the privacy unit.
5. **Method:** algorithm box (client update, server aggregation, communication payload), design rationale, complexity (per-round compute and bytes), and privacy mechanism.
6. **Theory:** assumptions (smoothness, bounded variance, bounded heterogeneity), main theorem with convergence rate and/or privacy guarantee, proof sketch in main text and full proof in appendix; discuss how assumptions relate to experiments.
7. **Experimental setup:** datasets (at least one controlled synthetic-partition benchmark such as CIFAR-10/100 with Dirichlet alpha in {0.1, 0.5, 1.0} and number of clients; one natural-partition benchmark such as FEMNIST/Shakespeare (LEAF) or FedScale; one real medical silo benchmark such as FLamby or FeTS/BraTS), models, client count, participation fraction, local epochs, rounds, optimizer; baselines (FedAvg, FedProx, SCAFFOLD, FedAdam/FedOpt, FedNova, MOON, FedDyn, plus personalization baselines if relevant, plus the closest 1-2 recent methods); hyperparameter search budget identical for all methods; metrics (accuracy/AUROC/Dice, worst-client, rounds-to-target, bytes-to-target, wall-clock); seeds (>=5 recommended; 3 is the minimum seen in published FL benchmarks); compute resources.
8. **Results:** main table with mean +/- CI over seeds, convergence curves, communication-efficiency view, heterogeneity sweep (alpha), scalability (clients, participation), per-client results, statistical tests or bootstrap CIs.
9. **Ablations:** each component removed; sensitivity to key hyperparameters (local epochs, mu, noise multiplier, clipping norm, compression ratio); heatmap of two interacting hyperparameters.
10. **Privacy/security analysis:** formal DP accounting (epsilon, delta, accountant, sampling) + utility vs epsilon sweep + empirical attacks (membership inference, gradient inversion) with and without secure aggregation; robustness to poisoning if claimed.
11. **Communication/computation cost:** per-round uplink/downlink bytes, client FLOPs/time, server cost; bytes-to-target vs baselines; deployment considerations.
12. **Medical-specific (if applicable):** TRIPOD+AI/CLAIM 2024 items, external-site hold-out, calibration, subgroup/per-site fairness, ethical approval/IRB or data-use agreements, data-governance statement.
13. **Limitations, ethics/broader impact, reproducibility statement, competing interests (incl. patents), AI-use disclosure, acknowledgments (hidden in review).**

**Minimum rigor bar for 2026 FL papers (synthesized):** >=5 seeds with CI (stratified bootstrap/IQM when many tasks); identical tuning budget per baseline; the same FedAvg variant (weighted) across baselines; at least two heterogeneity levels (e.g., Dirichlet alpha 0.1 and 0.5) and one natural-silo dataset; report global *and* per-client metrics; partition seeds released with code; communication and privacy reported under the stated conventions; code released (anonymized for review).

## Q3. Which figures and graphs are expected, and what design rules make them publication-quality?

### Takeaway
Expect a first-page teaser/overview figure, an architecture/pipeline diagram, an algorithm box, convergence and accuracy-vs-rounds curves with uncertainty bands, privacy-utility and communication-efficiency plots, ablation visuals, per-client and non-IID visualizations, and (medical) ROC/calibration/confusion/Dice plots. Quality rules: vector output with embedded fonts, 5-9 pt readable text at final column width, colorblind-safe palettes (Okabe-Ito / viridis), consistent styling, explicit uncertainty definitions, and self-contained captions.

### Cited Findings

**Design principles**
- Rougier, Droettboom & Bourne, "Ten Simple Rules for Better Figures" (PLOS Comput Biol 2014): (1) know your audience, (2) identify your message, (3) adapt the figure to the support medium, (4) captions are not optional, (5) do not trust the defaults, (6) use color effectively, (7) do not mislead the reader, (8) avoid chartjunk, (9) message trumps beauty, (10) get the right tool. — [PMC4161295](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC4161295/) [PRIMARY-paper; bodies of rules 7-10 only partly retrieved]
- Teaser figure: should help readers understand what the paper is about and spark curiosity without being a whole-method overview; use a representative, not cherry-picked, result; top of page 1, referenced from the introduction. — [SIGGRAPH Research "What are figures for and how do I compose them?"](https://research.siggraph.org/blog/guides/what-are-figures-for-and-how-do-i-compose-them) [3RD-PARTY guide]
- Captions: a figure should be interpretable from the caption alone; define every panel, symbol, abbreviation, error bar (SD, SE or CI) and sample size; describe what is shown in the caption, interpret in the Results text. — [CASRAI figure legend guide](https://casrai.org/guides/figure-legend-example) [3RD-PARTY]
- Uncertainty displays: plot replicate averages with error bars rather than raw points; say whether bars are SD or SE; Nature editorial checklist asks to show individual data points when possible and always when n <= 10, and to state what error bars are (SD, SE, CI). — [NeurIPS 2022 checklist guidelines](https://neurips.cc/Conferences/2022/PaperInformation/PaperChecklist) [OFFICIAL-excerpt]; Nature checklist as summarized in the npj search result [3RD-PARTY copy on Research Square]
- Shaded min-max band across 3 runs is a published FL convention for convergence curves. — [Seo et al., arXiv 2502.00182](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.00182)
- Statistical plots for many tasks: performance profiles, aggregate IQM/optimality-gap interval plots, probability-of-improvement plots (rliable plotting utilities). — [rliable README](https://github.com/google-research/rliable)

**Color**
- Okabe-Ito 8-color palette (popularized by Wong, Nature Methods 8:441, 2011): #E69F00 orange, #56B4E9 sky blue, #009E73 bluish green, #F0E442 yellow, #0072B2 blue, #D55E00 vermillion, #CC79A7 reddish purple, #000000 black; designed to remain distinguishable under common color-vision deficiencies. — [Okabe-Ito reference](https://conceptviz.app/blog/okabe-ito-palette-hex-codes-complete-reference) [3RD-PARTY blog; hex codes match the published palette]
- viridis: perceptually uniform, readable in grayscale and for common color blindness (weaker for tritanopia); cividis targets all forms of color blindness. — [viridisLite manual](https://cran.r-project.org/web/packages/viridisLite/refman/viridisLite.html)
- Make sure no information is lost in grayscale print; export graphs as vectors. — [LetPub / figure guidance](https://www.letpub.com/Preparing-Figures-for-Publication-Guidelines-for-Scientific-Manuscripts) [3RD-PARTY]

**Format, fonts, size**
- Nature: panels as vector artwork (.pdf/.eps) with editable text, not outlined; Arial/Helvetica; 5-7 pt text; 89 mm single / 183 mm double column; max height 170 mm; AI-generated figure images are not permitted (labelled exceptions only). — [Nature figure specs](https://research-figure-guide.nature.com/figures/preparing-figures-our-specifications); [Nature AI policy summary](https://manusights.com/blog/nature-ai-policy) [3RD-PARTY]
- Resolution is not stated on the official pages I reached; secondary sources say 300 dpi at final size (and 1000 for line art); verify. — [Manusights Nature figure guidelines](https://manusights.com/blog/nature-figure-guidelines) [3RD-PARTY, conflicting]
- Matplotlib: default PDF fonts are Type 3, which some venues/journals reject; set `pdf.fonttype = 42` and `ps.fonttype = 42` (since matplotlib 3.5 only glyph subsets are embedded so file size stays small); verify with `pdffonts`. — [Matplotlib fonts doc](https://matplotlib.org/stable/users/explain/text/fonts.html)
- SciencePlots provides `plt.style.use(['science','ieee'])`, which sets column width and font sizes for IEEE papers; requires LaTeX; also notebook style. — [SciencePlots README](https://github.com/garrettj403/SciencePlots)
- IEEE: AI-generated figures/images must be disclosed in the acknowledgments. — [IEEE AI-generated text guidelines](https://open.ieee.org/author-guidelines-for-artificial-intelligence-ai-generated-text/)

**FL- and medical-specific plots that exist in the literature/tooling**
- FLamby ships scripts to reproduce heterogeneity plots per dataset (LIDC site heterogeneity, ISIC center distributions, IXI intensity, KiTS heterogeneity, Camelyon16 histograms) and a `plot_results.py` benchmark summary plot. — [FLamby README](https://github.com/owkin/FLamby)
- NIID-Bench leaderboard presents accuracy and training curves per method, noting accuracy is a 3-run mean while the curve is a single run. — [NIID-Bench README](https://github.com/Xtra-Computing/NIID-Bench)
- Privacy-utility sweeps over epsilon (rather than a single epsilon) are used to show the trade-off; communication plots show accuracy vs cumulative bytes rather than only rounds (rounds and wall-clock diverge). — [FedEval](https://arxiv.org/html/2011.09655v1); [Fed-BAC](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.11815) (as summarized)
- CLAIM-type reporting for medical imaging expects performance with confidence intervals, bias/failure analysis (not a scoring tool); TRIPOD+AI reviewers look at calibration, external validation, subgroup fairness. — [CLAIM 2024 Update](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11304031); [JournalMetrics guide](https://www.journalmetrics.org/blog/publishing-medical-ai-research-2026) [3RD-PARTY]

**Design checklist**
- Export vector PDF/SVG; embed fonts (Type 42); minimum ~6-7 pt text at final printed size (background knowledge, not a sourced venue rule: two-column styles such as ICML/AAAI/IEEE have ~3.3 in columns and ~6.9 in full width, while NeurIPS/ICLR are single-column with ~5.5 in text width; verify against each style file); same font family as body text where possible; one style module shared by all figures so line widths, markers, and baseline colors are identical across the paper.
- Use a fixed method-to-color/marker mapping (Okabe-Ito for categories up to 8, viridis/cividis for sequential, a diverging map such as vik/coolwarm for signed differences); redundant encoding (marker shape or linestyle) so figures survive grayscale; put the proposed method in the most salient color and baselines in muted tones.
- Always label axes with units, show uncertainty (state SD/SE/CI/min-max and number of seeds in the caption), avoid truncated y-axes without marking, avoid dual axes, no chartjunk/3-D.
- Captions: bold one-sentence takeaway title, then what is plotted, dataset/setting, seeds, error-bar definition, abbreviations; keep interpretation in the text.
- Tools (background knowledge): matplotlib/seaborn (+SciencePlots) for data plots, TikZ/pgfplots for LaTeX-native diagrams, draw.io or Figma/Inkscape for architecture diagrams exported to vector PDF, Mermaid for quick flowcharts (rarely publication quality alone); keep plotting scripts and raw result CSVs in the repo so every figure is regenerable.

### Gaps
- No official ICML/NeurIPS/ICLR page states figure font-size or color-accessibility requirements; the numbers above for conference layouts are conventional, not venue rules.
- Tufte-based guidance (data-ink ratio) was not retrieved from a primary source; Rougier's "avoid chartjunk" rule is the cited proxy.
- Nature raster resolution (dpi) conflicts between secondary sources; official page not located for NMI specifically.
- Tool claims (TikZ, draw.io, Figma, Mermaid, seaborn) were not sourced; no search was run on them.
- IEEE PDF eXpress font/Type 3 requirements were not found.

## Q4. Do venues allow or restrict a paper whose invention is simultaneously being patented, and what disclosure statements are required?

### Takeaway
I found no ML venue that bars a paper because a patent is pending, and none that requires patent disclosure at submission; the real constraint is timing: any public disclosure (arXiv, public OpenReview submissions, conference publication) can destroy novelty in absolute-novelty jurisdictions such as the EPO, while the US allows a 12-month grace period for the inventor's own disclosure. Disclosure of patents arises mainly in journal competing-interests forms (Nature Portfolio, ICMJE-style forms) and NeurIPS-style competing-interests statements, not in conference submission rules. This is general information, not legal advice; a patent attorney should decide filing order.

### Cited Findings

**Patent-law timing**
- Europe: the EPC has strict novelty and no grace period; any public availability anywhere before the filing/priority date can bar patentability; preprints are often treated as prior art. — [EPO grace-period study executive summary](https://link.epo.org/web/european_patent_system_and_the_grace_period_study_executive_summary_en.pdf); [EU IP Helpdesk on the EPO survey](https://intellectual-property-helpdesk.ec.europa.eu/news-events/news/epo-survey-strict-novelty-requirements-might-hinder-valuable-innovations-2022-07-13_en) [OFFICIAL, via search excerpt]
- An EPO survey estimated pre-filing disclosure prevented ~7.8% of European university applications and ~3.7% of public research organization applications over three years. — [EU IP Helpdesk](https://intellectual-property-helpdesk.ec.europa.eu/news-events/news/epo-survey-strict-novelty-requirements-might-hinder-valuable-innovations-2022-07-13_en) [OFFICIAL, via summary]
- US, Australia, Canada, Japan have a 12-month grace period for the inventor's own disclosure; China 6 months. — [Novelty (patent) overview](https://www.Wikipedia.com/wiki/Novelty_(patent)); [IP-Coster grace-period page](https://ip-coster.com/academy/details/novelty_grace_period) [3RD-PARTY]
- Trap: the EPO has held that follow-up developments that are obvious in light of the researcher's own publication, or a broadened scope, are not rescued by the 12-month rule; UK and Australian courts have followed. — [York University IP: inadvertent publication](https://york.ac.uk/staff/research/external-funding/ip/inadvertent-publication); [Lund University IPR note](https://www.innovation.lu.se/en/our-services/ipr-patent/important-points-consider) [3RD-PARTY university IP offices]
- University IP advice: file a patent application before the article is sent for peer review, or at least before acceptance; the priority year (from first filing) and the grace period (from publication) run from different events. — [York University IP](https://york.ac.uk/staff/research/external-funding/ip/inadvertent-publication) [3RD-PARTY]
- Example of an issued/filed patent document relying on a NeurIPS 2019 paper as the inventor's own grace-period disclosure. — [USPTO document 11514318](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/11514318)

**Venue prepublication / public-visibility rules relevant to patents**
- NeurIPS: non-anonymous arXiv/website preprints allowed; dual submission to archival venues prohibited; reviews of accepted papers public; rejected papers only public if authors opt in; the funding and competing-interests disclosure (36-month look-back) is requested only for accepted papers and asks about "financial relationships with entities that could be perceived to influence" the work; the disclosure text retrieved does not mention patents. — [3rd-party NeurIPS 2026 guide](https://github.com/serre-ai/research/blob/main/docs/submission-guides/neurips-2026.md) [3RD-PARTY]; [NeurIPS funding disclosure](https://neurips.cc/public/guides/FundingDisclosure) [OFFICIAL-excerpt]
- ICML 2026: arXiv-style non-anonymized preprints allowed provided the submission does not refer to them; 2025 text added the work should not be advertised as an ICML submission during review. — [ICML 2026 CFP](https://icml.cc/Conferences/2026/CallForPapers) [OFFICIAL-excerpt]
- ICLR: submissions are uploaded to OpenReview with public discussion; official reviews are anonymous and publicly visible; 2026 analyses state rejected submissions, reviews and decisions are public; older calls say withdrawn papers stay publicly listed; a third-party summary says withdrawn 2027 papers are de-anonymized immediately (unconfirmed). Prior arXiv versions do not break anonymity. — [ICLR 2025 Author Guide](https://iclr.cc/Conferences/2025/AuthorGuide) [OFFICIAL-excerpt, older year]; [arXiv 2511.23439](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.23439v1) [3RD-PARTY]; [ICLR 2020 CFP](https://iclr.cc/Conferences/2020/CallForPapers)
- ICLR 2020's call stated that papers that are not accepted will be considered non-archival. — [ICLR 2020 CFP](https://iclr.cc/Conferences/2020/CallForPapers) [OFFICIAL-excerpt, older year]
- MICCAI: reviews of accepted papers published; Springer "Disclosure of Interests" section mandatory (declare competing interests or none). — [MICCAI 2026 reviewer guidelines](https://conferences.miccai.org/2026/en/REVIEWER-GUIDELINES.html); [MICCAI 2026 submission guidelines](https://conferences.miccai.org/2026/en/PAPER-SUBMISSION-GUIDELINES.html) [OFFICIAL-excerpt]
- Journals: JMLR does not allow simultaneous submission to conferences or other journals. — [JMLR information for authors](https://www.jmlr.org/author-info.html). IEEE: pre-submission drafts may be on arXiv/TechRxiv; Nature Portfolio: preprint posting does not affect consideration (see Q1 citations).
- Older precedent: SIGGRAPH Asia 2008 policy states patents/technical reports by the same team do not preclude publication of a full paper, and a work is "previously published" if in a peer-reviewed proceeding that is visibly, reliably, permanently available; third-party patents are prior art to be cited. SIGCSE (ACM) notes the official publication date (ACM DL availability) can precede the conference by up to two weeks and "affects the deadline for any patent filings". — [SIGGRAPH Asia 2008 policy](https://siggraph.org/asia2008/presenters/papers/policy.html); [SIGCSE Virtual 2024 authorship policies](https://sigcsevirtual2024.acm.org/info/authorship-policies)

**Copyright forms and patent rights**
- ACM copyright transfer: authors keep "all other proprietary rights except copyright ... such as patent rights"; (relevant to KDD/ACM venues). — [ACM copyright form](https://group.hosting.acm.org/conferences/group97/copyright_form.html); [CACM on ACM copyright policy](https://cacm.acm.org/?p=39411)
- IEEE copyright assignment: authors/employers retain proprietary rights in any process, procedure or article of manufacture described in the work (the word "patent" is not used in the excerpt). — [IEEE copyright form copy (ICICIP 2026)](https://conference.cs.cityu.edu.hk/icicip/ICICIP2026/IEEE%20Copyright%20Form.pdf)

**Competing-interest / patent disclosure statements in journals**
- Nature Portfolio competing-interests form classes patents and patent applications (awarded or pending) filed by authors or their institutions "whose value may be affected by publication" as a financial competing interest; details requested: applicant, inventor(s), application number, status, and which aspect of the manuscript the application covers; editors may judge some applications insufficiently substantive. — [Nature Research declaration form PDF](https://assets-eu.researchsquare.com/files/rs-10261702/v1/a42196b09a76b5050016e799.pdf); [Nature editors interview](https://ipscell.com/2014/07/interview-with-nature-on-their-editorial-process-in-wake-of-stap/); [Springer Nature COI guidance](https://communities.springernature.com/posts/researcher-riddle-conflicts-of-interest-aca51943-1307-4d7f-aef4-dcd6a1a70663) [3RD-PARTY copies of the form]
- ICMJE-style disclosure forms (used by many medical journals) ask "Do you have any patents, whether planned, pending or issued, broadly relevant to the work?" with a 36-month look-back for relationships. Lancet Digital Health's own form could not be verified. — [Example completed ICMJE form (JBJS 2019)](https://cdn-links.lww.com/permalink/jbjs/f/jbjs_2019_11_13_weistrofferdisclosure_19-00959_sdc1.pdf); [Haematologica example](https://haematologica.org/article/view/7281/44771) [3RD-PARTY, older form version]
- Historical enforcement (older evidence): a 2004 Nature-family brief communication received a corrigendum two weeks after publication because an earlier patent application had not been declared as a competing financial interest; a 2006 survey of Nature molecular-biology papers found 7 papers with undeclared patent applications related to the paper. — search-result summary only; the individual URLs (nature.com/articles/nm1001-1071 and a PMC survey record) were returned in the same query but I could not map each claim to a specific page [3RD-PARTY, unverified mapping]

### Inferences
- **No ML/medical-AI conference found restricts patenting or requires patent disclosure at submission.** The relevant disclosure hooks are (a) NeurIPS camera-ready competing-interests statement (a pending patent held by an author's employer arguably is a "financial relationship that could be perceived to influence" the paper; unconfirmed wording), (b) MICCAI/Springer "Disclosure of Interests," (c) journal competing-interests forms (Nature Portfolio explicitly lists patent applications; ICMJE-style forms ask about planned/pending/issued patents). Plan to list the application number/status in these statements.
- **Operational order for a patent-ready paper:** (1) file at least a provisional patent application (covering claims at least as broad as the paper's contribution, including variants such as parameters, privacy mechanism, deployment) BEFORE any public disclosure, (2) then post to arXiv or submit, (3) avoid posting claims broader than what was filed (EPO follow-up trap), (4) note that double-blind submission to a conference is generally confidential but ICLR's open-review makes submissions *public during review*, which counts as disclosure for absolute-novelty jurisdictions, so file before an ICLR submission; NeurIPS/ICML/MICCAI/AAAI keep submissions non-public until acceptance (inference from the cited policies; I did not verify confidentiality language for each).
- Publication timing: ACM Digital Library "official publication date" can precede the conference by ~2 weeks; the NeurIPS/ICML proceedings dates and arXiv posting dates also start the grace clocks. Record all disclosure dates.
- Reviewers' supplementary code is kept confidential and anonymized at NeurIPS (3rd-party), but do not put patent-sensitive details beyond the filed claims in code or supplements.
- Include in the paper a neutral competing-interests line ("A patent application covering aspects of this work has been filed by [institution], application no. [X]") where the venue's disclosure section allows, and keep it out of the double-blind body (NeurIPS camera-ready, MICCAI Disclosure of Interests, journal statements).

### Gaps
- No official statement from NeurIPS/ICML/ICLR/AAAI/KDD/MICCAI about pending-patent papers was found (searches returned only older unrelated conference policies); absence of a rule is not proof of absence.
- Lancet Digital Health, npj Digital Medicine and Nature Machine Intelligence current COI wording not retrieved from official pages (Nature form text via third-party copies).
- Confidentiality terms of double-blind review per venue not verified; ICLR 2027 public/withdrawal visibility only partly confirmed.
- Jurisdiction specifics (India, China, Japan, etc.) and provisional-patent strategy not researched; legal advice required.

## Q5. What common mistakes get ML papers rejected?

### Takeaway
Rejections cluster into (a) hard desk-rejects (page limit, missing checklist, anonymity breaches, dual submission, reviewer-LLM violations, prompt injection, undisclosed LLM use), and (b) weak science: untuned or missing baselines, missing ablations, no uncertainty, overclaiming, unacknowledged limitations, reproducibility gaps. Review outcomes are also noisy (NeurIPS 2021: 23% disagreement between independent committees), so papers must be robustly strong.

### Cited Findings
- Hard desk-reject triggers (NeurIPS 2026): exceeding 9 content pages, missing checklist, modified template margins/fonts, anonymity violations, dual submission, missing OpenReview profiles. — [3rd-party NeurIPS 2026 guide](https://github.com/serre-ai/research/blob/main/docs/submission-guides/neurips-2026.md) [3RD-PARTY]; [NeurIPS 2026 Handbook excerpt](https://neurips.cc/Conferences/2026/MainTrackHandbook) [OFFICIAL-excerpt]
- ICML 2026: main body over 8 pages = automatic rejection; prompt injection = direct rejection; ~497 papers (~2%) desk-rejected because their reciprocal reviewers violated LLM policy. — [ICML Author Instructions](https://icml.cc/Conferences/2026/AuthorInstructions); [ICML blog](https://blog.icml.cc/2026/03/18/) [OFFICIAL-excerpt]
- ICLR: over-length main text, identity leaks, excess submissions over per-author quotas, and (2026) an irresponsible nominated reviewer lead to desk rejection; undisclosed extensive LLM use led to desk rejection in 2026. — [ICLR 2027 Author Guidelines](https://www.iclr.cc/Conferences/2027/AuthorGuidelines); [ICLR 2026 LLM blog](https://blog.iclr.cc/2025/11/19/iclr-2026-response-to-llm-generated-papers-and-reviews/) [OFFICIAL-excerpt]; [arXiv 2512.17950](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.17950v1) [3RD-PARTY]
- MICCAI: over page limits, missing co-author reviewer sign-up, or any guideline violation can lead to desk rejection. — [MICCAI 2026 guidelines](https://conferences.miccai.org/2026/en/PAPER-SUBMISSION-GUIDELINES.html) [OFFICIAL-excerpt]
- Hallucinated/incorrect citations and misleading content violate the NeurIPS Code of Conduct; authors are responsible for all text, figures and references. — [NeurIPS 2026 Handbook excerpt](https://neurips.cc/Conferences/2026/MainTrackHandbook) [OFFICIAL-excerpt]
- Reviewer criteria: originality, quality (claims backed by theory/experiments, honest weighing of strengths and weaknesses), clarity (enough detail to reproduce), significance. Reviewers are told to substantiate critiques with evidence and to provide references when claiming lack of novelty. — [NeurIPS Reviewer Guidelines](https://neurips.cc/Conferences/2026/ReviewerGuidelines) [OFFICIAL-excerpt]
- Commonly cited weaknesses (practitioner patterns, not official): weak or untuned baselines; missing ablations; reproducibility gaps (code, seeds, compute, hyperparameters); overclaimed generality from one benchmark family; missing key related work; unacknowledged limitations; engineering-style novelty without an ablation isolating the benefit. — [Manusights ML pre-submission review](https://manusights.com/blog/pre-submission-review-machine-learning) [3RD-PARTY]
- One LLM-classified analysis of 500 rejected ICLR papers: execution issues ~48% of rejection criticisms, idea-related ~35%, both ~17% (LLM-labelled, treat as estimate). — [as summarized in the search result; source URL not individually returned]
- Review noise: in the NeurIPS 2021 consistency experiment two committees disagreed on 23% of papers; about half of accepted papers would change under a re-run. — [Beygelzimer et al., arXiv 2306.03262](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2306.03262)
- Reproducibility checks are real: 23% of examined ICML 2026 accepted papers had at least one falsified/contested claim in an automated audit. — [HF write-up via PlushCap](https://www.plushcap.com/content/huggingface/blog/huggingface-what-we-learned-by-reproducing-2200-papers-from-icml) [3RD-PARTY]
- FL-specific pitfalls from benchmark papers: tuned hyperparameters not transferring under non-IID, noisy evaluation, inconsistent FedAvg weighting, unstated Dirichlet parameter conventions, unintended client partitions or mis-aggregated metrics, ignoring time/memory cost, using only IID or single-seed results. — [Kuo et al.](https://proceedings.mlsys.org/paper_files/paper/2023/file/3796d87d44d7ee1d9fa2dc38377afa75-Paper-mlsys2023.pdf); [BackFed](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.04903); [Seo et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.00182); [Developer pain points](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.19621); [benchmark survey](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.06750)
- REAL ML workshop recommends explicitly articulating limitations of ML research. — [REAL ML, arXiv 2205.08363](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.08363)

### Inferences
- **Pre-submission rejection-proofing checklist for an FL paper:** formatting compliance (page count in the official style file, anonymization, checklist, AI-use statement); every claim in the abstract maps to a table/figure; baselines tuned with equal budget and include a recent strong method; >=5 seeds with CI; at least one natural-silo and one controlled non-IID dataset; ablation of each component; communication/computation/privacy costs stated with conventions; limitations section naming where the method fails (e.g., extreme non-IID, small client counts, DP at low epsilon); citations verified by hand (not LLM-generated); code + partition seeds + configs released (anonymized); named reviewer author for reciprocal-review venues.
- Medical-AI reviewers additionally reject for: no external/site-held-out validation, no calibration, label leakage across patients/sites, data-governance/IRB gaps, and absent subgroup analysis (inferred from TRIPOD+AI/CLAIM/FUTURE-AI emphasis, not from a rejection study).
- Given 23% reviewer-committee disagreement, plan a backup submission target (e.g., NeurIPS/ICML/AAAI then a journal), respecting dual-submission rules.

### Gaps
- No ranked, official list of rejection reasons exists for NeurIPS/ICML; the "common mistakes" above blend official desk-reject rules with practitioner guides and benchmark papers.
- The 500-paper ICLR analysis (48%/35%/17%) could not be traced to an individual URL in the search output; treat as unverified.
- Rejection reasons specific to MICCAI, KDD and medical journals were not found in any systematic study.
