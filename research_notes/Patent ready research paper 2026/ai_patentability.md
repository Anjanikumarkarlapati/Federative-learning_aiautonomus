## Q1. What are the current eligibility tests and recent case law/guidance in the US, EPO, India (plus China and UK) for ML / federated-learning claims, and which claim features make them pass or fail?

### Takeaway
All major offices now converge on one rule: an ML or federated-learning claim passes only if it is tied to a **technical improvement** (to the model's own operation, to the computer or network, or to a concrete technical field), and fails if it is the application of a generic or known ML technique to a new data environment or goal. The US has moved in opposite directions at the USPTO (more permissive: *Ex parte Desjardins*, MPEP update, SMED declarations) and the Federal Circuit (stricter: *Recentive*, *Rensselaer v. Amazon*, *Dental Monitoring v. Align*). The EPO, UK (since Feb 2026), and India (CRI Guidelines July 2025) all run a "technical effect / technical contribution" analysis, and China's guidelines (in force 1 Jan 2026) add data-with-technical-meaning and ethics gates.

### Cited Findings

**United States: framework and USPTO guidance**
- The 2019 revised eligibility guidance (January 2019) restructured Step 2A into Prong One (does the claim recite a judicial exception: mathematical concepts, mental processes, certain methods of organizing human activity) and Prong Two (is it integrated into a practical application); the Alice/Mayo two-step is the underlying test — [USPTO announcement](https://www.uspto.gov/about-us/news-updates/us-patent-and-trademark-office-announces-revised-guidance-determining-subject); [Greenberg Traurig summary](https://gtlaw.com/de/insights/2019/1/summary-of-new-uspto-subject-matter-eligibility-guidance). (Detail of the groupings is general knowledge; only the title/date is confirmed by these results.)
- The "2024 Guidance Update on Patent Subject Matter Eligibility, Including on Artificial Intelligence" was published 17 July 2024 (89 FR 58128, docket PTO-P-2024-0026). It reaffirms the existing PEG framework for AI, says it announces no new practice, and treats AI-assisted *invention* as an inventorship question, not an eligibility one — [Ballard Spahr](https://ballardspahr.com/insights/alerts-and-articles/2024/08/new-uspto-guidance-hones-ai-related-patent-eligibility-criteria); [Nixon Peabody](https://nixonpeabody.com/insights/alerts/2024/07/23/uspto-issues-updated-ai-subject-matter-eligibility-guidance); [Federal Register raw text](https://federalregister.gov/public-inspection/raw_text/202/415/377.txt); [Aug 2025 USPTO memo citing it](https://www.uspto.gov/sites/default/files/documents/memo-101-20250804.pdf).
- The July 2024 update's three examples: **Example 47** (ANN anomaly detection): the claim reciting a hardware ASIC/neuron-array implementation is eligible, while a method claim reciting generic training, detecting and analysing anomalies without saying how is ineligible; **Example 48** (speech separation): ineligible because the claim recites a mathematical operation and does not "reflect" the technical improvement the specification identifies; **Example 49** (AI-personalised fibrosis treatment): eligible where claims include a concrete post-ML treatment step that integrates the result into a practical application — [Sterne Kessler](https://www.sternekessler.com/news-insights/insights/subject-matter-eligibility-of-ai-innovations-usptos-updated-guidance/); [AFS](https://www.afslaw.com/perspectives/ai-law-blog/uspto-issues-updated-ai-subject-matter-eligibility-guidance). (Sources differ on Example 47's technical field: image/speech recognition vs. computer-network traffic.)
- **4 August 2025 Kim memo** (Deputy Commissioner Charles Kim to TCs 2100/2600/3600): reminders, expressly "not intended to announce any new USPTO practice." Points: (a) a limitation falls in the "mental process" grouping only if it can practically be performed in the human mind or with pen and paper, so large-scale data processing and model training generally do not; (b) "training a neural network" does not by itself recite an abstract idea, though claims that spell out specific mathematical formulas may; (c) distinguish claims that *recite* from those that merely *involve* an exception; (d) analyse the claim as a whole in Prong Two; (e) one firm summary says rejections should issue only where ineligibility is more likely than not (>50%) — [USPTO memo PDF](https://www.uspto.gov/sites/default/files/documents/memo-101-20250804.pdf); [Barnes & Thornburg](https://btlaw.com/en/insights/alerts/2025/uspto-issues-examiner-reminders-on-ai-and-software-patents); [Manatt](https://www.manatt.com/insights/newsletters/u-s-patent-office-memo-why-close-calls-on-patent-eligibility-should-favor-ai-and-machine-learning); [Mondaq (close calls)](https://www.mondaq.com/unitedstates/patent/1684656/how-to-handle-close-calls-on-101-uspto-provides-reminders-for-patent-examiners-on-evaluating-subject-matter-eligibility). One search summary said the memo uses "practically" rather than "reasonably" performed in the mind; I could not confirm the exact wording.
- Same-week data point: one firm counted a single § 101-only reversal versus 46 affirmances in TC 3600 appeals between 17 July and 12 Aug 2025 — [Norton Rose Fulbright](https://www.nortonrosefulbright.com/en/knowledge/publications/b7691556/august-uspto-subject-matter-eligibility-sme-reminder-memo-helpful-for-computer-implemented) (secondary; context for how little the Aug memo had yet changed).

**United States: Ex parte Desjardins (Appeals Review Panel)**
- Appeal No. 2024-000567, decided 26 Sept 2025 by the ARP (Director Squires writing, joined by Wallace and Kim per one summary); designated **precedential on 4 Nov 2025** (one Quinn Emanuel item implies precedential from issuance, which conflicts with the Nov 4 date used elsewhere). The Board had affirmed § 103 rejections and added a *new* § 101 ground; the ARP vacated/reversed that § 101 ground (USPTO memo says "vacated", USPTO news says "reversed") — [Baker Botts](https://ourtake.bakerbotts.com/post/102ltdl/director-squires-designates-ex-parte-desjardins-decision-as-precedential); [Alston](https://www.alston.com/en/insights/publications/2025/11/patent-case-summaries-november-7-2025); [USPTO PTAB notice](https://www.uspto.gov/subscription-center/2025/ptab-designates-precedential-appeals-review-panel-decision); [Quinn Emanuel](https://www.quinnemanuel.com/the-firm/publications/post-grant-patent-updates-march-2026).
- Subject matter: DeepMind/Google application claiming training an ML model on a **sequence of tasks** to avoid "catastrophic forgetting" (continual learning). The ARP held the claims integrate any abstract idea into a practical application because they improve **how the ML model itself operates**, relying on *Enfish*/*McRO*-style reasoning. The specification credited reduced storage, reduced system complexity and preserved performance on earlier tasks; the ARP said claims need not recite the improvement verbatim but must reflect the improvement identified in the specification, and warned examiners not to evaluate claims "at such a high level of generality" — [Crowell](https://www.crowell.com/en/insights/client-alerts/more-than-math-how-desjardins-recognizes-ai-innovations-as-patent-eligible-technology); [USPTO MPEP advance notice](https://www.uspto.gov/sites/default/files/documents/memo-desjardins.pdf); [Pearl Cohen](https://www.pearlcohen.com/a-new-era-in-ai-patent-eligibility-in-view-of-ex-parte-desjardins/); [KTS](https://ktslaw.com/en/insights/alert/2025/10/patent%20office%20appeals%20review%20decision%20suggests%20policy%20shift%20toward%20broader%20eligibility%20for%20ai-related%20patent%20applications).
- Eligibility did not equal allowance: the claims still stand rejected under § 103 — [Baker Botts](https://ourtake.bakerbotts.com/post/102ltdl/director-squires-designates-ex-parte-desjardins-decision-as-precedential).
- **5 Dec 2025 Kim memo "Advance notice of change to the MPEP in light of Ex parte Desjardins"** adds a paragraph at the end of MPEP § 2106.04(d)(III) (one firm lists §§ 2106.04(d), 2106.05(a), 2106.05(f); sources conflict, check the PDF). USPTO says it announces no new practice; commentators read it as pro-applicant — [USPTO memo](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/ANC-Desjardins-Memo-12-5-25.pdf); [PatentNext](https://www.patentnext.com/2025/12/further-update-uspto-issues-memo-integrating-desjardins-into-the-mpep/); [Oblon](https://www.oblon.com/uspto-issues-new-guidance-and-updates-the-mpep-following-ex-parte-desjardins); [Fish & Richardson](https://www.fr.com/insights/thought-leadership/blogs/uspto-adds-desjardins-to-mpep-subject-matter-eligibility-guidance/).
- **Subject Matter Eligibility Declarations (SMEDs, Rule 132):** Director's best-practices memo of 4 Dec 2025 (one alert says 5 Dec) with a companion examiner memo; **superseded by an updated memo of 30 April 2026**; a further **addendum with a two-page SMED evaluation flowchart on 29 Sept 2026** (Patently-O headline: "Tie Goes to the Applicant"; IPWatchdog says it raises questions for practitioners). SMEDs are optional; should be standalone, fact-based, signed by someone with first-hand knowledge, show measurable benefit vs. prior solutions (speed, cost, efficiency) and state of the art at filing, must nexus to the claims, cannot cure § 112 gaps, and are weighed by preponderance of the evidence — [Crowell (Apr 2026 memo)](https://www.crowell.com/en/insights/client-alerts/uspto-issues-updated-best-practices-memorandum-on-subject-matter-eligibility-declarations-smeds-under-rule-132); [Nixon Peabody (Jan 2026)](https://www.nixonpeabody.com/insights/alerts/2026/01/21/usptos-desjardins-and-smeds-reset-the-section-101-playbook-for-ai); [USPTO Sept 2026 memo PDF](https://www.uspto.gov/sites/default/files/documents/smeds-memo-20260929.pdf); [Patently-O](https://patentlyo.com/patent/2026/09/tie-goes-to-the-applicant-the-usptos-smed-flowchart.html); [IPWatchdog](https://ipwatchdog.com/2026/09/29/uspto-flowchart-subject-matter-eligibility-declarations-raises-questions-practitioners/); [AFS](https://www.afslaw.com/perspectives/alerts/smeds-the-spotlight-uspto-memo-highlights-use-rule-132-declarations-ss-101).
- PTAB trend: Patently-O reports the § 101 reversal rate hovered 8-12% for most of 2024-early 2025, hit 18% in Oct 2025 and 29% in Nov 2025 (headline: rate doubled under Director Squires); Polsinelli reports ~9.4% rising to ~21% in Q4 2025, then ~15% for April-July 2026. PTAB decisions also began demanding evidentiary support for "well-understood, routine, conventional" findings (*Ex parte Mercer*, rehearing 31 Oct 2025; *Ex parte Martineau*, Appeal 2026-000138) — [Patently-O](https://patentlyo.com/patent/2026/01/doubles-reversal-director.html); [Polsinelli](https://www.polsinelli.com/publications/patent-eligibility-wars-a-new-hope-at-the-ptab).

**United States: Federal Circuit (stricter)**
- ***Recentive Analytics v. Fox Corp.***, 134 F.4th 1205, No. 2023-2437 (Fed. Cir. 18 Apr 2025, precedential; Dyk, Prost, Goldberg): first-impression holding that claims that merely apply **established ML methods to a new data environment** are ineligible. Rejected: new field of use; faster/more efficient than humans; real-time dynamic maps as inventive concept. Iterative training and dynamic adjustment are not by themselves technological improvements. Court acknowledged ML *could* yield eligible improvements. Four patents (two "Machine Learning Training", two "Network Map" families). Rehearing/en banc denied 23 July 2025; **cert denied 8 Dec 2025** (No. 25-505) — [Schwabe](https://schwabe.com/publication/latest-federal-court-cases-recentive-analytics-inc-v-fox-corp); [Greenberg Traurig](https://www.gtlaw.com/en/insights/2025/4/federal-circuit-machine-learning-patents-ineligible-in-recentive-analytics-inc-v-fox-corp); [Nixon Peabody](https://www.nixonpeabody.com/insights/alerts/2025/06/24/federal-circuit-limits-patent-eligibility-of-machine-learning-in-recentive-analytics-v-fox); [Sterne Kessler (citation)](https://www.sternekessler.com/news-insights/insights/2025-federal-circuit-ip-appeals-recentive-analytics-inc-v-fox-corp-134-f-4th-1205-fed-cir-2025-dyk-prost-goldberg/); [IPWatchdog (cert denial)](https://ipwatchdog.com/2025/12/08/scotus-scraps-recentives-petition-seeking-clarity-eligibility-machine-learning-claims); [SCOTUS docket](https://www.supremecourt.gov/docket/docketfiles/html/public/25-505.html).
- ***Rensselaer Polytechnic Institute v. Amazon.com***, No. 2024-1725 (Fed. Cir. 24 Feb 2026, **nonprecedential**; Dyk, Schall, Stark): affirmed SJ of ineligibility of the '798 patent (case-based reasoning for NLP); "a conventional application of case-based reasoning, even to a novel environment, is abstract"; applies *Recentive*. (An earlier search summary said precedential status was unconfirmed; the later check says CAFC marks it nonprecedential.) — [CAFC opinion](https://www.cafc.uscourts.gov/opinions-orders/24-1725.OPINION.2-24-2026_2651991.pdf); [IPWatchdog](https://ipwatchdog.com/2026/02/24/federal-circuit-affirms-section-101-ineligibility-ai-patent-win-amazon/); [Patently-O](https://patentlyo.com/patent/2026/02/recentive-ratchet-environment.html).
- ***Dental Monitoring SAS v. Align Technology***, No. 2024-2270 (Fed. Cir. 7 July 2026, **nonprecedential**): deep-learning dental-image claims abstract; training on a particular (>1,000-image) dataset treated as ordinary ML; physical-world context (patients, appliances, imaging devices) insufficient — [IPWatchdog](https://ipwatchdog.com/2026/07/07/federal-circuit-finds-deep-learning-patents-ineligible/); [PatentNext](https://www.patentnext.com/2026/08/federal-circuit-finds-ai-based-dental-monitoring-patent-claims-abstract-under-section-101/).
- Net: USPTO guidance (Desjardins) and Federal Circuit case law (Recentive line) are not identical; the Federal Circuit decisions bind courts, whereas Desjardins binds examiners/PTAB. Commentary read in the "Recentive Ratchet" frame says a claim needs a specific technical improvement to how the system works, not a better result — [Patently-O](https://patentlyo.com/patent/2026/02/recentive-ratchet-environment.html); [Sterne Kessler](https://www.sternekessler.com/news-insights/client-alerts/ip-hot-topic-federal-circuits-rpi-v-amazon-questioning-tests-the-boundaries-of-machine-learning-patent-eligibility-post-recentive/).

**EPO**
- Art. 52(2)/(3) EPC "as such" exclusions; the EPO applies the same two-hurdle approach as for all computer-implemented inventions: (1) technical character (any technical means such as a computer suffices for a method/device claim, so the exclusion is effectively cleared), then (2) COMVIK-style inventive-step analysis where non-technical features are disregarded — [Boult Wade Tennant](https://www.boult.com/bulletin/updates-to-epo-guidelines-relating-to-artificial-intelligence/); [J A Kemp 2024 review](https://www.jakemp.com/knowledge-hub/review-of-epo-software-decisions-in-2024/); [USI (13 Apr 2026)](https://www.usi.ch/en/feeds/34773).
- **Guidelines G-II 3.3.1 (AI and ML):** computational models (neural networks, SVMs, k-means etc.) are "per se of an abstract mathematical nature, irrespective of whether they can be 'trained'"; examples — technical: neural network in a heart-monitoring apparatus identifying irregular heartbeats; classification of images/video/audio/speech on low-level features; non-technical: classifying text documents purely by textual content (linguistic purpose, T 1358/09); classifying abstract data records (e.g., telecom network records) with no technical use of the output; **training:** where a classification serves a technical purpose, generating the training set and training the classifier can also contribute to technical character if they support that purpose. Section first in force 1 Nov 2018; expanded 1 April 2025 — [EPO G-II 3.3.1](https://www.epo.org/law-practice/legal-texts/html/guidelines/e/g_ii_3_3_1.htm); [EPO 2025 edition](https://epo.org/en/legal/guidelines-epc/2025/g_ii_3_3_1.html); [Boult](https://www.boult.com/bulletin/updates-to-epo-guidelines-relating-to-artificial-intelligence/).
- **Guidelines 2026** entered into force **1 April 2026**; the AI-related changes found are in the General Part/Part E (use of AI tools, parties remain responsible for AI-assisted filings), not confirmed to be new G-II 3.3.1 examples; other headline changes G 1/24 (description always consulted for claim interpretation), G 1/23 — [EPO preview news](https://www.epo.org/en/news-events/news/preview-versions-guidelines-2026-published-send-us-your-feedback); [Mathys & Squire](https://www.mathys-squire.com/insights-and-events/news/european-patent-office-previews-the-2026-guidelines-for-examination/); [Solve Intelligence](https://www.solveintelligence.com/blog/post/epo-guidelines-2026-key-changes); [Keltie](https://www.keltie.com/knowledge/key-changes-to-the-epo-guidelines-for-examination-entered-into-force-on-1-april-2026).
- **T 702/20 (Mitsubishi, sparsely connected neural network, 7 Nov 2022):** a neural network is a class of mathematical functions; it counts for inventive step only if used to solve a technical problem, e.g., trained with specific data for a specific technical task; claimed task was too generic (regression/classification covers technical and non-technical uses, e.g., stock-market forecasting); the claim covered a new algorithm, not a technical implementation — [HGF](https://www.hgf.com/knowledge-hub/epo-board-of-appeal-decision-indicates-approach-to-core-ai-inventions); [Appleyard Lees](https://www.appleyardlees.com/t-0702-20-patentability-of-a-sparsely-connected-neural-network/); [EPO case law digest](https://www.epo.org/en/legal/case-law/2022/clr_i_d_9_2_11_e.html).
- **T 80/23 (Huawei, "Model parameter combination"):** per J A Kemp's 2024 review, a distributed or **federated ML** method with no particular technical application was treated as a "straightforward technical implementation of an abstract computation method on a conventional computer system", which cannot support inventive step; the Board also remarked that the applicant drafts the claims defining its protection. (Secondary only; decision text not read; title/assignee link comes from a second search result.) — [J A Kemp](https://www.jakemp.com/knowledge-hub/review-of-epo-software-decisions-in-2024/); [Mondaq repost](https://www.mondaq.com/unitedstates/new-technology/1594204/review-of-epo-software-decisions-in-2024).
- Other Board points: T 2803/18 doubted a single novel ML-derived feature gave a technical effect beyond normal processor operation; T 1425/21 (Google, distilled models; Art. 56 and 83) — effect not made credible across the whole claim; an older T 1510/10 said ML use alone is insufficient — [Boult (inventive step in ML cases)](https://www.boult.com/bulletin/insights-epo-board-of-appeal-highlights-the-difficulties-in-establishing-inventive-step-in-machine-learning-cases/); [J A Kemp](https://www.jakemp.com/knowledge-hub/review-of-epo-software-decisions-in-2024/); [Mondaq (sufficiency)](https://webiis08.mondaq.com/uk/patent/1355988/sufficiency-at-the-epo-for-ai-inventions).
- Where a claim lets the output be used for something other than a technical purpose, commentary on G 1/19 suggests the EPO would see the claim as non-technical over its whole scope — [Wolters Kluwer Patent Blog](https://legalblogs.wolterskluwer.com/patent-blog/patentability-of-ai-and-machine-learning-at-the-epo/) (search-summary attribution, not verified).

**India**
- Statutory hook: section 3(k) Patents Act 1970 excludes "a mathematical or business method or a computer programme per se or algorithms" (general knowledge; text not retrieved).
- **CRI Guidelines 2025:** first draft 25 March 2025; revised draft (v2.0) 26 June 2025 (one source says 29 June); **final released 29 July 2025**, superseding the 2017 Guidelines; includes a chapter on AI/ML/DL, blockchain and quantum, with scenario examples and sufficiency requirements; states they are not rule-making and the Act/Rules prevail — [PIB press release](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2149719&reg=48&lang=2); [Intepat](https://www.intepat.com/blog/cri-guidelines-2025-patent-india-3k); [Mondaq analysis](https://www.mondaq.com/india/patent/1663246/a-detailed-analysis-of-indias-new-cri-guidelines-2025); [EU IP Helpdesk](https://intellectual-property-helpdesk.ec.europa.eu/news-events/news/indian-patent-office-guidance-examining-ai-inventions-cri-guidelines-2025-2025-08-05_en).
- Content (secondary): novel hardware is no longer a prerequisite (rejected as a standard with no legal basis); the applicant should state the technical problem and technical solution and show a contribution beyond normal computer functioning; example effects: higher speed, reduced memory access time, better robotic-arm control, enhanced security, reduced latency; business methods remain excluded; if any claim form (method/apparatus/CRM) falls in the exclusion, the subject matter is not patentable; algorithms must be technically enabled — [Intepat](https://www.intepat.com/blog/cri-guidelines-2025-patent-india-3k); [Intepat AI/ML](https://www.intepat.com/blog/ai-machine-learning-patents-patentable-india); [Fox Mandal](https://foxmandal.in/patenting-ai-in-india-patent-office-revises-its-cri-guidelines/). Sources disagree on whether a *measurable* effect is demanded: one says yes, another notes the guidelines set no measurable benchmarks.
- **Case law:** *Ferid Allani v. Union of India* (Delhi HC, Dec 2019): software inventions patentable if they show "technical effect" / "technical contribution", moved the test away from "per se"; *Microsoft Technology Licensing v. Assistant Controller* (Delhi HC 2023, 2023 SCC OnLine Del 2772): use of algorithms does not make an invention a program per se; assess technical advancement and practical application (network-authentication security); *Raytheon v. Controller* (Delhi HC 2023): set aside 3(k) refusal for HPC scheduling invention; *Ab Initio* and *BlackBerry/Malikie* (Delhi HC, Aug 2024, same Single Judge, same day, opposite outcomes: one found technical effect inside and outside the computer, the other held that software instructions managing data flow did not meet the threshold); *Comviva Technologies* (Delhi HC, 12 Nov 2024): payment-card authentication security improvement; words like "payment/transaction" do not make a business method; *Kroll Information Assurance v. Controller* (Delhi HC, 1 July 2025, C.A.(COMM.IPD-PAT) 439/2022, Justice Amit Bansal; 2025 DHC 5096): refusal **upheld**, conventional hardware executing basic instructions; claims 1-7 "computer programme per se", claims 8-10 "algorithms" — [SCC Online (Ferid Allani)](https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2019/12/31/del-hc-technical-contribution-is-the-test-of-patentability-for-computer-programs-law-on-s-3k-of-patents-act-clarified/); [SCC Online (Microsoft)](https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2023/05/16/invention-not-to-be-deemed-a-computer-programme-per-se-solely-due-to-use-of-algorithms-legal-news/); [Khaitan](https://khaitanco.com/thought-leaderships/Delhi-High-Court-sets-out-parameters-for-patentability-of-computer-related-inventions); [SpicyIP (Aug 2024)](https://spicyip.com/2024/08/dhc-and-3k-this-time-using-the-technical-effect-test.html); [Verdictum (Comviva)](https://www.verdictum.in/columns/comviva-technologies-limited-vs-assistant-controller-of-patent-design-1562082); [Verdictum (Kroll)](https://www.verdictum.in/court-updates/high-courts/delhi-high-court/kroll-information-assurance-llc-v-the-controller-general-of-patents-designs-and-trademarks-2025-dhc-5096-1583129); [Raytheon](https://depenning.com/news-and-insights/raytheon-vs-controller/); [SFLC (critical view)](https://sflc.in/say-no-to-software-patents-ii-the-judgements-that-shaped-technical-effect/).
- 2026: a LiveLaw listing mentions a 30 April 2026 Delhi HC judgment (Justice Tejas Karia) treating an invention as a computer program per se and lacking inventive step (snippet truncated); a 27 Jan 2026 remand for natural-justice reasons — [LiveLaw listing](https://www.livelawbiz.com/patent/3). **China (brief)**
- CNIPA Order No. 84 amending the Patent Examination Guidelines (issued 10 or 13 Nov 2025, sources differ), **in force 1 Jan 2026**, building on the Dec 2024 trial "Guidelines for Patent Applications Related to AI". Key AI points (secondary): a model is more likely a "technical solution" where it processes data with definite technical meaning in a technical field, or has a specific technical relationship with computer-system internals (less storage, less data transmission, faster processing); algorithmic and technical features must cooperate functionally for inventive step; porting a known model to a new scenario without substantive technical change is unlikely to be inventive; improvements must be attributable to the claimed solution, not the known model's inherent capability; reproducible disclosure of architecture and training; ethics/legal-compliance gate (facial-recognition example); inventors must be natural persons — [KTS (Dec 2025)](https://ktslaw.com/insights/alert/2025/12/chinas-revised-patent-guidelines-and-drafting-strategy); [KTS (Jul 2026)](https://ktslaw.com/insights/alert/2026/7/chinas-revised-patent-examination-guidelines-for-ai-six-practice-tips-for-drafters); [Mathys & Squire](https://www.mathys-squire.com/insights-and-events/news/china-raises-the-bar-for-ai-patents-what-changes-from-1-january-2026/); [Kangxin](https://eservice.kangxin.com/html/1/393/7905.html); [IAM](https://www.iam-media.com/article/cnipa-updates-patent-examination-guidelines-address-emerging-technology-regulate-dual-filing-and-invalidations).

**United Kingdom (brief)**
- ***Emotional Perception AI v Comptroller-General* [2026] UKSC 3 (11 Feb 2026):** Supreme Court unanimously abandoned the *Aerotel* four-step test and aligned with the EPO (G 1/19, "any hardware", "intermediate step" filtering features that do not contribute to technical character); an ANN is a computer program in substance, so claims to an ANN as such are excluded, but ANN claims involving hardware/technical interaction are not excluded merely for being ANNs; application remitted to UKIPO (hearing 4 Aug 2026, decision not found) — [Linklaters](https://www.linklaters.com/insights/blogs/digilinks/2026/february/Emotional-Perception-AI-Supreme-Court-overhauls-decades-of-UK-case-law); [DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiper.com/insights/publications/2026/02/patentability-of-ai-the-supreme-court-perspective-on-emotional-perception); [Morgan Lewis](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2026/02/uk-supreme-court-resets-approach-to-patentability-of-computer-implemented-inventions).
- **UKIPO practice note, 14 July 2026:** examiners apply (1) any-hardware, (2) intermediate step, (3) novelty/inventive step; stop following Manual of Patent Practice 1.07-1.51.7; reconsider prior Aerotel-based excluded-matter objections; may refer to EPO G-II 3 but are not bound — [Fieldfisher](https://www.fieldfisher.com/en/insights/ukipo-issues-new-patent-examination-guidance-following-emotional-perception-ruling); [HGF](https://www.hgf.com/knowledge-hub/new-technical-character-guidance-formalised-by-ukipo-practice-note/); [Marks & Clerk](https://www.marks-clerk.com/insights/latest-insights/102ngb4-rewriting-the-rules-inside-the-ukipos-new-practice-note-on-ai-patentability/).

### Inferences
- Claim features that **pass** (cross-jurisdiction synthesis): (i) a modification to training/aggregation/communication mechanics that yields a stated system-level improvement (storage, bandwidth, convergence, robustness) AND is reflected in the claim (Desjardins; Example 48; CNIPA "reduced transmission"; CRI "reduced latency"); (ii) a specific hardware or system architecture (Example 47 ASIC; T 702/20 "technical implementation"; EP 4083868-type TEE architecture); (iii) a concrete downstream technical action tied to the model output (Example 49 treatment step; heart-monitor example in G-II 3.3.1); (iv) data with a definite technical meaning in a technical field (G-II 3.3.1; CNIPA).
- Claim features that **fail**: result-oriented "train a model on data X to predict Y"; ML applied to a new domain or dataset without model improvement (Recentive, RPI v. Amazon, Dental Monitoring); "uses the cloud/server/client" generic computers; "reduces communication costs" stated as a benefit without a claimed mechanism; generic regression/classification with no specified technical purpose (T 702/20, T 80/23); business, scheduling or text-only classification (Recentive; T 1358/09; Kroll).
- Strategy follows: file US claims that satisfy the *stricter Federal Circuit standard* (mechanism-level), because issued patents will be tested in court under *Recentive* rather than under Desjardins; use Desjardins and SMEDs for prosecution leverage but do not rely on them for litigation durability.
- Federated learning is conceptually distributed training. Under T 80/23 and Recentive it sits in the same "generic distribution of a known algorithm" bucket unless claim features are specific; Desjardins-style framing (model-level improvement identified in the specification and mirrored in claims) is the best US hook.

## Q2. What is the current rule on AI-assisted inventorship, and how should human contribution be documented?

### Takeaway
Everywhere that has ruled (US, EPO, UK, India, China), an inventor must be a natural person; AI cannot be named. In the US the USPTO **rescinded its February 2024 AI-assisted inventorship guidance on 26/28 Nov 2025** and replaced it with a single conception-based standard, treating AI as a tool and limiting *Pannu* factors to disputes between multiple human co-inventors. Documentation should show which named human conceived each claim limitation.

### Cited Findings
- **US, revised guidance:** "Revised Inventorship Guidance for AI-Assisted Inventions," 90 FR 54636, docket PTO-P-2025-0014, Federal Register date **28 Nov 2025** (one firm says released 26 Nov); rescinds the Feb 2024 guidance and its examples; no separate standard for AI-assisted inventions; conception (definite and permanent idea of the complete and operative invention, a specific settled solution, not a general goal) is the test; for a single human, the question is whether that person conceived the invention; *Pannu* factors apply only to multiple natural persons; AI is treated as a tool (like lab equipment, software, a database); named inventors presumed correct, no special scrutiny for AI use; applies to utility, design, plant; priority claims need at least one common natural-person inventor; implements EO 14179 (23 Jan 2025) — [Mayer Brown](https://www.mayerbrown.com/en/insights/publications/2025/12/united-states-patent-and-trademark-office-issues-revised-guidance-on-inventorship-for-ai-assisted-inventions); [KTS](https://ktslaw.com/insights/alert/2025/12/uspto-issues-revised-inventorship-guidance-eliminating-separate-standard-for-ai-assisted-inventions); [Squire Patton Boggs](https://aihub.squirepattonboggs.com/2025/12/new-inventorship-guidance-on-ai-assisted-inventions-ai-cant-be-an-inventor-but-ai-can-be-a-tool-in-the-inventive-process-for-now/); [Osha](https://www.obwb.com/newsletter/uspto-issues-new-guidance-on-aiassisted-inventorship-rescinding-its-2024-guidance); [Bitlaw copy of notice](https://www.bitlaw.com/source/pto/2025-AI-Inventorship-Guidance.html); [JIPLP commentary](https://academic.oup.com/jiplp/article/21/5/272/8528856).
- ***Thaler v. Vidal*** (Fed. Cir. 2022): AI system cannot be an inventor under the Patent Act; anchors the USPTO position — [Morgan Lewis](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2025/12/uspto-issues-revised-inventorship-guidance-for-ai-assisted-inventions); [KTS](https://ktslaw.com/insights/alert/2025/12/uspto-issues-revised-inventorship-guidance-eliminating-separate-standard-for-ai-assisted-inventions).
- 2024 examples (e.g., a person who merely poses a problem to an AI may not be an inventor) belong to the rescinded document and should be used only as analogies — [Goodwin](https://www.goodwinlaw.com/en/insights/publications/2025/12/alerts-lifesciences-uspto-issues-revised-inventorship-guidance).
- Practitioner documentation tips from the commentary: keep dated records of which person conceived each claimed feature; interview inventors claim-by-claim; ensure each limitation traces to a human; version-control AI workflows (inputs chosen, outputs kept or modified, tool configuration or training); disclosure forms should list only natural-person contributors; AI-interaction logs may become material if claims cover machine-generated embodiments no human conceived; maintain duty-of-candour practices from the April 2024 AI-assisted-practice notice (one commentator); make sure at least one common human inventor spans related foreign filings — [Brownstein Hyatt](https://www.bhfs.com/insight/uspto-issues-revised-inventorship-guidance-for-ai-assisted-inventions-what-it-means-for-patent-strategy/); [Holland & Knight (Feb 2026)](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/02/the-human-element-uspto-clarifies-inventorship); [Jones Day](https://www.jonesday.com/en/insights/2025/12/uspto-revises-guidance-on-aiassisted-inventorship); [Davis Wright Tremaine](https://www.dwt.com/insights/2025/12/uspto-revises-ai-assisted-inventions-guidance); [PatentNext (Mar 2026)](https://www.patentnext.com/2026/03/the-usptos-revised-guidance-regarding-ai-assisted-inventions/).
- **EPO:** Legal Board of Appeal **J 8/20 and J 9/20** (DABUS; hearing Dec 2021, reasons published July 2022) held the inventor under Arts. 60(1) and 81 EPC must be a natural person with legal capacity; a "no inventor, owner of DABUS has the right" fallback also failed; inventor designation is checked formally, independently of patentability — [EPO press communique](https://epo.org/en/law-and-practice/boards-of-appeal/communications/press-communique-decisions-j-820-and-j-920-legal); [Baker McKenzie](https://connectontech.bakermckenzie.com/ai-as-an-inventor-only-human-inventors-for-european-patents/); [Clifford Chance](https://www.cliffordchance.com/insights/resources/blogs/talking-tech/en/articles/2022/03/epo-rules-only-humans-can-be-inventors-another-blow-to-dabus-a.html).
- **UK:** *Thaler v Comptroller-General* [2023] UKSC 49 (20 Dec 2023), unanimous: inventor under Patents Act 1977 must be a natural person; Court expressly did not decide whether AI-generated technical advances are patentable — [UKSC press summary](https://caselaw.nationalarchives.gov.uk/uksc/2023/49/press-summary).
- **India:** Controller refused Thaler's DABUS application (No. 202017019068, food container) because DABUS cannot be "true and first inventor" and declaration/proof-of-right requirements were unmet; a later Controller decision reported **27 July 2026** held an inventor must be a natural or legally recognised person capable of holding and transferring rights (ownership of the AI system/code/hardware is irrelevant) and also found the claims obvious; a Delhi HC appeal was reported with notice issued (status not confirmed). The 2025 CRI Guidelines draft commentary separates AI-generated inventions without meaningful human contribution from AI-as-tool cases — [EU IP Helpdesk](https://intellectual-property-helpdesk.ec.europa.eu/news-events/news/indias-patent-office-rejects-ai-inventor-landmark-dabus-decision-2026-07-27_en); [K&S Partners](https://ksandk.com/intellectual-property-rights/ai-cannot-be-inventor-dabus-patent-application-india/); [Bar & Bench](https://www.barandbench.com/news/litigation/can-ai-be-considered-inventor-under-patent-law-delhi-hc-seeks-patent-office-response-to-us-scientists-plea); [Intepat](https://www.intepat.com/blog/uspto-ai-assisted-inventorship-guidance-vs-india-dabus-position).
- **China:** revised Guidelines (in force 1 Jan 2026) confine inventor entries to natural persons; "AI" cannot be listed — [Osha](https://www.obwb.com/newsletter/cnipa-releases-amendments-to-patent-examination-guidelines-effective-january-1-2026); [Mathys & Squire](https://www.mathys-squire.com/insights-and-events/news/china-raises-the-bar-for-ai-patents-what-changes-from-1-january-2026/).
- **EPO 2026 Guidelines** add statements that parties remain fully responsible for AI-assisted filings — [Solve Intelligence](https://www.solveintelligence.com/blog/post/epo-guidelines-2026-key-changes).

### Inferences
- For a federated-learning invention developed with AI assistance (e.g., LLM-suggested aggregation variants), the safe record is: the human defined the technical problem and selected/modified the claimed mechanism (conception of "the particular solution"), prompts and outputs are retained, and each independent-claim limitation is mapped to a named person. That position satisfies all five jurisdictions, since all require natural-person inventors and the USPTO's conception test is the most demanding on specificity.
- Because the 2024 USPTO examples are withdrawn, there is no current USPTO safe harbour for "merely prompting" an AI; the conservative reading is that prompting without forming the definite solution risks no named inventor, an unpatentable invention, or an unenforceable one.

## Q3. What disclosure is needed to satisfy enablement and written description for ML inventions?

### Takeaway
No jurisdiction requires disclosing the actual training dataset or exact weights, but all expect enough on architecture, training procedure, input/output definitions and a working example to let a skilled person reproduce the claimed effect over the **full claim scope**. The EPO is explicitly strictest (T 161/18, T 1191/19, T 1669/21), the CNIPA and IPO guidelines now push in the same direction, and in the US *Amgen v. Sanofi* ties disclosure to claim breadth while the PTAB (*Husain*, Jan 2026) has been relatively accommodating.

### Cited Findings
- **US, *Amgen v. Sanofi* (2023):** the more a claim covers, the more the specification must enable; no need to describe every embodiment with particularity; commentators expect it to affect AI/functional claims — [Baker Botts](https://www.bakerbotts.com/thought-leadership/publications/2023/june/us-supreme-court-affirmed-patent-enablement-requirement-in-amgen); [Mondaq "Amgen and AI"](https://www.mondaq.com/unitedstates/patent/1261676/amgen-and-ai).
- ***In re Starrett*** (Fed. Cir. 2023, nonprecedential): affirmed enablement rejection of ML-task claims where neither claim nor specification described *how* the ML element was implemented — [Mondaq](https://mondaq.com/unitedstates/patent/1385856/the-federal-circuit-hints-at-enablement-requirements-for-artificial-intelligence-ai-inventions).
- ***Ex parte Husain*** (PTAB, 29 Jan 2026, per search summary): claims adequately supported by passages on generating and training an ML model even without a specific architecture or working examples; Board cited MPEP language that lack of working examples should not be the sole basis for an enablement rejection (attribution to a specific article unconfirmed; likely [Solve Intelligence](https://www.solveintelligence.com/blog/post/ptab-case-studies-of-ai-disclosure-requirements-part-ii) or [Baker Botts](https://ourtake.bakerbotts.com/post/102luaq/navigating-the-new-landscape-of-ai-patent-protection)).
- **Desjardins/SMED link:** the ARP tied eligibility to improvements *identified in the specification*, and SMEDs cannot cure § 112 gaps — [USPTO memo](https://www.uspto.gov/sites/default/files/documents/memo-desjardins.pdf); [Crowell](https://www.crowell.com/en/insights/client-alerts/uspto-issues-updated-best-practices-memorandum-on-subject-matter-eligibility-declarations-smeds-under-rule-132). So the specification must contain the technical-improvement evidence at filing.
- **EPO Art. 83:** **T 161/18** (aortic pressure estimation by ANN; priority 2005): refused because the application did not say which training inputs were suitable or give at least one usable dataset; **T 1191/19**: claim defined by training data with no example dataset or guidance, "an invitation to a research programme"; **T 1669/21** (refractory-lining wear prediction, Oct 2024): mere mention of ML "woefully insufficient"; gaps in parameter selection, architecture and especially training data (source and characteristics across the parameter space); generally the dataset itself need not be disclosed; **T 1425/21** (Google, distilled models): effect not credible over whole claim scope (Art. 56/83) — [EPO T 161/18 digest](https://epo.org/en/legal/case-law/2019/clr_ii_c_6_1.html); [Kilburn Strode (T 161/18)](https://www.kilburnstrode.com/Knowledge/European-IP/T-0161-18-draft-case-Law-article); [Kilburn Strode (T 1669/21)](https://www.kilburnstrode.com/Knowledge/AI/AI-ML-Patent-Disclosure-T166921); [Marks & Clerk](https://www.marks-clerk.com/insights/latest-insights/102jvzb-ai-inventions-and-sufficiency-at-the-epo/); [Boult](https://boult.com/bulletin/t0161-18-sufficiency-and-machine-learning-at-the-epo); [Managing IP](https://www.managingip.com/article/2gjp9mu025scleyu9hjwg/sponsored-content/shedding-light-on-the-black-box-ai-inventions-under-article-83-scrutiny). Caveat: commentary notes T 161/18 relied on 2005-era skilled-person knowledge of AI.
- **India (CRI 2025):** commentators report expectations of structural detail on the network, training dataset characteristics, pre-processing pipeline, training parameters, and validation results showing the claimed technical effect; the draft required identifying training data (NASSCOM and others objected, asking that describing data *characteristics* suffice); one submission frames a conditional rule (dataset characteristics must be disclosed if the technical effect depends on them, unless derivable from common general knowledge); critics say the draft's sufficiency expectations go beyond the Act — [Intepat AI/ML](https://www.intepat.com/blog/ai-machine-learning-patents-patentable-india); [NASSCOM](https://community.nasscom.in/communities/public-policy/nasscoms-feedback-draft-guidelines-examination-computer-related); [BananaIP comments](https://www.bananaip.com/intellepedia/comments-on-draft-cri-guidelines-ai-ml-blockchain/); [Section3k](https://www.section3k.com/post/india-s-draft-cri-guidelines-2-0-2025-a-sharper-blueprint-for-patentability-in-the-age-of-ai).
- **China:** disclose model architecture, training method and implementation in enough detail to reproduce; technical effects supported by concrete embodiments; features supporting inventive step should be recited in the claims — [Kangxin](https://eservice.kangxin.com/html/1/393/7905.html); [KTS](https://ktslaw.com/insights/alert/2026/7/chinas-revised-patent-examination-guidelines-for-ai-six-practice-tips-for-drafters).
- Disclosure checklist assembled by commentators: architecture (layers, connections, node maths); named framework alone is not enough; training data, process, hyperparameters, optimisation; concrete input/output variable definitions; guidance on parameter selection with working examples — [Baker Botts](https://ourtake.bakerbotts.com/post/102luaq/navigating-the-new-landscape-of-ai-patent-protection); [HLK](https://www.hlk-ip.com/news-and-insights/drafting-ai-patent-applications-for-success-at-the-epo-1-patent-eligibility-and-claim-drafting/).

### Inferences
- **Federated-learning specification checklist** (my synthesis from the above): (1) topology (cross-device vs cross-silo; horizontal/vertical/hybrid; server-client or peer-to-peer; number and types of clients); (2) local model architecture and global model; (3) local training procedure (optimiser, local epochs, batch size, learning rate, ranges); (4) the claimed mechanism in algorithmic detail (selection rule, compression/quantisation operator, aggregation formula, weighting, noise calibration/privacy budget, secure aggregation protocol, key management); (5) message formats and the data actually transmitted; (6) at least one worked example with datasets described by characteristics (modality, size, heterogeneity/non-IID partitioning, number of sites), baselines (e.g., FedAvg), metrics, and quantitative results showing the claimed improvement (bytes transmitted, rounds to target accuracy, privacy loss epsilon, attack success rate); (7) ranges/alternatives only where actually supported, to avoid *Amgen*-type breadth challenges; (8) statement of the technical problem and improvement in the specification so claims can "reflect" it (Desjardins, Example 48).
- Provide ablations and negative results where possible: they double as SMED evidence and EPO "credible effect across the claim scope" support.
- Datasets: publicly known benchmarks (e.g., public medical imaging sets) can be named for reproducibility; confidential clinical data need only be characterised (per T 1669/21 commentary and Indian stakeholder comments).

### Gaps
- No US decision located that squarely requires hyperparameter values or dataset disclosure; guidance is by analogy.
- *Husain* and *Starrett* are known only through secondary summaries; *Starrett* is nonprecedential.
- Final text of the IPO's AI/ML sufficiency chapter not obtained; draft-era comments may not reflect the final July 2025 text.
- USPTO § 112 treatment in MPEP 2161-2164 and any 2025-26 § 112 AI guidance not searched specifically.
- Not found: EPO 2026 Guidelines F-III changes on ML sufficiency (if any).

## Q5. What are typical reasons ML patent applications are rejected, how are they overcome, and how should a federated-learning claim be drafted to pass eligibility?

### Takeaway
Rejections cluster in four groups: (1) abstract idea / non-technical (US § 101; EPO Art. 52/56; India s. 3(k)); (2) lack of inventive step because the only novelty is mathematical or non-technical (US § 103; EPO Art. 56 via COMVIK); (3) insufficient disclosure (US § 112(a); EPO Art. 83); (4) formal/functional-claim issues. Overcoming them means (a) amending to a claimed mechanism that carries the technical improvement, (b) relocating the improvement evidence into the specification and, in the US, a SMED, and (c) matching disclosure to claim breadth.

### Cited Findings
- **US § 101 responses that work or are encouraged:** argue "involves vs. recites" and "practically performed in the mind" (Aug 2025 memo); show improvement to ML model operation reflected in claims (Desjardins; Example 47/48); demand evidence for WURC findings (*Mercer*, *Martineau*); file a SMED showing the state of the art and measurable benefit; ask for a § 101 rejection to meet a >50% probability threshold — [USPTO Aug 2025 memo](https://www.uspto.gov/sites/default/files/documents/memo-101-20250804.pdf); [Polsinelli](https://www.polsinelli.com/publications/patent-eligibility-wars-a-new-hope-at-the-ptab); [Crowell](https://www.crowell.com/en/insights/client-alerts/uspto-issues-updated-best-practices-memorandum-on-subject-matter-eligibility-declarations-smeds-under-rule-132).
- Desjardins itself shows § 101 success is not allowance: § 103 stood — [Baker Botts](https://ourtake.bakerbotts.com/post/102ltdl/director-squires-designates-ex-parte-desjardins-decision-as-precedential).
- **EPO:** frequent grounds are lack of technical contribution (T 702/20, T 80/23, T 2803/18), generic task (T 702/20), effect not shown across the claim scope (T 1425/21), and Art. 83 (T 161/18, T 1191/19, T 1669/21). Commentary (Carpmaels) lists three reasons applied-AI applications fail at the EPO (article not read) — [HGF](https://www.hgf.com/knowledge-hub/epo-board-of-appeal-decision-indicates-approach-to-core-ai-inventions); [J A Kemp](https://www.jakemp.com/knowledge-hub/review-of-epo-software-decisions-in-2024/); [Carpmaels](https://www.carpmaels.com/patenting-ai-three-reasons-why-patent-applications-for-applied-ai-inventions-do-not-succeed-at-the-epo/). EPO-specific drafting advice: define a specific technical purpose in the claim, make training steps serve it, give reproducible training detail, and expect the claim to be read with the description (G 1/24) — [HLK](https://www.hlk-ip.com/news-and-insights/drafting-ai-patent-applications-for-success-at-the-epo-1-patent-eligibility-and-claim-drafting/); [Solve Intelligence](https://www.solveintelligence.com/blog/post/epo-guidelines-2026-key-changes).
- **India:** typical objections are 3(k) (program per se, algorithm, business method) and inventive step (even where 3(k) is overcome, e.g., *Ab Initio* remand); responses succeed by pleading technical problem/solution, hardware or system interaction, and quantifiable technical effect; Delhi HC outcomes are inconsistent (Kroll vs. Microsoft/Comviva) — [AsiaIPLaw (2024 decisions)](https://asiaiplaw.com/index.php/article/seven-key-decisions-of-2024-on-computer-related-inventions-and-their-impact-on-high-tech-innovations-in-india); [Intepat](https://www.intepat.com/blog/cri-guidelines-2025-patent-india-3k).
- **Federated-learning-specific signals from commentary and filings:** (i) T 80/23 treats generic distributed/federated ML as a conventional-computer implementation; (ii) communication is a real technical constraint, and mechanisms that act on message volume, rounds, or update speed (e.g., Google's structured/sketched updates; update-relevance checking that skips non-aligned updates; one-time registration of active parameter indices) are the type of concrete technical measure described in the literature; (iii) concrete security architecture (TEE on server and client, secure channel, layer-wise training in client TEEs, EP 4083868) is more tangible than stating that FL "preserves privacy"; (iv) a mmWave filing that only says FL reduces communication cost and latency is the weak pattern (benefit statement, not mechanism) — [J A Kemp](https://www.jakemp.com/knowledge-hub/review-of-epo-software-decisions-in-2024/); [Google WO2018057302A1](https://patents.google.com/patent/WO2018057302A1/en); [EP 4083868](https://data.epo.org/gpi/EP4083868A1); [arXiv 2211.09320 (gradient compression)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2211.09320).
- CNIPA: a model works as a technical solution where it reduces data-storage or transmission volume or improves processing speed, with attribution to the claimed solution — [KTS](https://ktslaw.com/insights/alert/2026/7/chinas-revised-patent-examination-guidelines-for-ai-six-practice-tips-for-drafters).
- Trade-secret alternative: commentary notes *Recentive* raises the value of trade secrecy for ML innovation — [WilmerHale](https://www.wilmerhale.com/en/insights/client-alerts/20250429-federal-circuit-opinion-on-patent-eligibility-of-machine-learning-applications-underscores-potential-value-of-trade-secrecy-to-protecting-ai-innovation).

### Inferences — practical claim-drafting guide for a federated-learning method (synthesis; not from a single source; test against counsel's judgment)
1. **Pick one improvement axis and claim its mechanism.** Choose from: *communication cost* (e.g., client computes a sparse/quantised/low-rank update using a claimed rule and transmits only indices+values, server reconstructs by a claimed operator; cite bytes per round and rounds-to-target), *privacy guarantee* (e.g., clip-and-noise with a client-side calibrated noise scale derived from a claimed rule, with a bounded privacy budget accounted across rounds; or secure aggregation with a specific masking/key scheme), *convergence/accuracy under heterogeneity* (e.g., drift-correcting term, adaptive weighting by a named statistic), *security/robustness* (e.g., claimed outlier-update detection and exclusion before aggregation, TEE attestation before accepting an update), *memory/compute at the edge*. This mirrors the Desjardins improvement ("how the model itself operates"), Example 47/48, the EPO "technical implementation" requirement and the Chinese "reduced transmission" criterion.
2. **Put the improvement in the claim, not only in the specification.** Example 48 and Desjardins ("reflect the improvement identified in the specification") mean the independent claim should recite the steps that produce the improvement; avoid result-only language ("such that communication cost is reduced").
3. **Avoid reciting the math as the point of novelty; recite operations on technical data.** Claim "transmit a compressed update over a network link" and the specific data structure, not just "compute gradient". Keep formulas in dependent claims or the specification; the Aug 2025 memo says naming "training a neural network" is not itself a recited exception, but explicit formulas can be.
4. **Tie to a technical setting where the invention is domain-specific** (e.g., medical image segmentation across hospital PACS sites with specified image-quality metric, or wireless devices with bandwidth constraints), per T 702/20 / T 80/23 (generic task) and G-II 3.3.1 (technical purpose). But do not rely on field-of-use alone: *Recentive* and *Dental Monitoring* rejected it, so the domain limitation is for EPO/UK/CN/IN, while the mechanism carries US eligibility.
5. **Add a downstream technical action** (control of a device, scheduling of radio resources, adjusting an imaging acquisition parameter, triggering a security action) in a dependent or alternative independent claim (Example 49; heart-monitor example).
6. **Claim set architecture:** client-side method; server-side method; system (server + clients with memory storing instructions); CRM; with a fallback ladder of narrower mechanisms. Avoid pure "module configured to" functional language without algorithmic structure (a § 112(f)/(b) risk; not source-verified).
7. **Specification support:** the technical problem, the prior-art limitation, the mechanism, ablations, quantitative comparison to FedAvg/baselines, and ranges actually tested (Q3 checklist). In the US prepare a SMED with first-hand declarant data if § 101 is raised. For India, state "technical problem / technical solution / technical effect" in the specification in those words. For China, recite effect-bearing features in the claims and give embodiments.
8. **Prior-art positioning:** distinguish explicitly from Google's structured/sketched updates (US 10,657,461 family), FedAvg, and hospital-FL work such as Sheller 2018; show a specific unexpected effect to support non-obviousness.
9. **Inventorship hygiene:** keep human conception records (Q2).
