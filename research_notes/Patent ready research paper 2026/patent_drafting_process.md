## 1. What are the exact sections of a provisional versus a complete specification, and what quality bar must a provisional meet to support later claims?

### Takeaway
A US provisional needs no claims, no formal oath and no examination, but whatever it says must satisfy 35 U.S.C. 112(a) (written description and enablement) for each later claim, or that claim gets no priority date. An Indian provisional specification needs only a title and a description, but the complete specification must be filed within 12 months and must add claims, an abstract and the best method; claims that go beyond the provisional's disclosure take the later date.

### Cited Findings

**US provisional: contents and quality bar**
- Claims are not required in a provisional; the specification should preferably follow the nonprovisional arrangement, but it must comply with 35 U.S.C. 112(a) and refer to drawings where necessary for understanding — [MPEP 601](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/s601.html)
- The benefit of a provisional attaches claim by claim: the effective filing date is the provisional date only "for any claims which are fully supported under 112" by the provisional — [MPEP 211](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/s211.html)
- The provisional's written description and drawings must "adequately support and enable the subject matter of the claim in the later filed nonprovisional"; a claim gets no benefit if the earlier disclosure does not enable a skilled person to "use" the claimed invention. MPEP 211.05 summarises a case where a provisional did not support an "angle limitation", so the patent lost the provisional date and was invalid over intervening art — [MPEP 211](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/s211.html)
- Best mode disclosure is excused for benefit purposes (112(a) "other than the requirement to disclose the best mode") — [MPEP 211](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/s211.html)
- Filing mechanics: a cover sheet (Form SB/16, an Application Data Sheet, or a cover letter identifying the filing as provisional under 37 CFR 1.51(c)(1)) is needed, otherwise the paper is treated as a nonprovisional under 1.53(b); the SB/16 copy found was labelled "approved through 05/31/2024", so use the current form — [USPTO provisional applications guide](https://www.uspto.gov/sites/default/files/documents/provisional_applications.pdf); [MPEP 201.04 (annotated)](https://blueironip.com/mpep/mpep-201-04/)
- A provisional is not examined and is automatically abandoned 12 months after filing; priority can be restored by petition under 37 CFR 1.78(b) if the nonprovisional (or PCT designating the US) is filed within 14 months and the delay was unintentional, with a petition fee — [BlueIron IP on 1.78(b) restoration](https://blueironip.com/?p=9838)
- A provisional is not published; if a later nonprovisional claims it, the provisional becomes publicly viewable about 18 months after its date (unless non-publication was requested in the nonprovisional); a never-converted provisional stays confidential — [Patent Trademark Blog](https://www.patenttrademarkblog.com/provisional-patent-applications-published-or-made-publicly-available/)
- Provisionals may still be filed in PDF; the $400 non-DOCX surcharge (effective 17 Jan 2024) applies to regular nonprovisional utility filings, not provisionals, and drawings are not subject to the DOCX rule — [Mondaq/firm news on DOCX fee](https://www.mondaq.co.uk/pressrelease/129820/uspto-reiterates-january-17-2024-as-the-effective-date-of-the-non-docx-filing-fee-december-22-2023-firm-news); [IPWatchdog DOCX guide](https://ipwatchdog.com/2024/03/11/mastering-uspto-docx-formats-ultimate-guide/)

**US complete (nonprovisional) specification anatomy**
- 37 CFR 1.77(a): filing papers should appear in order: transmittal form, fee transmittal, Application Data Sheet, specification, drawings, then inventor's oath/declaration — [37 CFR 1.77 text](https://law.counselstack.com/cfr/37/1/1.77)
- 37 CFR 1.77(b) specification order: title; cross-reference to related applications; statement regarding federally sponsored research/development; names of parties to a joint research agreement; incorporation-by-reference statement; then background, summary, brief description of the several views of the drawings, detailed description (item 10), claims (item 11), abstract (item 12); item (13) onward was cut off in the result and should be checked — [37 CFR 1.77 text](https://law.counselstack.com/cfr/37/1/1.77); [MPEP 608.01(a)](https://blueironip.com/mpep/mpep-608-01-a/)
- MPEP treats this order as preferable, not mandatory; headings in uppercase without underlining/bold; empty sections should read "Not Applicable"; provisionals use the same general format but claims are optional — [MPEP 608.01(a)](https://blueironip.com/mpep/mpep-608-01-a/)

**India: provisional vs complete specification (Patents Act 1970)**
- Section 9(1): a complete specification must be filed within 12 months of the provisional filing date or the application is deemed abandoned; Section 9(2): one complete specification may cover several cognate provisionals of the same applicant; Section 9(4): after filing the complete specification the applicant may ask (before grant) to cancel the provisional and post-date the application to the complete specification — [IP India s.9 text](https://ipindia.gov.in/writereaddata/Portal/ev/sections/ps9.html); [IP India s.9](https://ipindia.gov.in/acts/patent-act-1970/section-9)
- Conflict: one version of s.9(1) carries a proviso allowing the complete specification up to 15 months with a request and fee; the IP India text retrieved omitted it, and another source says there is no ordinary extension — [Intepat on provisional/complete](https://www.intepat.com/blog/patent/can-draft-patent-specification/); [AdvocateKhoj s.9](https://www.advocatekhoj.com/library/bareacts/patents/9.php)
- A provisional specification needs a title and a description (field, background, objects, statement of invention) but no claims or abstract; the Manual advises including as much information as the applicant has at filing — [Intepat on specifications](https://www.intepat.com/blog/understanding-the-patent-specification-of-an-invention); [Maheshwari & Co](https://www.maheshwariandco.com/blog/provisional-vs-complete-patent/)
- Complete specification (s.10(4)-(5)): full and particular description of the invention and its operation, the best method known to the applicant, claims at the end (single invention or group forming a single inventive concept, clear, succinct, fairly based on the disclosure), and an abstract accompanying it — [IP India s.10](https://ipindia.gov.in/writereaddata/Portal/ev/sections/ps10.html); [Intepat on specifications](https://www.intepat.com/blog/understanding-the-patent-specification-of-an-invention)
- Specification is filed in Form 2 (Rule 13(1)); title on first page, under about 15 words, should correspond to the broadest claim, without inventor names or the word "patent"; abstract max 150 words (stated in a commentary on the 2025 CRI guidelines) — [Intepat on drafting](https://www.intepat.com/blog/patent/can-draft-patent-specification/); [Conventus/Mondaq on 2025 CRI guidelines](https://conventuslaw.com/report/india-a-detailed-analysis-of-indias-new-cri-guidelines-2025/)
- A claim gets the provisional's earlier priority date only if the provisional supports it; claims beyond the provisional's disclosure carry the complete specification's later date — [Intepat on drafting](https://www.intepat.com/blog/patent/can-draft-patent-specification/)
- Convention, PCT national-phase and divisional applications cannot use the provisional route in India — [Intepat on drafting](https://www.intepat.com/blog/patent/can-draft-patent-specification/)
- The Manual of Patent Office Practice and Procedure is a practical guide without the force of law; IP India's publications page lists the 2019 Manual as current and a draft Manual (version 4.0, 2026) has been circulated, status unconfirmed — [IP India publications/manuals](https://ipindia.gov.in/publications-manuals); [Intepat on provisional/complete](https://www.intepat.com/blog/patent/can-draft-patent-specification/)

**India: the form set a filer will meet**
- Form 1 (application), Form 2 (provisional/complete specification), Form 3 (statement and undertaking on foreign filings; due with the application or within six months), Form 5 (declaration of inventorship, filed within one month where priority is claimed or a complete specification follows a provisional), Form 26 (agent authorisation), Form 9 (early publication), Form 18/18A (examination request), Form 28 (small entity/startup/educational institution declaration) — [Banana IP filing requirements](https://www.bananaip.com/intellepedia/?p=1336); [Banana IP how to file](https://www.bananaip.com/intellepedia/?p=32299)
- Patents (Amendment) Rules 2024 (effective 15 Mar 2024): Form 3 is now filed with the application (or within six months) and again within three months of the First Examination Report, with extension of up to three months via Form 4; the Controller can still require a fresh Form 3 within two months — [Mondaq on Patent Amendment Rules 2024](https://www.mondaq.com/india/patent/1445504/patent-amendment-rules-2024); [Intepat on Section 39/foreign filings](https://www.intepat.com/blog/section-8-form-3-pct-national-phase)

**EPO sufficiency and fee-relevant length limits**
- EPO Guidelines 2026 (in force 1 April 2026) add explanation on insufficient disclosure for AI inventions: applicants should disclose mathematical methods and training datasets in enough detail that a skilled person can reproduce the invention and the stated results are repeatable — [Solve Intelligence on EPO Guidelines 2026](https://www.solveintelligence.com/blog/post/epo-guidelines-2026-key-changes); [Marks & Clerk on 2026 Guidelines](https://www.marks-clerk.com/insights/latest-insights/102mn4e-2026-epo-guidelines-how-the-epo-is-approaching-ai-in-practice/)
- EPO excess-page fee is €17 per page from the 36th page and excess claims 16th-50th cost €290 each from 1 April 2026 — [Forresters on EPO fees 2026](https://forresters-ip.com/changes-to-fees-at-the-epo-from-1-april-2026/); [WPT on EPO fee increases 2026](https://www.wpt.co.uk/news/epo-fee-increases-2026)

**Claim anatomy (independent/dependent; system-method-medium)**
- Software practitioners usually work with three claim families: apparatus/system, process/method and computer-readable-medium (CRM) claims; CRM claims may avoid some Williamson problems — [Finnegan on CRM claims](https://www.finnegan.com/en/insights/articles/the-computer-readable-medium-claim-the-best-of-the-apparatus-and.html)
- Williamson v. Citrix (en banc, 2015): a "distributed learning module" was treated as means-plus-function under 112(f) without the word "means"; for computer-implemented functions the specification must disclose an algorithm; generic placeholders like "module" are high risk, whereas "processor", "circuitry" were held not to trigger 112(f) — [Greenberg Traurig (gtlaw) on software drafting](https://gtlaw.com/ar/insights/2015/11/drafting-software-patents-after-recent-federal-circuit-decisions); [GFR Law on software MPF claiming](https://gfrlaw.com/what-we-do/insights/software-means-plus-function-claiming)
- Independent claims give broad coverage; each dependent claim refers to an independent claim or an intervening dependent claim and adds limits (e.g., "wherein the algorithm is supervised") — [PatentPC on AI/ML claim drafting](https://patentpc.com/blog/tips-for-drafting-patent-claims-for-ai-and-machine-learning-inventions); [Mondaq on supervised-ML claim drafting](https://webiis08.mondaq.com/unitedstates/patent/1059082/drafting-claims-for-supervised-machine-learning-systems)
- ML inventions typically innovate in: model structure, training process, input data preparation, input mapping to the model, and post-processing of outputs; consider claiming the training method itself and claim the same novel feature in both application and training claims — [Patentdocs (Borella) on ML claims](https://patentdocs.org/2018/11/25/how-to-draft-patent-claims-for-machine-learning-inventions/); [Mondaq on supervised-ML claim drafting](https://webiis08.mondaq.com/unitedstates/patent/1059082/drafting-claims-for-supervised-machine-learning-systems)
- India: Section 3(k) does not limit grants to only system or only method claims; both can be independent claims if supported; CRM and computer-program-product claims were reported as still non-patentable under the 2025 CRI guidelines; means-plus-function terms require disclosed structure in the specification — [Conventus/Mondaq on 2025 CRI guidelines](https://conventuslaw.com/report/india-a-detailed-analysis-of-indias-new-cri-guidelines-2025/)

### Inferences
- The practical quality bar for a provisional is "a complete specification without formalities": for each claim that may later be filed (system, method, CRM, server-side, client-side, privacy-mechanism variants), the provisional should contain an explicit, enabling description including algorithms, equations, parameters, alternative embodiments and at least one worked example. A manuscript pasted in as the provisional is likely to leave claim-support gaps because papers describe one embodiment and rarely include alternatives or claim-style generalisation (inference from the claim-by-claim support rule in MPEP 211).
- For an Indian filer, the Indian provisional is a cheap placeholder, but because the complete specification must "fairly base" its claims on the provisional's disclosure to keep the date, the same "write it like a full spec" advice applies.
- Section order for any jurisdiction can follow: Title; Cross-reference; Statement of government support (if any); Field; Background; Summary; Brief description of drawings; Detailed description; Claims; Abstract. This is consistent with 37 CFR 1.77(b) and Indian Form 2 practice, but the Indian-specific headings (field, objects, statement of invention) are only reported by secondary sources.

### Gaps
- Current text of MPEP 608.01 and 37 CFR 1.77(b)(13+) could not be read; EPC Rules 42-43 (content of description and claims) and PCT Rule 5 were not retrieved.
- The Indian Manual's chapter on the structure of a specification was not retrieved; the Section 9 15-month proviso conflict remains unresolved.
- No source was found on whether the best-mode requirement still matters for US validity (it is excused for benefit claims per MPEP 211 as quoted, but its status as an invalidity ground was not searched).

## 2. What drawing and figure types does a software/ML patent application typically need, and what are the formal requirements?

### Takeaway
US rules require the drawing to show every claimed feature, but conventional components may be shown as labelled boxes; flowcharts and block diagrams are the natural vehicles. Formal rules differ (US 37 CFR 1.84: black ink, A4 or letter, 1 inch top/left margins; India Rule 15: A4 with 4 cm top/left and 3 cm bottom/right margins), and for Indian computer-related inventions the CRI guidelines expect flowcharts for methods and component-connectivity drawings.

### Cited Findings

**US requirements**
- 37 CFR 1.83(a): the drawing in a nonprovisional must show every feature specified in the claims; conventional features whose detailed illustration is not essential should appear as a graphical symbol or "a labeled rectangular box"; tables and sequence listings should not be duplicated in the drawings — [37 CFR 1.83 text](https://law.counselstack.com/cfr/37/1/1.83)
- Examiner form paragraph for missing features: "The drawings are objected to under 37 CFR 1.83(a) because they fail to show [..] as described in the specification" — [MPEP 608.02(d)](https://blueironip.com/mpep/mpep-608-02-d/)
- 37 CFR 1.84(a): drawings in black ink with solid black lines; color only where it is the only practical medium and must remain reproducible in black and white — [37 CFR 1.84 (Cornell LII)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/37/1.84); [BlueIron on 1.84](https://blueironip.com/?p=25244)
- Sheet size A4 (21.0 x 29.7 cm) or 8.5 x 11 in; margins top 2.5 cm (1 in), left 2.5 cm (1 in), right 1.5 cm (5/8 in), bottom 1.0 cm (3/8 in); usable area up to 17.0 x 26.2 cm on A4; no frames around the usable area; scan cross-hair targets at two diagonally opposite margin corners — [37 CFR 1.84 (Cornell LII)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/37/1.84); [BlueIron on size and margins](https://blueironip.com/?p=9776)
- Reference characters (1.84(p)): Arabic numerals, at least 1/8 inch high, not on hatched areas; the same part always carries the same character and one character must not designate different parts — [BlueIron on 1.84](https://blueironip.com/?p=25244); [MPEP 608.02(e)](https://blueironip.com/mpep/mpep-608-02-e/)
- eCFR showed 1.84 as current through July 2026 in the search result; some mirror sites carry older wording — [eCFR 1.84](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-37/chapter-I/subchapter-A/part-1/subpart-B/subject-group-ECFRc7605aa2d3f3782/section-1.84)
- Application-size fee: $450 (undiscounted), $180 (small), $90 (micro) per additional 50 sheets of specification plus drawings beyond 100 sheets — [USPTO fee schedule](https://www.uspto.gov/sites/default/files/documents/USPTO-fee-schedule_current.pdf)

**India (Patents Rules 2003, Rule 15) and CRI practice**
- Drawings on standard A4 sheets with clear margins of at least 4 cm on top and left and 3 cm at bottom and right; applicant-supplied drawings accompany the specification; no drawing or sketch needing special illustration may appear in the specification text; at least one neat copy on durable paper; no dimensions marked on the drawing — [IP India Rule 15](https://ipindia.gov.in/writereaddata/portal/ev/rules/pr15.html); [IP India Rule 15 (acts)](https://www.ipindia.gov.in/acts/patent-rules-2003/rule-15)
- Sheets must be numbered and bear the applicant's name at top-left corner; a secondary summary adds consecutive sheet number and signature, and says a drawing sheet may carry no descriptive matter except flow diagrams where required (the official text retrieved was truncated before this clause) — [IP India Rule 15](https://ipindia.gov.in/writereaddata/portal/ev/rules/pr15.html); [Kanakkupillai on patent drawings](https://www.kanakkupillai.com/patent-drawing)
- Under the 2017 CRI guidelines the specification should show each feature with illustrative drawings (product claims), the necessary sequence of steps with flowcharts (process claims), the working relationship and connectivity of components, and the desired result; neither drawings nor flowcharts are mandatory under the Act itself — [IAM on post-CRI drafting practice](https://www.iam-media.com/article/software-patent-application-drafting-practices-post-cri-examination-guidelines); [Kanakkupillai on patent drawings](https://www.kanakkupillai.com/patent-drawing)
- Block diagrams in "hollow rectangles interconnected by lines" form are described in the US MPEP ("In a typical computer application, system components are often represented in a block diagram format"), not in Indian sources found — [IAM on post-CRI drafting practice](https://www.iam-media.com/article/software-patent-application-drafting-practices-post-cri-examination-guidelines)
- The 2025 Indian CRI guidelines (issued 29 July 2025, replacing the 2017 version; one commentary still called them a draft) require the claimed subject matter to be tied to the technical problem solved and its implementation (2025 change) — [Conventus/Mondaq on 2025 CRI guidelines](https://conventuslaw.com/report/india-a-detailed-analysis-of-indias-new-cri-guidelines-2025/); [AIPPI: Decoding CRI Guidelines 2025](https://www.aippi.org/news/decoding-cri-guidelines-2025/)

### Inferences
- A typical drawing set for a federated-learning (FL) invention that satisfies 1.83(a) and the Indian CRI expectations would include: (1) system architecture block diagram (aggregation server, client devices/silos, network, optional secure-aggregation or trusted-execution component, each as labelled boxes); (2) server-side method flowchart (initialise, select clients, broadcast, receive updates, aggregate, update global model, convergence check); (3) client-side method flowchart (receive model, local training on private data, clip/noise/encrypt, upload); (4) sequence diagram of one training round across server and clients (shows the actor split relevant to claim drafting); (5) data-flow diagram of the privacy mechanism (e.g., differential-privacy noise, homomorphic encryption or secret sharing) and of the novel aggregation or weighting rule; (6) model or module architecture diagram; (7) hardware block diagram of a representative device; (8) optional graphs of experimental results (these also support enablement and technical effect). Sequence and data-flow diagrams are standard practice but no retrieved authority specifically requires or defines them; the only retrieved textual support is 1.83(a) (labelled boxes) and the Indian CRI flowchart/connectivity expectation.
- Every element recited in any claim should appear as a numbered box or step in at least one figure; maintain a reference-numeral register to meet 1.84(p) consistency (same numeral = same part).
- Prepare drawings once in A4 with margins satisfying the stricter of the two regimes (Indian 4 cm top/left, 3 cm bottom/right) to avoid redrawing for US/PCT/India.

### Gaps
- No retrieved source defines sequence diagrams or data-flow diagrams as required; the Indian Manual's drawings chapter, EPC Rule 46 and PCT Rule 11 (drawing rules for PCT filings) were not retrieved. An IP5 document "Allowable Features in Drawings" (link.epo.org/ip5) exists but its content was not read.
- The 2025 CRI guidelines' own drawing/flowchart provisions were not retrieved; the 2017 summary may be outdated.
- Whether provisionals require formal-quality drawings was not found (MPEP 601 only says to refer to drawings where necessary).

## 3. What is the typical timeline and cost for US, India and PCT paths in 2026, and which fast-track or reduced-fee options exist?

### Takeaway
US government fees (effective 19 Jan 2025, schedule "last revised 14 Aug 2026") are about $2,000 undiscounted, about $800 small entity and about $400 micro entity for filing+search+examination of a basic utility application, and $325/$130/$65 for a provisional; US first-action pendency is reported at about 22.6 months, with Track One as the paid fast track. India's official fees are 80% lower for natural persons, startups and educational institutions, the examination request is now due within 31 months of filing/priority for applications filed from 15 Mar 2024, and expedited examination (Form 18A) is available for startups, small entities and applicants who chose India as ISA/IPEA. The EPO raised most fees about 5% on 1 April 2026, and PCT international filing is CHF 1,330 (+CHF 15 per sheet over 30), with national phase at 30 months (US) or 31 months (EPO, India).

## 4. How do inventorship, authorship, employer/university ownership and funder rules interact?

### Takeaway
Inventorship (US 35 U.S.C. 116) turns on contribution to the conception of at least one claim and may legitimately differ from the ICMJE-style authorship list; getting it wrong can invalidate a patent. Ownership is separate: it flows from employment/IP-policy assignments (present-tense "hereby assign" language matters, per Stanford v. Roche), and public funding adds disclosure/election/filing deadlines (US Bayh-Dole) or funder-specific rules (India: ANRF, DBT, ICMR, institution policies).

### Cited Findings

**Inventorship versus authorship**
- Patent inventorship is a legal question of conception; each joint inventor must contribute in some significant manner to the conception of at least one claim, but need not have worked together, contributed equally or contributed to every claim (35 U.S.C. 116(a)) — [KTS Law inventorship primer](https://ktslaw.com/-/media/2019/ip-desk-reference-2015/navigating-the-muddy-waters-of-inventorship.pdf); [Tucker on 35 USC 116](https://www.tuckerup.com/?p=8046)
- ICMJE authorship requires all four criteria (substantial contributions to conception/design or data acquisition/analysis/interpretation; drafting or critical revision; final approval; accountability); funding acquisition, providing materials or general supervision alone do not qualify — [CASRAI/ICMJE criteria guide](https://casrai.org/guides/icmje-authorship-criteria)
- Nonjoinder (omitting a true inventor) and misjoinder (naming someone who contributed to no issued claim) are the two error types; the AIA removed the deceptive-intent condition; Fortress Iron v. Digger Specialties (Fed. Cir., 2 Apr 2026) affirmed invalidity of two patents for failure to name a co-inventor and refused correction because the s.256 notice-and-hearing prerequisite could not be satisfied (2025-26 change) — [JD Supra on Fortress Iron](https://www.jdsupra.com:443/legalnews/fortress-iron-v-digger-specialties-8939064/); [Miller Johnson on unnamed inventors](https://millerjohnson.com/unnamed-inventor-invalid-patent/)
- Courts do not correct inventorship of pending applications; the USPTO Director does — [Casrai guide on correcting inventorship](https://www.casrai.org/guides/correcting-inventorship-issued-patent)
- Every inventor holds an equal, undivided interest until conveyed — [Miller Nash on inventorship](https://www.millernash.com/industry-news/patent-inventorship-key-considerations)
- AI-assisted inventions: on 26-28 Nov 2025 the USPTO rescinded its Feb 2024 guidance (including the Pannu-factor approach for AI-assisted inventions); AI is treated as a tool, only natural persons can be inventors, and the test remains conception ("definite and permanent idea of the claimed invention"); the Pannu factors apply only among multiple natural persons; applications naming AI as inventor face rejection (2025-26 change) — [USPTO notice](https://www.uspto.gov/subscription-center/2025/revised-inventorship-guidance-ai-assisted-inventions); [Federal Register 28 Nov 2025](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2025-11-28/html/2025-21457.htm); [Morgan Lewis](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2025/12/uspto-issues-revised-inventorship-guidance-for-ai-assisted-inventions)
- India: Form 5 is the inventorship declaration naming the "true and first inventors"; a patent application may be filed by the true and first inventors or an assignee who acquired authority from them — [Banana IP filing requirements](https://www.bananaip.com/intellepedia/?p=1336); [Banana IP how to file](https://www.bananaip.com/intellepedia/?p=32299)

**Employer/university ownership and assignment**
- Stanford v. Roche: the Federal Circuit read "agree to assign" (Stanford) as a promise to assign in future and "do hereby assign" (Cetus) as a present assignment of future inventions; Roche's predecessor thus gained title first and Stanford lacked standing; the Supreme Court held Bayh-Dole does not automatically vest title in federal contractors and did not review the contract reading — [Perkins Coie](https://perkinscoie.com/en/news-insights/bd-of-trs-of-the-leland-stanford-junior-univ-v-roche-molecular.html); [Taft](https://taftlaw.com/news-events/law-bulletins/stanford-university-v-roche-molecular-131-s-ct-2188-2011); [Waltmire on assignment language](https://www.waltmire.com/2023/01/27/language-for-assigning-patents-and-patentable-inventions-in-employment-or-consulting-agreements/)
- US university policy examples: University of Washington requires employees to assign inventions as a condition of employment; Johns Hopkins leaves ownership with the inventor when the university provides no support; Catholic University gives inventors 100% of net royalties to $10,000 then a 50/50 split, subject to funder limits — [UW policy](https://policy.uw.edu/?p=1044); [Johns Hopkins IP policy](https://ventures.jhu.edu/the-johns-hopkins-ip-policy); [Catholic University patent policy](https://policies.catholic.edu/faculty-staff/intellectualproperty/patent.html)
- Indian institute examples (from a campus explainer, not the official texts): IIT Bombay inventor share 70% up to INR 100 lakh, 50% for the next 100 lakh, 30% beyond; IIT Kanpur 65/45/25%; IIT-K and IIT-D hold a fixed 10% of royalty for IP promotion; IIT Kanpur's policy leaves IP with the inventor where no institute-ownership situation applies; IIT-BHU claims IP created with substantial institute support — [Insight IIT Bombay explainer](https://insightiitb.org/yours-intellectually-the-ip-policy/); [IIT Kanpur IPR policy](https://www.iitk.ac.in/ipr/data/IPR-Policy.pdf); [IIT-BHU IPR cell](https://iitbhu.ac.in/contents/institute/dean/dord/doc/dord_ipr_cell.pdf)
- A law-firm commentary states Indian law provides no default allocation between employer and employee inventors, so title depends on a written, executed assignment recommended at each disclosure and filing — [Khurana & Khurana on assignment clauses](https://www.khuranaandkhurana.com/patent-ownership-in-employee-inventions-drafting-assignment-clauses)
- PCT 90% fee reduction is lost if an applicant is obliged to assign to an ineligible party, which matters for employees under mandatory-assignment IP policies — [EPO PCT Guidelines A-III 8.1](https://epo.org/en/legal/guidelines-pct/2025/a_iii_8_1_2.html)
- Micro-entity status is available to applicants employed by, or assigning to, a qualifying institution of higher education — [BlueIron on 1.29(d)](https://blueironip.com/?p=10269)

**Funder obligations**
- US Bayh-Dole (37 CFR Part 401, a NIH page lists last amendment 3/20/2026; a 2026 Federal Register document on Part 401 was seen but not read): disclose each subject invention to the funding agency within two months after the inventor's written disclosure to the contractor's patent staff (via iEdison for NIH); elect title within two years of disclosure (or 60 days before a statutory bar date); file the initial patent application within one year after election (a provisional counts); foreign/PCT filings within 10 months of initial filing; include a government-support statement in the specification of provisional, nonprovisional and PCT applications; confirmatory license once a nonprovisional is filed; failure to disclose/elect/file can force conveyance of title if the agency acts within 60 days of learning; agencies may grant extensions; awards before 1 Oct 2018 may follow older terms — [Sterne Kessler Bayh-Dole tab](https://www.sternekessler.com/app/uploads/2023/05/tab_14_-_federally_funded_inventions_and_compliance_with_the_bayh-dole_act.pdf); [Fenwick on Bayh-Dole requirements](https://www.fenwick.com/insights/publications/avoiding-loss-of-title-to-inventions-made-under-federal-funding-as-new-bayh-dole-requirements-affect-patent-rights); [NIST iEdison](https://www.nist.gov/iedison/about-iedison); [DOE patent reporting](https://science.osti.gov/OCC-CH/Invention-and-Patent-Reporting)
- India has no Bayh-Dole-style statute; the earlier Protection and Utilisation of Publicly Funded IP Bill was never enacted, and ownership follows funding agreement and institution policy — [CASRAI summary of India IPR policy/ANRF](https://casrai.org/guides/india-national-ipr-policy-anrf-tech-transfer); [SpicyIP on publicly funded R&D bill](https://spicyip.com/2008/07/public-funded-r-d-bill-does-india-need.html)
- ANRF (Anusandhan National Research Foundation, 2023 Act): IP from ANRF-funded research vests in the grantee institution by default; for fellowships the host institution's IP policy applies; ANRF may impose licensing principles (including open licensing) or deviate for critical/sensitive areas; the guideline document appears to be a February 2026 revision (2025-26 change) — [ANRF IP policy guideline (IISc-hosted PDF)](https://org.iisc.ac.in/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/ANRF_IP_PolicyGuideline_REV_clean_9_2_26.pdf)
- DBT: patent may name inventors but must be assigned to the institution and DBT, GoI; institution retains earnings but inventor share is capped at one-third of actual earnings and at least 25% goes to a Patent Facilitating Fund; joint industry-institution IP may be co-owned under a written agreement — [DBT Annexure I](https://dbtindia.gov.in/sites/default/files/Annexure-I_2.pdf)
- ICMR: intramural IP is Council-owned with revenue shared with employee inventors; extramural IP is generally ICMR-owned but negotiable; the IPR Unit helps scientists seek patent protection before publication — [ICMR IP management document](https://www.dndi.org/wp-content/uploads/2010/12/ip_management_icmr.pdf)

### Inferences
- Because ICMJE authorship rewards writing, accountability and data work, whereas inventorship rewards conception of claimed features, a statistician, data engineer or senior supervisor may be an author but not an inventor, and a person who conceived a claimed aggregation or privacy feature but did not write the manuscript may be an inventor but not an author. The lists can legitimately differ and should be decided by a claim-by-claim conception analysis with the patent attorney, and re-checked whenever claims are amended or narrowed during prosecution.
- Keep a dated contribution log (lab notebook or Git history) before filing; with AI coding assistants in the workflow, document the human conception of the claimed features (consistent with the Nov 2025 USPTO position).
- Before filing, obtain written present-tense assignments from every inventor to the institution (or confirm the institution's policy contains one) and confirm funding sources so the right government-support statement and disclosures (iEdison or Indian funder reporting) are made on the first filing.
- Entity status choices (US micro/small, India educational institution/startup, EPO Rule 7a(3), PCT natural-person reduction) are all sensitive to who the applicant is and who has assigned what; decide ownership before choosing the fee category.

### Gaps
- Official IIT/IISc policy texts and current royalty splits were not retrieved (percentages above come from a campus explainer); DST's current IP guidance and ANRF Annexure-A (commercialisation terms and inventor share) could not be retrieved.
- No source was found on Indian employee-inventor remuneration rules beyond the commentary cited; EPO/UK employee-inventor rules were not researched.
- The 2026 Federal Register document on Part 401 (2026-19088) and the 3/20/2026 eCFR amendment were not read, so any changes in Bayh-Dole timelines in 2026 are unverified.

## 5. How should a researcher align the paper and the patent to avoid disclosure mistakes and keep claims strong?

### Takeaway
File (at least a provisional) before any public disclosure, because only the US has a general 12-month inventor grace period; India's grace period is narrow and conditional, the EPO has almost none, and China/Japan differ. After filing, the paper should disclose what is needed for science but avoid admissions, contradictions with the claims, or mislabeling pending applications; "patent pending" is accurate once an application (including a provisional) is on file in the US, and pending applications should be declared as competing interests.

### Cited Findings

**Grace periods and the file-first rule**
- US (AIA 102(b)(1)): inventor-originated disclosures within one year before the effective filing date (which can be a provisional's date) are not prior art; third-party independent disclosures are not excused — [BlueIron on 102(b)(1) grace](https://blueironip.com/?p=18013); [MPEP 2153](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/s2153.html)
- If a journal article lists a co-author who is not a named inventor, an examiner may still apply it, and the applicant may need a Rule 130(a) declaration to show it is inventor-originated — [CLFIP on MPEP 2155.01](https://www.clfip.com/ip/blog/mpep/2155-1/); [MPEP 2153](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/s2153.html)
- India has no general grace period: s.31 covers only a notified exhibition (and publications arising from it), unauthorised use during it, and a paper read before / published in transactions of a learned society, each with a 12-month window and Form 31 under Rule 29A; ordinary journal articles, conference papers and preprints should not be assumed to qualify — [Intepat on s.31](https://www.intepat.com/blog/grace-period-under-section-31-of-the-patents-act-in-india); [Asia IP on public display as prior art](https://asiaiplaw.com/section/ip-analysts/when-does-public-display-become-prior-art-anticipation-under-the-patents-act-1970)
- EPO (Art. 55 EPC): only disclosures within six months before the actual filing date that result from evident abuse or display at an official/officially recognised exhibition are disregarded; there is no general grace period — [EPC Art. 55](https://www.epo.org/law-practice/legal-texts/html/epc/2020/e/ar55.html); [EPO case law on Art. 55](https://www.epo.org/en/legal/case-law/2025/clr_i_c_2_5.html)
- China: 6 months, narrow, novelty only; Japan: 12 months with procedural requirements — [Sterne Kessler on IP5 grace periods](https://www.sternekessler.com/news-insights/insights/patent-grace-period-laws-ip5-patent-offices-some-similarities-largely/)
- University tech-transfer guidance: preprints can compress review into days and destroy European novelty; file a provisional before posting; count every channel (repositories, talks, posters, social media, demonstrations) as publication if there is no confidentiality; once a patent application is filed, researchers can generally publish without losing the filing date; consult the tech-transfer office before submitting a manuscript or preprint — [Bionow on preprints and IP](https://bionow.co.uk/news/preprints-and-social-media-balancing-early-publication-commercial-ip-protection); [Emory tech transfer on preprints](https://scholarblogs.emory.edu/techtransfer/?p=2914); [Preprints.org on IP](https://www.preprints.org/blog/post/protecting-your-intellectual-property)

**Confidentiality / NDAs**
- EPO: prior art must be available to the public without any bar of confidentiality; confidentiality can be implicit but depends on facts (commercial sale is incompatible with an implied duty; T 2702/18 found no implicit NDA; public-body participants raise doubt); explicit NDAs are assessed document by document — [EPO case law on secrecy](https://www.epo.org/en/legal/case-law/2022/clr_i_c_3_4_12.html); [EPO T 0945/09](https://legacy.epo.org/boards-of-appeal/decisions/pdf/t090945eu1.pdf)
- US: Helsinn v. Teva (2019) held that a confidential sale still triggers the on-sale bar, so a confidential supply/evaluation deal can start the one-year clock — [Mondaq on Helsinn](https://www.mondaq.com/unitedstates/patent/775962/supreme-court-holds-that-confidential-sales-still-qualify-as-patent-prior-art); [IAM on secret sales](https://www.iam-media.com/global-guide/iam-yearbook/2019/article/patent-confidential-sale-bar-and-secret-sales-in-america-invents-act-era)
- University guidance: sharing with an external collaborator without an NDA leaves little chance of patenting; a meeting where all attendees are bound by confidentiality is not a public disclosure; sign an NDA before discussing enabling information — [UNT innovation FAQ](https://research.unt.edu/support-units/innovation-commercialization/faqs.html); [Glasgow tech transfer note](https://www.gla.ac.uk/media/Media_829832_smxx.pdf)

**Consistency, admissions and marking**
- MPEP 2129: characterising work in the specification as "prior art" can be treated as an admission usable for anticipation or obviousness even if it would not otherwise qualify under 102; Jepson claim preambles imply admissions; background sections are a common source; merely listing a reference in an IDS is not an admission — [MPEP 2129](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/s2129.html); [BlueIron on admissions](https://blueironip.com/ufaq-category/mpep-2129-admissions-as-prior-art)
- 35 U.S.C. 292: using "patent pending" or "patent applied for" when no application was made or it is no longer pending is false marking; liability requires intent to deceive; fine up to $500 per offence plus competitor suits; a source says "patent pending" can be used once a US application, including a provisional, is on file, but another notes IP Australia views a provisional as not an application per se, so marking rules differ by country; the open question on virtual marking of pending applications was not resolved — [35 U.S.C. 292](https://www.govinfo.gov/link/uscode/35/292); [Day Pitney on "patent pending" (Jan 2026)](https://www.daypitney.com/patently-enabled-january-2026-new-year-new-inventions-the-meaning-of-patent-pending)
- ICMJE conflict-of-interest form section 4 asks about patents "planned, pending or issued" broadly relevant to the work, defines pending as filed but not issued, and covers the 36 months before publication; journals usually ask for updated disclosures at acceptance — [ICMJE disclosure form example](https://cdn-links.lww.com/permalink/jbjs/g/jbjs_2020_07_23_schutgens_19-01056_sdc1.pdf)
- Technical-effect framing differs by office: USPTO (Aug 2025 memo; Ex parte Desjardins, designated precedential 4 Nov 2025) treats claims that improve how a machine-learning model or computer functions as eligible, whereas applying standard ML to new data is insufficient (Recentive v. Fox); Indian 2025 CRI guidelines require a technical problem and real-world technical application; EPO requires technical character and every inventive-step feature to contribute technically, and the 2026 Guidelines demand reproducible disclosure of mathematical methods and training data — [Barnes & Thornburg on Aug 2025 memo](https://btlaw.com/en/insights/alerts/2025/uspto-issues-examiner-reminders-on-ai-and-software-patents); [Bloomberg Law on Desjardins](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/ip-law/ai-patent-eligibility-has-shifted-with-one-machine-learning-case); [Conventus on India CRI 2025](https://conventuslaw.com/report/india-a-detailed-analysis-of-indias-new-cri-guidelines-2025/); [Solve Intelligence on EPO 2026](https://www.solveintelligence.com/blog/post/epo-guidelines-2026-key-changes)

### Inferences
- Recommended order (consistent with the tech-transfer guidance cited): finish experiments -> invention disclosure to the institution/TTO -> prior-art search -> inventorship and ownership decision -> file provisional (US and/or Indian) covering the paper's content plus broader embodiments -> only then submit the manuscript, preprint or talk -> within 12 months file complete specification/PCT. Because India and the EPO treat most pre-filing publications as novelty-destroying, the provisional date must precede the first public release, including arXiv and conference camera-ready deposits.
- Division of content: the paper should carry the scientific narrative, benchmarks and ablations; the specification should carry everything needed for claim support (algorithms, alternatives, variants, parameter ranges, deployment contexts, training-data descriptions) and the claims should be drafted from the technical improvement (communication reduction, convergence, privacy-utility trade-off, robustness) that the paper proves. Anything disclosed in the paper but absent from the provisional is not entitled to the provisional date outside the grace-period jurisdictions.
- Consistency checklist: (1) use the same terms for the same elements in the paper, specification and figures; (2) do not describe your own earlier work or the paper as "prior art" or "conventional" in the background; (3) avoid paper statements such as "a straightforward extension", "trivially", "any standard technique can replace" for features you intend to claim; (4) avoid absolute statements in the paper ("only", "requires") that contradict broader claims or dependent-claim embodiments; (5) make sure experimental claims in the paper (e.g., percent gains) are not stated more broadly than the specification supports; (6) if the paper's author list differs from the inventor list, expect a Rule 130(a)-type explanation; (7) state in the paper's disclosure/competing-interest section that a patent application has been filed (with application number once assigned), without calling it a granted patent; (8) cite a patent number only after grant, and use "patent pending"/"patent applied for" wording while pending; update the status at proofs.
- Each claim should be mapped to a paragraph and a figure in the specification, and the paper's methods section should be cross-checked against that claim-support matrix before submission.

### Gaps
- Journal- and publisher-specific policies (IEEE, Elsevier, Springer, NeurIPS/ICML/ICLR double-blind and preprint rules) were not researched; whether arXiv or a specific conference qualifies as a "learned society" transaction in India is untested.
- The interaction of the admissions doctrine with the US grace period is a search-summary inference, not a quoted holding; confirm with counsel.
- No source was found that confirms whether virtual marking can reference a pending application, or on "patent pending" use for Indian provisionals.

## 6. Prior-art searching and freedom-to-operate basics (tools, keywords, CPC classes relevant to federated learning)

### Takeaway
A free first pass combines Google Patents, Espacenet, Lens.org, WIPO PATENTSCOPE, USPTO Patent Public Search (which replaced PatFT/AppFT in late 2022) and IP India's InPASS, using both keywords and CPC codes; for federated learning the core code is G06N 3/098 (distributed learning, e.g., federated learning), usually with G06N 20/00, H04L 9/008 for homomorphic encryption, and G16H for healthcare applications. Free tools can flag candidates but a freedom-to-operate (FTO) conclusion needs claim-by-claim attorney review.

### Cited Findings
- Tools: Google Patents, Espacenet, Lens.org, WIPO PATENTSCOPE and others are free; use multiple databases; Lens has fine-grained filters but can return empty results for some queries — [UpCounsel patent search tools](https://www.upcounsel.com/patent-search-tools); [Rutgers library guide](https://libguides.rutgers.edu/patents/patent-search)
- US: PatFT and AppFT were retired (30 Sep / 1 Oct 2022 per different USPTO pages) in favour of Patent Public Search, which requires a Chromium-based browser and uses different search fields (a concordance is provided) — [USPTO announcement](https://www.uspto.gov/about-us/news-updates/convenient-patent-public-search-tool-replacing-four-legacy-systems-fall); [Brownstein on legacy retirement](https://www.bhfs.com/insight/uspto-transitions-from-legacy-search-tools-and-databases-to-new-online-search-tool)
- India: InPASS is IP India's free Indian Patent Advanced Search System with full-text search of published applications and granted patents plus legal status, supporting wildcards, truncation and Boolean operators — [Banana IP on InPASS](https://www.bananaip.com/intellepedia/?p=21159); [Intepat InPASS guide](https://www.intepat.com/de/blog/conduct-indian-patent-search)
- CPC G06N 3/098 "Distributed learning, e.g. federated learning" entered the scheme in January 2023, and the CPC office notes the group is incomplete pending reclassification from G06N 3/08, so both G06N 3/08 and G06N 3/098 should be searched — [CPC definition G06N](https://www.uspto.gov/web/patents/classification/cpc/html/defG06N.html); [Patent Classification Explorer G06N 3/098](https://patentclassificationexplorer.com/ipc/g06n-3-098)
- G06N 20/00 is the general machine-learning group (neural networks sit in G06N 3/00) and is often co-assigned with G06N 3/098; for example, a European application lists G06N 3/04, G06N 20/20 and G06N 3/098 together — [Patent Classification Explorer G06N 20/00](https://patentclassificationexplorer.com/ipc/g06n-20-00); [EPO GPI EP 4170498](https://data.epo.org/gpi/EP4170498A3)
- H04L 9/008 (cryptography involving homomorphic encryption) co-occurs with G06N 3/098 in, for example, Nokia's EP 4404105 on keeping the model confidential during federated training — [EP 4404105 publication](https://data.epo.org/publication-server/rest/v1.2/patents/EP4404105NWA1/document.html)
- No federated-specific G16H group was found; healthcare FL patents receive application-based G16H codes such as G16H 50/20 (computer-aided diagnosis) and G16H 50/70 (mining medical data) — [EP 4156201 (Google Patents)](https://patents.google.com/patent/EP4156201C0/de)
- A vendor dataset of 2,019 federated/privacy-preserving patents (cutoff July 2026) reports G06N at about 91% and G16H at about 9% of records, with privacy-focused filings also in H04L 9 and G06F 21 — [Patsnap landscape](https://www.patsnap.com/resources/blog/rd-blog/federated-and-privacy-preserving-learning-patent-landscape/)
- Search method: build a claim tree and list of essential features; use synonym/abbreviation lists; combine keywords with CPC; search cited and citing documents; apply the right cut-off date; include non-patent literature (papers, proceedings, websites) — [WIPO search training deck](https://www.wipo.org/edocs/mdocs/pct/en/wipo_ip_mnl_19/wipo_ip_mnl_19_t4.pdf); [USPTO 7-step search strategy](https://WWW.USPTO.GOV/sites/default/files/documents/patent-7step-classification.pdf)
- FTO: the ability to use technology without infringing others' IP; differs from a novelty search in that it examines claims of active patents (and pending applications) against a product or process; scope by date (about 20 years), jurisdiction and document type; repeat several times during R&D; options on finding a blocking patent are design-around, licence or acquisition; a patent grant on your own invention does not give freedom to operate — [iPleaders on FTO](https://blog.ipleaders.in/all-you-need-to-know-about-freedom-to-operate-search/); [Athena on FTO](https://www.myathena.de/en/patent-management/patent-search/freedom-to-operate-search/); [Mondaq on FTO](https://mondaq.com/uk/patent/1220138/freedom-to-operate)

### Inferences
- Suggested keyword cluster for a federated-learning invention: federated learning, federated averaging/FedAvg, decentralized/distributed training, client/edge/silo, aggregation server, secure aggregation, differential privacy, homomorphic encryption, secret sharing, split learning, personalised/clustered FL, non-IID, communication-efficient, client selection, gradient compression, poisoning/Byzantine robust, blockchain-based FL, plus application terms (medical imaging, EHR, IoT, 6G). The H04L 9/00 family and G06F 21/62 (privacy) are plausible adjacent classes because the sources show privacy-focused FL filings in H04L 9 and G06F 21; specific sub-groups beyond H04L 9/008 were not verified.
- Use Google Patents/Lens for fast scoping, Espacenet for family and classification navigation, InPASS to check Indian status (especially for Section 3(k) and Indian prior filings), and Patent Public Search for US full text; log queries and dates so the search is reproducible and can feed an IDS (US) or Form 3-type disclosures.
- FL claims need an FTO-style check on aggregation, secure-aggregation and DP-in-FL families from large companies; this is an inference from the density of existing FL filings (2,019 records in one vendor dataset).

### Gaps
- Exact current CPC sub-groups under H04L 9/xx (e.g., secret sharing, secure multi-party computation) and G06F 21/6245 were not retrieved; Lens/Espacenet query syntax was not documented; no official IP India page on InPASS was read directly; the EPO's Espacenet and WIPO PATENTSCOPE features were not independently verified.

## 7. Subject-matter eligibility and claim strategy for federated-learning (ML) inventions in the US, India and EPO, including divided infringement

### Takeaway
All three offices require a technical contribution rather than "ML applied to data": the USPTO (post Aug 2025 memo and Ex parte Desjardins) accepts claims that improve how the model/computer functions, India's s.3(k) and 2025 CRI guidelines require a technical effect and implementation detail and treat CRM claims as risky, and the EPO needs technical character plus technical contribution with reproducible disclosure. FL claims should also be drafted so that one actor performs all steps (server-only or client-only claims) to avoid divided infringement.

### Cited Findings
- USPTO 4 Aug 2025 memo (Deputy Commissioner Kim to TC 2100/2600/3600): reminders that a limitation is a mental process only if practically performable in the mind, to analyse the claim as a whole, and to reject only when ineligibility is likely; it cites Recentive Analytics v. Fox for the point that applying standard ML to new data or automating a task is insufficient (2025-26 change) — [Barnes & Thornburg](https://btlaw.com/en/insights/alerts/2025/uspto-issues-examiner-reminders-on-ai-and-software-patents); [Fitch Even](https://www.fitcheven.com/2025/08/13/uspto-issues-guidance-clarifying-subject-matter-eligibility-for-ai-and-software-claims/); [Solve Intelligence](https://www.solveintelligence.com/blog/post/uspto-memo-reminders-for-ai-software-inventions)
- Ex parte Desjardins (Appeals Review Panel, 26 Sep 2025; designated precedential 4 Nov 2025): vacated a 101 rejection of claims to training machine-learning models because the claims improved how the model functions; the claims were still rejected under 103, and the decision cautions against categorical exclusion of AI under 101 — [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/ip-law/ai-patent-eligibility-has-shifted-with-one-machine-learning-case); [IP Update on Desjardins](https://www.ipupdate.com/tag/ex-parte-desjardins/); [Mondaq on 101 rejections in AI](https://www.mondaq.com/unitedstates/new-technology/1666994/uspto-raises-bar-for-101-rejections-in-ai-patents)
- India s.3(k) excludes "a computer programme per se", mathematical or business methods and algorithms; the Delhi High Court (Ferid Allani and later cases) reads the bar narrowly and applies a technical effect / technical contribution test, no novel hardware required, business methods barred absolutely, algorithms patentable where the inventive feature lies in the implementation; the court has also suggested s.3(k) needs re-examination — [Mondaq on Ferid Allani](https://www.mondaq.com/india/patent/897160/patentability-of-computer-programmes-with-technical-effect-contribution-in-light-of-feridallani-v-uoi); [Asia IP on s.3(k)](https://asiaiplaw.com/section/ip-analysts/not-all-computer-related-inventions-are-hit-by-section-3k-of-the-patents-act); [Intepat on AI/ML patents in India](https://www.intepat.com/blog/ai-machine-learning-patents-patentable-india); [Mondaq on Delhi HC](https://mondaq.com/india/patent/1353616/patent-act-needs-to-be-re-examined-says-delhi-high-court)
- Indian CRI Guidelines 2025 (29 July 2025; replace the 2017 version; a four-limb approach): an abstract mathematical AI/ML construct is not patentable merely by technical language; patentability arises on implementation in a tangible technical application; claims must state "how" not only "what"; two-step test (substance, then abstract vs concretely implemented); CRM and CPP claims reported as still non-patentable; examples of allowable and non-allowable claims are included — [Conventus/Mondaq](https://conventuslaw.com/report/india-a-detailed-analysis-of-indias-new-cri-guidelines-2025/); [AIPPI](https://www.aippi.org/news/decoding-cri-guidelines-2025/); [Bar & Bench](https://www.barandbench.com/law-firms/view-point/revised-cri-guidelines-2025)
- EPO Guidelines G-II 3.3.1: ML models and algorithms fall under the mathematical-methods guidance; a claim to a method using technical means or a device has technical character as a whole, but inventive-step features must still contribute technically; examples: ML in a heart-monitor identifying irregular heartbeats is technical; classifying text by content is linguistic, not technical; generating training sets and training a classifier can add to technical character when serving a technical purpose; labels such as "neural network" alone do not suffice; the 2025 edition generalised the algorithm definition and the 2026 edition (1 Apr 2026) added disclosure guidance — [EPO Guidelines G-II 3.3.1](https://www.epo.org/law-practice/legal-texts/html/guidelines/e/g_ii_3_3_1.htm); [Solve Intelligence on EPO 2025](https://www.solveintelligence.com/blog/post/epo-guidelines-2025); [Solve Intelligence on EPO 2026](https://www.solveintelligence.com/blog/post/epo-guidelines-2026-key-changes)
- Divided infringement (US): a method claim is directly infringed only where all steps are performed by or attributable to one party; Akamai v. Limelight (en banc, 2015) allows attribution through direction/control or joint enterprise, including conditioning participation or a benefit on performing steps and establishing the manner or timing; a typical FL arrangement splits steps between server operator and client device owners — [Hunton on Akamai](https://huntonak.com/insights/legal/akamai-techs-inc-v-limelight-networks-inc-federal-circuit-en-banc-decision-sets-new-standard-for-divided-infringement); [Sedona Conference article on client/server claims](https://d10.thesedonaconference.org/sites/default/files/publications/117-130%20Lemley_117-130%20Lemley.qxd__0.pdf)
- Example FL claim styles: Samsung US 12,236,370 claim 1 is directed to a method performed at a client device; Application US 2024/0177057 describes a method performed by a server; Samsung's summary also includes a client apparatus with a processor and non-transitory storage medium — [US12236370](https://patents.google.com/patent/US12236370); [US 2024/0177057](https://patents.justia.com/patent/20240177057)

### Inferences
- Draft at least three claim families with each having a single actor: (A) server-side method/system (receive updates, privacy-preserving aggregation, update/redistribute global model) with a matching non-transitory CRM; (B) client-side method/device (local training, clipping/noise/encryption, upload); (C) a training-method claim for the technical improvement; include dependent claims for each variant (differential-privacy budget, secure aggregation, client selection, compression) as fallbacks. For India, lead with system/method claims and use CRM claims only where tied to a technical effect, with an apparatus fallback.
- Express the technical improvement as an improvement in how the system works (reduced communication cost, faster convergence under non-IID data, resistance to poisoning, privacy-utility trade-off, memory/compute reduction on edge devices) and support it with data in the specification; this serves US Desjardins-style eligibility, India's technical-effect test and the EPO problem-solution approach.
- Because the paper will naturally emphasise accuracy and benchmark gains, the specification should additionally describe the technical mechanism and the problem solved to avoid "better informational output only" objections (Indian guidelines) and "linguistic or abstract" objections (EPO).

### Gaps
- Official text of the 2025 Indian CRI guidelines and the USPTO memo/Desjardins decision were not read; the "more than 50% likelihood" threshold attributed to the memo was a commentator's reading and unconfirmed.
- Post-2015 Federal Circuit developments on divided infringement were not checked; no FL-specific infringement decisions were found; non-US analyses (EPO/UPC, India, China) of split-actor claims were not researched.

## 8. Patent attorney/agent versus DIY: what costs, what can be done by the researcher, and confidentiality practice

### Takeaway
Attorney drafting for a software/ML specification is commonly estimated in the high four to low five figures (USD), with a separate prior-art search; registered practitioners are required to represent applicants before the USPTO, but an individual can file pro se, and free help exists for under-resourced inventors. In India an applicant can e-file directly with a digital signature or use a registered agent (Form 26). The researcher can do the invention disclosure, prior-art search, figure drafting and technical write-up, leaving claim drafting and filing strategy to the professional.

### Cited Findings
- Estimates (non-AIPLA): simple inventions USD 5,000-7,000; moderately complex (software, electronics) USD 7,000-12,000; highly complex USD 12,000-20,000+; a law firm puts a software application at USD 10,000-20,000+ plus filing fees; prior-art search USD 1,500-4,000+; a UI design patent USD 2,000-5,000 — [UpCounsel patent lawyer cost](https://upcounsel.com/patent-lawyer-cost); [PatentPC on software patent cost](https://patentpc.com/blog/how-much-does-it-cost-to-get-a-software-patent); [Arapacke on software patent cost](https://arapackelaw.com/patents/softwaremobile-apps/software-patent-cost/)
- The 2025 AIPLA Economic Survey reports typical charges for patent prosecution tasks (flat-fee vs hourly) but is members-only; the 2023 edition reported median US patent application charges up about 10% in 2022 vs 2020; the figure of "$10,000 for electrical/computer applications since 2006" is a blog's secondhand citation — [AIPLA 2025 Economic Survey](https://www.aipla.org/detail/journal-issue/2025-report-of-the-economic-survey); [AIPLA 2023 Economic Survey](https://www.aipla.org/detail/journal-issue/2023-report-of-the-economic-survey)
- Only registered patent practitioners may represent inventors before the USPTO; the Patent Pro Bono Program matches volunteer practitioners with financially under-resourced inventors (typical criteria: household income under three times the poverty guideline, knowledge of the patent system such as a filed provisional, and ability to describe the invention); an individual may still file without a lawyer — [USPTO Patent Pro Bono overview](https://www.uspto.gov/sites/default/files/documents/3-Patent-Pro-Bono-Program-Overview-April-12-2024-Pathways.pdf); [USPTO Patent Pro Bono Program](https://www.uspto.gov/patentprobono)
- India: e-filing software with a digital signature lets an applicant or an authorised patent agent file directly; Form 26 (or a power of attorney on INR 100 stamp paper) documents agent representation and is submitted at filing; Form 28 and its proofs support reduced-fee status — [Banana IP how to file](https://www.bananaip.com/intellepedia/?p=32299); [Banana IP filing requirements](https://www.bananaip.com/intellepedia/?p=1336); [Intepat on Form 28](https://www.intepat.com/blog/foreign-applicant-startup-small-entity-indian-patent)
- Confidentiality practice: see Q5 (NDAs, implicit confidentiality at the EPO, Helsinn in the US) — [EPO case law on secrecy](https://www.epo.org/en/legal/case-law/2022/clr_i_c_3_4_12.html); [UNT innovation FAQ](https://research.unt.edu/support-units/innovation-commercialization/faqs.html)
- A university or TTO route can reduce cost: many institutions handle disclosure, patent counsel selection and fees under their IP policy, and in India the IPR cells at IITs run similar processes — [Insight IIT Bombay explainer](https://insightiitb.org/yours-intellectually-the-ip-policy/); [IIT Kanpur IPR policy](https://www.iitk.ac.in/ipr/data/IPR-Policy.pdf)

### Inferences
- DIY-friendly tasks: invention disclosure form with a contribution log, state-of-the-art search (Q6), drafting figures and a first-pass description of embodiments and alternatives, filing an Indian provisional or US provisional on one's own (low fee, no claims required but substance matters), and administrative filings (Form 28, Form 9 docketing, RFE deadline tracking).
- Professional-friendly tasks: independent/dependent claim architecture, 112(f)/s.3(k)/Art. 52 risk management, inventorship determination, PCT strategy and national-phase selection, responses to office actions, FTO opinions.
- A hybrid approach (researcher writes a detailed technical disclosure and figures; attorney converts it to a specification with claims) typically reduces billable time, but this cost-saving claim is an inference rather than a sourced finding.

### Gaps
- No 2026-specific attorney fee survey figures were obtained (AIPLA medians are paywalled); Indian patent-agent fee ranges were not found; whether foreign-domiciled applicants need a US-registered practitioner for patents (as for trademarks) was not researched.
