## 1. Exactly which disclosures destroy novelty in the US, EPO, India, China and Japan, and what grace period (if any) applies?

### Takeaway
Only the US (12 months, inventor-originated, counted back from the effective filing date) and Japan (12 months, formalities required) have a broad grace period for the inventor's own paper, talk or preprint. China's 6 months covers only listed events (including "prescribed" conferences, not journals or preprints), India's 12 months covers only notified exhibitions and papers read before or published by a "learned society", and the EPO has essentially none (Art. 55: 6 months, third-party abuse or official international exhibitions only). Because a grace period in one country does not help in another, the only safe assumption for global rights is that any public disclosure before the first (priority) filing is novelty-destroying.

### Cited Findings

#### United States (AIA first-inventor-to-file; 35 USC 102)
- 102(b)(1) removes two kinds of otherwise-qualifying 102(a)(1) prior art, but only if made one year or less before the effective filing date: (A) disclosures by the inventor or a joint inventor, or by someone who obtained the subject matter from them; (B) later disclosures by others of the same subject matter after the inventor's own public disclosure. — [Blue Iron IP summary of 102(b)(1)](https://blueironip.com/?p=20649); MPEP overview at [USPTO MPEP 2153](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/s2153.html) (only an excerpt was visible to the search tool)
- If the one-year window ends on a weekend or federal holiday it rolls to the next business day (stated as MPEP 2153.01(a) content); MPEP 2153.01(a) applies only to applications examined under AIA first-inventor-to-file rules. — [Blue Iron IP, MPEP 2153.01(a)](https://blueironip.com/mpep/mpep-2153-01-a/); [CLFIP copy of MPEP 2153.01(a) R-07.2022](https://www.clfip.com/ip/blog/?p=14151)
- Establishing the exception: a statement in the specification can carry the same information as a 37 CFR 1.130(a) declaration; if the specification specifically references the earlier disclosure and explains it, the Office treats inventor origin as apparent absent contrary evidence; otherwise a 1.130(a) declaration is used. — [Blue Iron IP summary of MPEP 2153](https://blueironip.com/?p=20650); [USPTO MPEP 2153](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/s2153.html)
- Co-author trap: if the application names MORE inventors than the publication names authors, inventor origin is apparent; if the publication names MORE authors than the application names inventors, it is treated as 102(a)(1) prior art unless evidence shows a 102(b)(1) exception, so a 1.130(a) declaration is needed. The accepted showing is (1) an unequivocal statement that the named inventor(s) invented the disclosed subject matter and (2) a reasonable explanation of the additional authors (e.g., students who assisted but did not conceive); conclusory declarations fail, and a co-author's contrary letter defeated declarations in Ex parte Kroger. 1.130(c) is unavailable where the disclosure is more than one year before the effective filing date. — [Blue Iron IP on additional authors](https://blueironip.com/?p=22641); [CLFIP MPEP 2155.01 (R-07.2022)](https://www.clfip.com/ip/blog/?p=14165); [Mintz on inventors' own work](https://www.mintz.com/insights-center/viewpoints/2231/2023-12-06-basics-how-inventors-own-work-affects-patent)
- What counts as a 102(a)(1) disclosure: "printed publication" includes electronic/Internet publications if accessible to persons concerned with the art, dated from when publicly posted; a document with no provable posting date cannot be relied on, and examiners can ask the USPTO's STIC to find the earliest posting date. — [USPTO MPEP 2128](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/s2128.html) via [Blue Iron IP, MPEP 2128](https://blueironip.com/mpep/mpep-2128/)
- Public accessibility, not distribution, is the test: In re Klopfenstein, 380 F.3d 1345 (Fed. Cir. 2004) held a poster shown for about 2.5 days at a cereal-chemists meeting (plus under a day at a university event), with no copies handed out and no cataloguing, was a "printed publication"; the court said the key inquiry is whether the reference was "publicly accessible". — [Justia, In re Klopfenstein](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/380/1345/533650)
- Theses: In re Hall (Fed. Cir. 1986) treated an indexed, shelved doctoral thesis as a printed publication; In re Cronyn (Fed. Cir. 1989) held student theses indexed only by author name and title cards were NOT publicly accessible. — [CLFIP, MPEP 2128.01 public accessibility](https://www.clfip.com/ip/blog/mpep/2128-1/); [summary of Klopfenstein/Hall/Cronyn](https://www.clfip.com/ip/blog/?p=14049)
- On sale / public use: Helsinn v. Teva (2019) held a sale to a buyer bound to confidentiality can still trigger the AIA on-sale bar, rejecting the argument that "otherwise available to the public" excludes secret sales. Sources disagree on whether MPEP 2152.02(d) fully reflects this; courts follow the Supreme Court. — [Taft on Helsinn](https://www.taftlaw.com/news-events/law-bulletins/supreme-court-says-confidential-sale-may-still-trigger-on-sale-bar-post-aia-helsinn-v-teva/); [Ropes & Gray](https://www.ropesgray.com/en/insights/alerts/2019/01/supreme-court-affirms-that-secret-sales-are-still-prior-art-can-bar-patenting-in-counter-to-current)
- Experimental-use exception: the USPTO has said neither the AIA nor its history expressly addresses whether it survives under 102(a)(1) and will decide case by case; commercial exploitation more than one year before the effective filing date is not excused by experimental motive. — [Blue Iron IP, MPEP 2133.03(e)(5)](https://blueironip.com/mpep/mpep-2133-03-e-5/)
- Grace period is US-only: a public disclosure can destroy absolute-novelty rights in most foreign jurisdictions immediately. — [Blue Iron IP summary](https://blueironip.com/?p=20649)

#### EPO (EPC Art. 54, 55; Guidelines Part G)
- Absolute novelty: a document is state of the art only if made available to the public before the filing date; Internet disclosures are state of the art. — [EPO Guidelines G-IV 7.5 (2025)](https://www.epo.org/en/legal/guidelines-epc/2025/g_iv_7_5_3_1.html)
- Art. 55(1) EPC ignores a disclosure only if it occurred no earlier than 6 months before filing AND was due to (a) evident abuse in relation to the applicant or legal predecessor, or (b) display by the applicant at an official or officially recognised international exhibition; the exhibition route needs a statement at filing and a certificate filed within the time limit. — [EPC Art. 55 (2020 text)](https://www.epo.org/law-practice/legal-texts/html/epc/2020/e/ar55.html); [EPO case-law digest I.C.2.5](https://epo.org/en/legal/case-law/2019/clr_i_c_2_5.html)
- There is no general self-disclosure exception: the EPO's own training material describes Art. 55 as limited to those two routes, "all other pre-filing disclosures lead to loss of patentability". Abuse case law concerns third parties (T 173/83: recipient not authorised to pass information on; T 585/92: early publication by a government agency through error was not necessarily "abuse"). — [EPO case-law digest I.C.2.5](https://epo.org/en/legal/case-law/2019/clr_i_c_2_5.html); [Barlocci presentation (UB)](https://www.ub.edu/centredepatents/pdf/doc_dilluns_CP/barlocci_divulgacionesinocuas.pdf)
- G 3/98 / G 2/99: the 6 months are counted back from the ACTUAL European filing date, not from the priority date, so the grace window cannot be stacked on the priority year. — [G 3/98 decision (EPO)](https://www.epo.org/fr/node/575944); [G 3/98 text](https://legacy.epo.org/boards-of-appeal/decisions/pdf/g980003ex1.pdf)
- Priority interval: under G 3/93 a document published between the priority date and the EP filing date is citable under Art. 54(2) only to the extent the priority is NOT validly claimed; validity depends on "the same invention" (G 2/98). So a paper published after a provisional is safe at the EPO only for subject matter the provisional supports. — [G 3/93](https://link.epo.org/web/g930003.pdf); [G 2/98 text](https://legacy.epo.org/boards-of-appeal/decisions/pdf/g980002ex1.pdf); [EPO case-law digest II.D.3.1.1](https://epo.org/en/legal/case-law/2022/clr_ii_d_3_1_1.html)
- Restricting access or charging for access does not stop a web page being prior art; it suffices that it is in principle available without a bar of confidentiality. — [EPO Guidelines G-IV 7.5 (2025)](https://epo.org/en/legal/guidelines-epc/2025/g_iv_7_5_2.html)
- The EPO ran a stakeholder survey on a possible grace period (from December 2021); I found no adopted change to Art. 55. — [Mondaq on EPO grace-period consultation](https://mondaq.com/uk/patent/1147914/epo-launches-consultation-on-grace-periods); [Wiggin](https://www.wiggin.co.uk/?p=19442)
- The 2026 Guidelines (in force 1 April 2026) changed selection-invention novelty, marketed products as prior art (G 1/23: reproducibility no longer required) and claim interpretation (G 1/24); nothing found on Art. 55 or Art. 87. — [Mondaq: 2026 EPO Guidelines, novelty](https://www.mondaq.com/uk/patent/1761120/2026-epo-guidelines-whats-new-for-novelty-and-prior-art)

#### UK (relevant to "UK/EP priority")
- No general grace period. s.2(4) Patents Act 1977 disregards, for 6 months before filing, disclosures obtained unlawfully or in breach of confidence and (c) display at certain international exhibitions; the exhibition route needs a written statement and, within 4 months of filing, a certificate and authenticated statement from the exhibition authority. The 6 months run from the filing date of the application, and I found no UKIPO guidance on interaction with a priority claim. — [Patents Act 1977 s.2](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1977/37/section/2); [gov.uk guidance s.2](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/the-patent-act-1977/section-2-novelty); [Patents Rules 2007 Part 2](https://legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2007/3291/part/2/data.html)

#### India (Patents Act 1970, ss.29-34, Chapter VI "Anticipation")
- The Act does not define "anticipation"; ss.29-32 only list circumstances that do NOT constitute anticipation (Madras HC, Jayasingh v. Mishra Dhatu Nigam); s.34 says other circumstances do cause anticipation. — [FoxMandal on anticipation](https://foxmandal.in/anticipation-of-invention-patent-and-latent-threats/); [IP India s.32 page](https://ipindia.gov.in/acts/patent-act-1970/section-32)
- s.29: a prior publication is disregarded where the matter was obtained from the applicant/title-holder and published without consent, but only if the applicant files as soon as reasonably practicable after learning of it; burden is on the applicant; protection is lost if the invention was commercially worked in India before the priority date other than for reasonable trial. — [IP India s.29 page](https://ipindia.gov.in/acts/patent-act-1970/section-29); [FoxMandal](https://foxmandal.in/anticipation-of-invention-patent-and-latent-threats/)
- s.30: communication to the Government (or someone it authorises to investigate) is not anticipation. s.32: public working in India within one year before the priority date is not anticipation if by/with consent of the applicant, for reasonable trial only and reasonably necessary. — [FoxMandal](https://foxmandal.in/anticipation-of-invention-patent-and-latent-threats/); [IP India s.32 page](https://ipindia.gov.in/acts/patent-act-1970/section-32)
- s.31 ("Anticipation by public display, etc."): not anticipation if the invention was (a) displayed with the true and first inventor's consent at an exhibition NOTIFIED by the Central Government, (b) described in a publication resulting from that display, (c) used by others without consent during that exhibition, or (d) described in a paper read by the true and first inventor before a "learned society" or published with consent in the society's transactions, provided the application is made within 12 months of the opening of the exhibition or the reading/publication of the paper. "Learned society" and "transactions" are undefined; UK decisions (Ralph's Application; Ethyl Corporation's Patent) are used as guidance; the clause traces to s.51(2) UK Patents Act 1949. — [Intepat on s.31](https://www.intepat.com/blog/grace-period-under-section-31-of-the-patents-act-in-india); [Khurana & Khurana on s.31](https://khuranaandkhurana.com/?p=1979); [Asia IP analysis](https://asiaiplaw.com/sector/patents/when-does-public-display-become-prior-art-anticipation-under-the-patents-act-1970); [s.31 text (lawx.in)](https://lawx.in/acts/section/22/274/2640)
- The Patents (Amendment) Rules 2024 (in force 15 March 2024) added Rule 29A and Form 31 for claiming the s.31 grace period (earliest date and evidence of the disclosure, e.g., gazette notification of the exhibition or proof of consent/learned-society record). Fee reported as Rs 500 (natural person/startup/small entity/educational institution) and Rs 2,500 (others) by one source; another says Rs 2,500 for a large entity. — [Intepat](https://www.intepat.com/blog/grace-period-under-section-31-of-the-patents-act-in-india); [LiveLaw/S.S. Rana on 2024 rules](https://www.livelawbiz.com/law-firms/law-firm-articles-/patent-rules-ipr-ss-rana-co-253592); [Mondaq on 2024 rules](https://www.mondaq.com/india/patent/1442800/patent-amendment-rules-2024-notified)
- Practitioner caution: an un-notified trade show is outside s.31; a thesis uploaded online before filing can cost novelty; file a provisional before disclosing and treat s.31 as a fallback. — [Intepat on s.31](https://www.intepat.com/blog/grace-period-under-section-31-of-the-patents-act-in-india); [Intepat, when to file](https://www.intepat.com/blog/what-is-the-right-stage-for-filing-a-patent)
- The India UK-comparison: s.31 is described as largely identical to the UK provision but with 12 months instead of 6. — [Khurana & Khurana](https://khuranaandkhurana.com/?p=1979)

#### China (Patent Law Art. 24, as amended; effective 1 June 2021)
- Art. 24: novelty is not lost for disclosures within 6 months before the filing date in four situations: (1) first disclosure in an emergency state or for public interest (new in 2021), (2) first display at an international exhibition sponsored or recognised by the Chinese government, (3) first publication at a prescribed academic or technical conference, (4) disclosure by others without the applicant's consent. — [Lee & Li](https://www.leeandli.com/EN/NewslettersDetail/7204.htm); [Fieldfisher](https://www.fieldfisher.com/en/locations/china/insights/understanding-the-fourth-amendment-of-chinese-patent-law)
- Revised Implementing Regulations (State Council 21 Dec 2023, in force 20 Jan 2024) widened the conference limb from meetings organised by State Council departments or national academic bodies to include conferences organised by international organisations recognised by those departments; no list of recognised conferences was published and geographic limits are unclear. — [AIPPI](https://www.aippi.org/news/cnipa-published-revised-implementing-regulations-of-patent-law-and-patent-examination-guidelines/); [IPWatchdog](https://ipwatchdog.com/2024/06/19/patents-china-developments-2024-whats-come/); [Lee & Li](https://www.leeandli.com/EN/NewslettersDetail/7204.htm)
- Journals, theses and Internet posts are not listed; in a CNIPA invalidation decision an online journal article published within 6 months of filing was not excused because the patentee had not filed the "without consent" declaration within two months of learning of it. — [CNIPA decision (Chinese)](https://www.cnipa.gov.cn/art/2022/6/15/art_2648_176031.html)
- "Without consent" reaches breaches of explicit or implicit confidentiality, coercion, fraud or espionage; the declaration is due within two months of filing or of learning of the leak. — [Chinalawinsight](https://www.chinalawinsight.com/?p=34173); [CNIPA (Chinese)](https://www.cnipa.gov.cn/art/2021/12/3/art_2650_171861.html)
- The window is counted from the Chinese application date even when priority is claimed (not from the priority date). — [Lee & Li newsletter as relayed by search summary (URL could be 5265, 6693 or 7204)](https://www.leeandli.com/EN/Newsletters/5265.htm)
- The general restoration of missed time limits does not extend to the Art. 24 period (per Art. 6 of the Implementing Regulations as printed in People's Daily). — [People's Daily text](https://paper.people.com.cn/rmrb/html/2024-01/05/nw.D110000renmrb_20240105_1-12.htm)
- A CIPA exam sample answer states China has no grace period for the applicant's own prior disclosure, which overlooks Art. 24's limited exceptions (conflicting framing). — [CIPA sample script](https://www.cipa.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/FC3-sample-script-1-for_PUB.pdf)

#### Japan (Patent Act Art. 30)
- Since the 2018 amendment the grace period is 12 months (was 6) for applications filed on or after 9 June 2018 covering disclosures on or after 9 December 2017; Art. 30(2) covers the applicant's own acts and Art. 30(1) disclosures against the applicant's will; it does not neutralise third-party prior art. — [Novagraaf](https://www.novagraaf.com/jp/node/449); [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/ip-law/insight-japan-expands-grace-period-protection-for-inventors); [Keisen Associates](https://keisenassociates.com/?p=1349)
- Formalities for the applicant's own disclosure: a document claiming the exception must be submitted with the application, and a proving document within 30 days of the filing date; JPO guidance dated 2020 (updated Dec 2020) and a note that April 2023 Patent Act changes may affect the late-submission relief periods. — [JPO operational guidelines page](https://www.jpo.go.jp/e/system/laws/rule/other/patent/hatumei_reigai.html); [JPO operational guidelines PDF](https://www.jpo.go.jp/e/system/laws/rule/other/patent/document/hatumei_reigai/operational-guidelines.pdf)
- The window is measured from the Japanese filing date (for Paris-priority cases the date of the Japanese application), while novelty is judged at the priority date. — [Search-summary of JPO operational guidelines](https://www.jpo.go.jp/e/system/laws/rule/other/patent/document/hatumei_reigai/operational-guidelines.pdf); [Keisen Associates Q&A](https://keisenassociates.com/?p=4008)
- The exception is territorial: it works only in Japan. — [JPO pages (search summary)](https://www.jpo.go.jp/e/system/laws/rule/other/patent/internet/index.html)

### Inferences
- PCT route and grace periods: I found no source stating a PCT-level grace period; each designated office applies its own national rule (as above), so a PCT filing preserves only the priority/filing date. This is inferred from the national rules plus the national-phase structure in the [WIPO time-limit table](https://www.wipo.int/en/web/pct-system/texts/time_limits).
- Treat the first public disclosure as the one date that matters: before the first filing (priority date), no disclosure anywhere; after it, disclosures are safe only for subject matter the priority document supports.
- Stacking trap: in the EPO (G 3/98), China and Japan the grace window is counted from the actual filing date. If you publish first and rely on the US grace year, filing a provisional at month 11 and a PCT at month 23 puts the PCT filing date more than 12 months after the paper, losing Japan's 12 months and China's/EPO's rights entirely, while the US still works because the provisional date is the effective filing date (provided the provisional supports the claims).
- India: for practical purposes assume no grace period for a journal article, preprint or most conference talks; s.31(d) probably helps only for a paper read before, or published in the transactions of, a genuine learned society, and the narrow reading is the safe one.
- China: a talk at a "prescribed" conference is the main route; whether a given conference qualifies is uncertain, so do not plan around it.

## 2. Recommended safe sequence (provisional first, then preprint, then paper) and minimum provisional content

### Takeaway
File the first application (US provisional, Indian provisional, or UK/EP filing) BEFORE any preprint, submission to a publicly visible venue, code release, talk or thesis deposit; then post the preprint/submit the paper; then file the PCT/convention applications within 12 months of the priority date. A provisional only protects what it describes: it must contain a full 35 USC 112(a)-quality written description and enabling disclosure of everything you will later claim, including content that will appear in the paper, supplementary material and code.

### Cited Findings
- US provisional requirements: a specification meeting 35 USC 112(a) (written description and enablement), drawings where necessary, a cover sheet (ADS or cover letter identifying it as provisional), and the fee; claims, oath/declaration and IDS are NOT required; filing date is when the specification is received (for filings on/after 18 Dec 2013); not examined, never published unless a later filing claims it; deemed abandoned 12 months after filing, not revivable; no provisional for design. — [USPTO provisional page](https://www.uspto.gov/ProvisionalPatent); [Blue Iron IP, MPEP 601.01(b)](https://blueironip.com/mpep/mpep-601-01-b/)
- Benefit only if supported: a nonprovisional gets the provisional's date only if the provisional supports the claims under 112(a) (New Railhead v. Vermeer; Dynamic Drinkware), and the Federal Circuit in Dental Monitoring v. Align (10 Aug 2026, No. 2025-1752) vacated a PTAB decision, holding the AIA did not change the requirement that a provisional used as prior art give written-description support for at least one claim of the reference. — [Morgan Lewis, Aug 2026](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2026/08/federal-circuit-reaffirms-dynamic-drinkware-for-aia-prior-art); [Steptoe on Dental Monitoring](https://www.steptoe.com/en/news-publications/step-into-ip-blog/federal-circuit-adds-hurdle-to-reliance-upon-provisional-filing-dates-for-prior-art-under-the-aia-in-dental-monitoring.html)
- US restoration: a petition under 37 CFR 1.78(b) within 14 months of the provisional (fee under 1.17(m), unintentional-delay statement) can restore benefit; it fixes the date but not a support gap. — [Blue Iron IP on restoration](https://blueironip.com/?p=9838); [Morgan Lewis, Aug 2026](https://www.morganlewis.com/pubs/2026/08/federal-circuit-reaffirms-dynamic-drinkware-for-aia-prior-art)
- Application size fee applies to provisionals above 100 sheets (add-on $90 per 50 sheets micro, $180 small), per 2026 provisional-cost guides (exact page within the result set uncertain). — [IP Boutique Law 2026 guide](https://ipboutiquelaw.com/cost-provisional-patent-application-2026-guide-dc-usa/); [Tmarkmetric](https://tmarkmetric.com/insights/provisional-patent-cost)
- Indian provisional: Section 9 gives 12 months from filing to file the complete specification or the application is deemed abandoned; practitioners report no extension or revival and that the 6-month conversion extension is unavailable where a provisional was filed; provisionals are not published or examined unless followed by a complete application. — [IP India s.9 page](https://ipindia.gov.in/writereaddata/Portal/ev/sections/ps9.html); [Intepat](https://www.intepat.com/blog/what-is-the-right-stage-for-filing-a-patent); [R K Dewan](https://www.rkdewan.com/articles/article-section-9-of-the-indian-patents-acts-acts-as-a-bar-for-post-dating-of-a-provisional-application)
- Indian-resident inventors: s.39 requires either filing in India first and waiting 6 weeks (provided no s.35 secrecy direction) or obtaining written permission on Form 25 (Rule 71(1)), normally decided within 21 days; there is no retroactive permission in India. Breach historically carried criminal liability under s.118 (up to 2 years) and risked deemed abandonment under s.40 and revocation under s.64. A 2026 amendment (Jan Vishwas Act, reported in force 1 June 2026) disapplies s.118 where the Central Government concludes the invention was not relevant to defence or atomic energy at the time; s.39/s.40 consequences are unchanged; the Madras HC in Selfdot Technologies (2023) treated a technical breach as not triggering s.40. — [Mondaq on s.39](https://www.mondaq.com/india/patent/1066932/section-39-of-the-patents-act-1970); [Intepat on s.118 easing](https://www.intepat.com/blog/foreign-filing-penalty-eased-india-section-118-patents-act); [Conventus Law on Jan Vishwas 2026](https://conventuslaw.com/report/jan-vishwas-amendment-of-provisions-act-2026-further-rationalises-patent-and-copyright-offences-in-india/)
- US inventions: an application filed in the US is treated as including a petition for a foreign filing licence (35 USC 184, 37 CFR 5.12(a)), granted by filing receipt, normally after 6 months absent a secrecy order; filing abroad without a licence risks invalidity/unenforceability under s.185 and requires a retroactive-licence petition under 37 CFR 5.25 (facts-based showing, fee, can take months). — [Finnegan on foreign filing licences](https://www.finnegan.com/en/insights/blogs/prosecution-first/need-a-foreign-filing-license.html); [Mondaq, retroactive licence petitions](https://mondaq.co.uk/unitedstates/patent/1398290/i-forgot-retroactive-foreign-filing-license-petitions-for-us-inventors)
- China: inventions "developed in China" (regardless of nationality) need a confidentiality examination/foreign filing licence before filing abroad; foreign filing in breach can bar the Chinese patent; reported time limits conflict (4 and 6 months in one source; notice in 2 months or 4 in complex cases and decision in 4 months in another). — [Sterne Kessler](https://www.sternekessler.com/news-insights/insights/foreign-filing-requirements-china/); [Mondaq on 2023 Implementing Regulations](https://www.mondaq.com/china/patent/1436412/third-revision-of-the-implementing-regulations-of-the-patent-law-of-the-peoples-republic-of-china-filing-procedures-incorporation-by-reference-and-priority-system); [NatLawReview](https://natlawreview.com/node/57248/printable/print)
- PCT priority restoration: an international application filed within 2 months after the 12-month priority period can have priority restored under Rule 26bis.3 on "due care" (effective in all designated states) or "unintentional" (effective only where national law has a comparable test); the receiving office chooses its test. — [WIPO PCT RO Guidelines 166A-166T](https://www.wipo.int/en/web/pct-system/texts/ro/ro166a_166t)
- Priority-interval publications: see Q1 (G 3/93, G 2/98). A document published in the priority interval is safe at the EPO only for subject matter validly in the priority document.
- EPO "same invention" test (G 2/98) governs whether the provisional supports the later claims for priority. — [G 2/98 text](https://legacy.epo.org/boards-of-appeal/decisions/pdf/g980002ex1.pdf)
- Universities/funders support "patent first, then publish": Horizon Europe lets beneficiaries postpone dissemination to protect results first and says open access is not an obligation to publish; Indian institutes (IIT Delhi FITT) state that publication before patenting is not advisable and offer an internal provisional-filing form. — [EU IPR Helpdesk](https://intellectual-property-helpdesk.ec.europa.eu/news-events/news/open-science-vs-ipr-horizon-europe-which-one-wins-2021-09-17_en); [IIT Delhi FITT form](https://www.fitt-iitd.in/web/document/licensing/Disclosure%20form%20for%20Complete%20Patent%20application.doc)

### Inferences
- Recommended timeline (my synthesis from the cited deadlines; P = first filing date):
  - P-2 to P-6 weeks: invention disclosure to the university TTO/funder as required (Q5); decide where inventors reside (India s.39, US s.184, China Art. 19) and which office to file first in.
  - P: file the provisional (or Indian/UK/EP first filing) containing everything described in the next bullet.
  - P to P+12 months: post the preprint (arXiv), submit to journals/conferences, release code/data; new matter discovered later goes into a NEW provisional or a combined filing BEFORE it is published.
  - P+12 months: deadline for the PCT / Paris-convention applications / US nonprovisional / Indian complete specification (Bayh-Dole-funded work: 10 months, see Q5). P+14 months: last-resort restoration windows (US 1.78(b); PCT Rule 26bis.3 covers filing up to 2 months late).
  - P+18 months: PCT international publication and Indian s.11A publication (Q4). P+30/31 months: national phase.
- Minimum content that supports later claims (my synthesis of 112(a), New Railhead/Dynamic Drinkware and G 2/98): the full technical problem and solution; every embodiment to be claimed, expressed at generic and specific levels (e.g., all algorithm variants, parameter ranges, architectures, training/aggregation procedures, hardware options); step-by-step methods with pseudo-code and data flow; figures and flowcharts; working examples and experimental data sufficient to show the method works; alternatives and fallbacks; definitions; and, for each claim you expect, an explicit, literal passage. A provisional need not have claims, but drafting claim-like "embodiments" paragraphs is the cheapest way to prove support. Attach the actual paper draft, supplementary material and README/code description as appendices so nothing in them is unsupported.
- Anything in the paper that was not in the provisional gets only the later filing date and, outside the US, is not covered by any grace period: treat the provisional as a snapshot and refile before publishing material improvements.
- For Indian-resident inventors, filing the first application in India (provisional, then waiting 6 weeks before any foreign filing) satisfies s.39 without Form 25 and creates a Paris priority; a US provisional first would need Form 25. Alternatively get Form 25 first (21 days).
- Preprint timing: posting on arXiv after the provisional is safe for novelty provided the provisional supports what is posted; avoid arXiv posts that add undisclosed improvements.
- Common mistakes that destroy rights (synthesised from cited sources): publishing/presenting/posting before the first filing; relying on the US grace year while planning to file abroad; thin or non-enabling provisionals; missing the 12-month deadline (no extension for Indian or US provisionals; only 14-month/2-month restoration); filing abroad without Indian/US/Chinese foreign filing permission; co-author/inventorship mismatch (US 1.130 declaration problems); confidential sales/licensing/supply or MTAs before filing (Helsinn); treating peer review as confidentiality (Q3); public-repository or public-review (e.g., OpenReview) visibility before filing; uploading theses; failing to docket Bayh-Dole disclosure/election deadlines (Q5).

## 3. How peer-review submissions, conference proceedings, arXiv versions, open-source code and dataset releases count as disclosures

### Takeaway
Anything accessible to the interested public without a binding confidentiality duty is prior art as of the date it became accessible: published journal articles (online-first date), proceedings, arXiv v1, public repositories, and even briefly displayed posters. Peer review is an ethical, not a legal, confidentiality regime, so a manuscript under review is probably but not certainly non-public; the EPO requires proof of explicit or implicit confidentiality, and many ML venues expose submissions publicly. Open-source code and datasets are disclosures of whatever a skilled person can derive from them, and I found no authority specific to datasets or model weights.

### Cited Findings
- Journal/online articles and preprints: electronic publications are "printed publications" if accessible to skilled persons and are dated from public posting; dateless documents cannot be relied on; applicants can contest dates by declaration. — [USPTO MPEP 2128](https://www.uspto.gov/web/offices/pac/mpep/s2128.html) via [Blue Iron IP summary of MPEP 2128](https://blueironip.com/mpep/mpep-2128/); [Blue Iron IP on publication dates](https://blueironip.com/?p=21911)
- EPO Guidelines treat eprint archives (named examples: arXiv, the Cryptology ePrint Archive, CiteSeerX) as "print equivalent" publications; explicit dates on Internet disclosures are generally accepted at face value, the burden being on the applicant to show otherwise; balance-of-probabilities standard; Wayback Machine, revision histories (wikis and version-control systems) and "last modified" dates are acceptable dating evidence; journal pre-publication versions may differ from the final text. — [EPO Guidelines G-IV 7.5.3.2 (2025)](https://epo.org/en/legal/guidelines-epc/2025/g_iv_7_5_3_2.html); [EPO Guidelines G-IV 7.5.2 (2024)](https://epo.org/en/legal/guidelines-epc/2024/g_iv_7_5_2.html); [EPO Guidelines G-IV 7.5.4 (2024)](https://epo.org/en/legal/guidelines-epc/2024/g_iv_7_5_4.html)
- Time zone: in a UK Court of Appeal decision, the date of an online publication is judged by the time zone of the patent office of filing, so a posting made close to the filing date needs care. — [Kluwer Patent Blog](https://patentblog.kluweriplaw.com/2017/04/20/court-of-appeal-confirms-that-prior-art-publication-date-is-to-be-determined-by-reference-to-the-timezone-of-the-patent-office-of-filing/)
- Posters/talks (US): Klopfenstein (poster, about 2.5 days, no copies) was a printed publication; a commentator notes it is unclear how brief a display must be to count as transient. — [Justia, In re Klopfenstein](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/appellate-courts/F3/380/1345/533650); [Patently-O](https://patentlyo.com/patent/2004/page/11)
- Theses: Hall (indexed, shelved thesis = prior art) vs Cronyn (name-only index = not prior art); at the EPO, T 151/99 treated a master's thesis as "highly plausibly" not confidential, becoming a "virtual certainty" once referred to in published literature. — [CLFIP MPEP 2128.01](https://www.clfip.com/ip/blog/mpep/2128-1/); [EPO case-law digest on confidentiality](https://new.epo.org/en/legal/case-law/2022/clr_i_c_3_4_11.html)
- Peer review is not a legal confidentiality guarantee: COPE-based journal guidance requires reviewers to treat manuscripts as confidential during and after publication, and publishers (Wiley) extend this to reviews and related communications; IntechOpen treats pre-publication data as confidential "unless posted on a preprint server"; ISSCC warns that a competent review means a limited number of reviewers, some of whom may be competitors, and recommends seeking patent protection before submitting. — [Wiley reviewer confidentiality policy](https://authorservices.wiley.com/Reviewers/journal-reviewers/tools-and-resources/review-confidentiality-policy.html); [ISSCC confidentiality statement](https://isscc.org/confidentiality-statement); [IntechOpen reviewer guidelines](https://www.intechopen.com/journals/1/manuscript-review-and-publication/guidelines-for-reviewers)
- EPO confidentiality case law (as reported by search summary of EPO digests): confidentiality must be proven with rigour; "private" labels do not suffice (T 2239/15 – explicit obligations binding all with lawful access); implicit confidentiality can be inferred in commercial relationships on evidence (T 201/13); no evidence of an implied duty meant a machine was public prior art (R 8/23). I found no decision on a journal manuscript under peer review. — [EPO case-law digest I.C.3.4](https://new.epo.org/en/legal/case-law/2022/clr_i_c_3_4_11.html); [CLR I C 3.4.9](https://xepc.eu/node/clr_i_c_3_4_9); see also [Hoffmann Eitle on a 2025 Board decision confirming public prior use despite a confidential analysis](https://www.hoffmanneitle.com/en/news/2025-02-25-confirmation-EPO-Board-public-prior-use)
- GitHub/open-source: a public repository can be prior art, including a repository that "discloses your idea"; the 2023 PTAB decision in IPR2023-00895 (AO Kaspersky v. Open Text) declined institution because an undated screenshot showing a commit date and a "Public" label did not prove public accessibility on a given date (visibility can be changed; commit dates record changes, not access; Wayback capture dates can lag). The lesson cuts both ways: your own repo's release date is provable from logs and archives, and EPO Guidelines accept version-control timestamps as dating evidence. — [PTAB Litigation Blog on Kaspersky v. Open Text](https://www.ptablitigationblog.com/undated-screenshot-insufficient-to-prove-public-accessibility-of-github-repository/); [Arapacke Law on open-source patenting](https://arapackelaw.com/patents/open-source-patent/); [EPO Guidelines G-IV 7.5.4](https://epo.org/en/legal/guidelines-epc/2024/g_iv_7_5_4.html)
- The US 102(b)(1)(B) shield against intervening third-party disclosures requires that the third party disclosed the same subject matter the inventor had already publicly disclosed, so a narrower early talk or abstract gives limited protection against a later rival publication. — [Blue Iron IP summary](https://blueironip.com/?p=20649)

### Inferences
- Disclosure date = the earliest date a member of the public could access it, not submission, acceptance or "final version" date. For arXiv this is the v1 announcement/posting date; for journals the online-first date; for GitHub the date the repository (or release) became public, not the first commit date; for conference abstracts, the date the abstract book or website went live (often earlier than the talk).
- Content = what a skilled reader can derive from the whole document including supplements, appendices, figures, code and README; any claim you intend to make must be absent from (or supported by an earlier priority filing relative to) these items.
- Peer review: safe to assume a journal manuscript under review is NOT public (the Klopfenstein accessibility test is not met by a handful of reviewers under ethical duties), but do not rely on it for the EPO, India or China; risks are venues with open/public reviewing, preprint-on-submission policies, reviewers who are competitors, and leaks. A prior provisional removes the issue.
- Datasets and model weights: no authority located; apply the same public-accessibility test. A dataset or checkpoint release with documentation of architecture, training recipe or federated-aggregation procedure discloses that procedure to the extent derivable. Release licence terms (open licence) do not create confidentiality.
- ML-specific trap (inference): release of code, model cards or benchmarks to Hugging Face/GitHub/Kaggle/Zenodo before filing counts as a disclosure at the EPO, in India and in China regardless of academic purpose.

### Gaps
- No primary source found on: arXiv-specific EPO or USPTO decisions; datasets/model-weight releases as prior art; OpenReview public submissions as disclosures; journal manuscripts under review at the EPO or USPTO; Indian or Chinese treatment of preprints or code repositories.
- EPO Guidelines text cited is the 2024/2025 edition; I did not confirm that the 1 April 2026 edition left G-IV 7.5 unchanged.
- The specific Board decisions (T 151/99, T 2239/15, T 201/13, R 8/23) are as reported in search summaries of EPO case-law digests; I did not open the decisions.
- The Hall/Klopfenstein/MIT v. AB Fortia conference-presentation case details (audience of up to 500, 6 copies) appear in one search summary attributed inconsistently; not repeated as a finding.
- Unverified background knowledge: the Klopfenstein factors list (display duration, audience expertise, expectation of copying, ease of copying); MIT v. AB Fortia; In re Wyer.

## 4. PCT deadlines, high-level costs, and how 2026 fee schedules (USPTO micro/small/large entity; Indian Patent Office startup/individual/educational-institution) change the strategy

### Takeaway
Paris/PCT filing deadline is 12 months from the first filing; international publication follows at 18 months; national phase is 30 months for the US, China (32 with surcharge), Japan, and 31 months for the EPO and India (India's 31 months is strict). At USPTO rates a provisional costs $325/$130/$65 (large/small/micro) and a PCT filed through the USPTO costs roughly $2,200-$4,400 before national phase depending on entity status and searching authority; India's reduced fees for startups, natural persons and educational institutions (about 80% off) make an Indian-first provisional the cheapest compliant starting point for Indian-based inventors.

### Cited Findings

#### Deadlines
- WIPO's time-limit table: China 30 months (late entry possible to 32 with surcharge), Japan 30, United States 30 (no extension; abandoned if basic national fee not paid), India 31, European Patent Organisation 31 (both Chapters I and II); Luxembourg and Tanzania shorter (20 and 21 months under Chapter I). — [WIPO time-limit table](https://www.wipo.int/en/web/pct-system/texts/time_limits); [WIPO PCT Applicant's Guide, US chapter (valid from 20 Aug 2026)](https://pctlegal.wipo.int/eGuide/view-doc.xhtml?doc-code=US&doc-lang=en); [China chapter (valid from 1 Sept 2026)](https://pctlegal.wipo.int/eGuide/view-doc.xhtml?doc-code=CN&doc-lang=en)
- EPO: 31 months from priority; steps include translation if needed, filing and designation/examination fees; failure means the Euro-PCT is deemed withdrawn; EPO does not begin processing before 31 months unless early processing is requested. — [EPO: entry into the European phase](https://www.epo.org/en/applying/international/entry-european-phase)
- India: Rule 20(4) requires entry within 31 months from priority; Madras HC (Nokia) allowed some discretion up to 32 months, Delhi HC (Diebold, 2022) held 31 months mandatory; PCT Rule 49.6 restoration does not apply in India. Request for examination for applications filed on/after 15 March 2024 is due 31 months from earliest priority (was 48). — [Law.asia on Indian national phase deadline](https://law.asia/indian-patent-national-phase-deadline/); [Mondaq, national phase India](https://mondaq.com/india/patent/1491988/filing-a-national-phase-patent-application-in-india-mind-the-deadline); [Mondaq on 2024 Rules](https://mondaq.co.uk/india/patent/1442048/patents-amendment-rules-2024-and-patent-second-amendment-rules-2024-come-into-force)

### Inferences
- Cost arithmetic (my sums from the cited fees; excludes attorney fees, extra pages, national-phase costs): PCT through the USPTO with USPTO as ISA: large entity $285 + $1,667 + $2,400 = $4,352; small entity $114 + $1,667 + $960 = $2,741; micro entity $57 + $1,667 + $480 = $2,204. With EPO as ISA and a large entity: $285 + $1,667 + $2,237 = $4,189. Add roughly $2,000/$800/$400 for a US nonprovisional (filing, search, exam) if filed in parallel.
- Strategy effects of the 2026 USPTO schedule: the provisional is the cheapest compliant first step ($65-$325), so cost should never justify publishing first; a micro-entity declaration is only safe if every inventor and assignee meets the income and application-count tests (and the university/employer licensing tests), otherwise pay small-entity rates; PCT fees have no small-entity tier on the WIPO component, so the discount is only on transmittal and USPTO-ISA search fees; larger specifications, IDS size fees and excess claims are not discounted for small/micro entities and can erode savings.
- Strategy effects of the Indian schedule: an Indian provisional costs about the natural-person/startup/educational-institution rate and secures priority and s.39 compliance; Form 9 early publication is cheap (Rs 2,500 at the lowest tier) and speeds examination, but it makes the application public early, so use it only after the paper/preprint plan is settled; the examination-request clock is now 31 months for new filings, aligning with the national-phase date.
- The 18-month publication is the first moment a provisional-based family becomes public absent a preprint; plan the paper before then, but never before the first filing.

## 5. What do universities and funders (e.g., Bayh-Dole in the US) require before publication?

### Takeaway
Under Bayh-Dole the institution must disclose each federally funded "subject invention" to the agency within 2 months of the inventor's written disclosure to its patent staff, elect title within 2 years of disclosure, and file within 1 year of election and in any case before a statutory bar (publication) takes effect; since 2018 a provisional counts as the initial application but must be converted within 10 months. NIH now requires accepted manuscripts in PubMed Central with no embargo, and Horizon Europe lets grantees delay dissemination to protect results. India has no Bayh-Dole equivalent; rules come from funder guidelines (DBT 2023) and institute IP policies that generally require disclosure to the IP cell before any publication.

### Cited Findings
- Bayh-Dole standard patent rights clause (37 CFR 401.14): the contractor must disclose each subject invention to the agency within 2 months after the inventor discloses it in writing to the contractor's patent personnel; elect title in writing within 2 years of the agency disclosure (the agency can shorten this, but not to less than 60 days before the statutory bar expires); file the initial application within 1 year after election, or earlier if a publication/sale/public-use bar would otherwise expire. If deadlines are missed, title can pass to the government; the government always keeps a royalty-free, non-transferable licence for governmental purposes. — [37 CFR 401.14 (Cornell LII)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/37/401.14); [eCFR 401.14](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-37/chapter-IV/part-401/section-401.14); [Fenwick](https://www.fenwick.com/insights/publications/avoiding-loss-of-title-to-inventions-made-under-federal-funding-as-new-bayh-dole-requirements-affect-patent-rights); [IPWatchdog on Bayh-Dole and the AIA](https://www.ipwatchdog.com/2011/07/01/bayh-dole-compliance-obligations-meet-the-america-invents-act/)
- 2018 amendments: "initial patent application" now includes provisional and PCT applications; a provisional must be converted to a nonprovisional or PCT within 10 months (not 12); foreign filings must be made within 10 months of the first filing (or 6 months after secrecy-order permission); extension requests are automatically granted for one year unless the agency objects within 60 days; sources conflict on whether failure to convert a provisional is a ground for government title (Quarles: no; HRFM: possible), and on the paragraph number of the extension rule ((c)(4) per NIH, (c)(5) per law firms). — [NIH Notice NOT-OD-18-233](https://grants.nih.gov/grants/guide/notice-files/NOT-OD-18-233.html); [Quarles](https://quarles.com/publications/practical-advice-regarding-changes-to-bayh-dole-act-obligations); [HRFM](https://www.hrfmlaw.com/patent-rights-issues-regarding-the-2018-changes-to-the-bayh-dole-act/); [Barnes & Thornburg](https://btlaw.com/insights/alerts/2019/new-rules-issued-for-reporting-disclosures-and-filing-patent-applications-funded-by-a-federal-agency)
- NIH Public Access Policy (2024 policy, effective 1 July 2025; NOT-OD-25-047): the author accepted manuscript must be deposited in PubMed Central upon acceptance and made publicly available on the official publication date with no embargo; covers manuscripts accepted on/after 1 July 2025 from NIH-funded work; edge cases depend on award timing. I found no NIH text linking this policy to patents. — [Duke Medical Center Library](https://mclibrary.duke.edu/news/new-nih-public-access-policy-effective-july-1-2025); [Columbia Research](https://research.columbia.edu/node/4125)
- Horizon Europe: dissemination is subject to restrictions for IP protection and can be postponed; beneficiaries must give other beneficiaries default 15 days' notice before disseminating (they can object if their legitimate interests would be significantly harmed); open access is not an obligation to publish; beneficiaries must retain rights to comply with open access; Horizon 2020 wording was stricter. — [EU IPR Helpdesk](https://intellectual-property-helpdesk.ec.europa.eu/news-events/news/open-science-vs-ipr-horizon-europe-which-one-wins-2021-09-17_en); [FFG Austria](https://www.ffg.at/en/europe/heu/legal-financial/project-results)
- India: no Bayh-Dole-style statute (the Protection and Utilisation of Public Funded IP Bill, 2008 was not enacted); DBT IP Guidelines (6 Sept 2023) say publication and IP protection are both dissemination routes and the host institution decides; they are guidelines without force of law. — [DBT IP Guidelines 2023 (PDF)](https://dbtindia.gov.in/sites/default/files/DBT%20IP%20Guidelines%202023%20final.pdf); [SpicyIP on DBT guidelines](https://spicyip.com/2023/09/bayh-dole-esque-guidelines-notified-by-dept-of-biotechnology.html); [PRS India bill text](https://PRSINDIA.org/files/bills_acts/bills_parliament/2008/1229425658_The_Protection_and_Utilisation_of_Public_Funded_Intellectual_Property_Bill__2008.pdf)
- Indian institute examples: IIT Mandi runs invention disclosure to its IP & TT cell, a patentability search and a committee filing decision, and may fund up to 90% of Indian and 50% of international filing costs; IIT Delhi (FITT) advises against publication before patenting and offers a provisional-filing form; IIIT Dharwad requires competent-authority approval for provisional, complete, early-publication and PCT/foreign filings; IIT Roorkee requires prompt invention-disclosure reporting; Thiagarajar College requires disclosure before publication, conference presentation or thesis submission. — [IIT Mandi procedure](https://iitmandi.ac.in/sric/iptt/forms/procedure.pdf); [IIT Delhi FITT form](https://www.fitt-iitd.in/web/document/licensing/Disclosure%20form%20for%20Complete%20Patent%20application.doc); [IIIT Dharwad IPR policy](https://www.iiitdmj.ac.in/downloads/IPR%20Policy%20Final%20V1%2016_6_2020.pdf); [IIT Roorkee IP management](https://heip-link.old.ogpi.ua.es/sites/default/files/IP%20Management%20IIT%20Roorkee-1.pdf); [TCARTS IP policy](https://tcarts.in/research/01_IP%20Policy.pdf)
- Journals' own policy interplay: IntechOpen and others treat preprint-posted data as non-confidential for review purposes. — [IntechOpen](https://www.intechopen.com/journals/1/manuscript-review-and-publication/guidelines-for-reviewers)

### Inferences
- A US federally funded lab should send the invention disclosure to the TTO before (not after) the paper is drafted; the 2-month agency disclosure clock starts when the inventor tells the TTO in writing, and a provisional filed on day 0 gives 10 months, not 12, to convert for Bayh-Dole inventions.
- A publication or preprint before the agency disclosure and election leaves very little room under the "before the statutory bar" language; the safe path is TTO disclosure -> provisional -> publication.
- NIH PMC deposit makes the accepted manuscript public on the publication date, so it adds no new pre-publication risk but removes any embargo cushion; file before journal publication.
- For Indian institutions, treat the institute's IP-cell approval as a gating step before any submission, thesis deposit or talk; DBT-funded work follows host-institute policy, not a mandatory filing-first rule.
