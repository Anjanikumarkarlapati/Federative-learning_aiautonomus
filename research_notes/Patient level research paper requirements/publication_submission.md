## 1. Authorship criteria and contributor statements

### Takeaway
ICMJE's four-criteria test is the common baseline. Journals differ on how contributions are recorded, with CRediT increasingly used for role statements. Several sources report a 2026 ICMJE revision that is not confirmed against icmje.org.

### Cited Findings
- ICMJE authorship requires meeting all four criteria: substantial contribution to conception/design or to data acquisition, analysis or interpretation; drafting or critically reviewing the manuscript; approval of the final version; agreement to be accountable for accuracy and integrity. People who do not meet all four should be acknowledged, not listed as authors. (Secondary; the April 2025 ICMJE PDF was the only text surfaced: [ICMJE recommendations PDF](https://www.icmje.org/icmje-recommendations.pdf); summary via [CASRAI ICMJE authorship criteria](https://www.casrai.org/guides/icmje-authorship-criteria))
- ICMJE changed the second criterion from "revising it critically" to "reviewing it critically" in its May 2023 update, per a secondary source. Older documents still use "revising". (Secondary, same CASRAI-linked search results)
- A secondary source reports ICMJE published a new edition in January 2026 that moved AI out of the authorship section into a standalone Section V and added a provision on authors' access to underlying data. **Unverified** from ICMJE itself: [Journal Metrics, ICMJE 2026 recommendations](https://www.journalmetrics.org/blog/icmje-2026-recommendations-authors)
- COPE and ICMJE both position AI tools as non-authors; humans are responsible for AI-assisted content. Secondary summary: [Editage, ICMJE recommendations on AI](https://www.editage.com/insights/the-icmje-recommendations-on-ai-advice-for-authors-and-peer-reviewers). Primary COPE text was not retrieved.
- CRediT (ANSI/NISO Z39.104) defines 14 contributor roles for statements. Role list from memory, **unverified this session**; primary source credit.niso.org was unreachable.
- NEJM, JAMA, Lancet and Nature Medicine all reportedly prohibit AI authorship (secondary sources; see Section 4).

### Inferences
- Practical default for a patient-level paper: list each human who meets all four ICMJE criteria as an author, put everyone else in Acknowledgements, and add a CRediT-style or journal-specific contribution statement where the target journal asks for one. Check which version of the ICMJE text the target journal cites.
- Because some sources say the 2026 ICMJE edition moved AI into its own section, any AI declaration should be drafted against the current ICMJE text rather than older summaries.

### Gaps
- Primary ICMJE 2026 text (not confirmed). Primary COPE AI position text (not retrieved). Primary CRediT role definitions (not retrieved). Journal-specific contribution-statement rules for NEJM, Lancet, BMJ, JAMA, Nature Medicine, npj Digital Medicine and PLOS ONE were not retrieved.

## 2. Required declarations: funding, competing interests, AI use, data and code availability

### Takeaway
Journals ask for funding, competing-interest, ethics, data-availability and AI-use statements at submission. Placement of AI disclosure differs by journal and is the main inconsistency found.

### Cited Findings
- ICMJE expects journals to ask at submission whether AI-assisted tools were used, and authors who used them must describe how in both the cover letter and the manuscript. Source: [Journal Metrics, ICMJE 2026 recommendations](https://www.journalmetrics.org/blog/icmje-2026-recommendations-authors) (secondary; the RSNA editorial reporting the May 2023 update is cited in [RSNA Radiology 10.1148/radiol.239024](https://pubs.rsna.org/doi/epdf/10.1148/radiol.239024))
- Placement guidance, as summarised by secondary sources: writing, editing, translation and language polishing go in Acknowledgements; AI used for data collection, analysis, classification or figure generation goes in Methods. Source: [Editage](https://www.editage.com/insights/the-icmje-recommendations-on-ai-advice-for-authors-and-peer-reviewers)
- AI tools should not be cited as references. Authors remain responsible for accuracy; AI text needs a plagiarism check. Source: [UT Southwestern library, AI publishing ICMJE](https://utsouthwestern.libguides.com/artificial-intelligence/ai-publishing-icmje)
- Reviewers who want to use AI must obtain editor permission (ICMJE, January 2024 update, as summarised in the same [Editage piece](https://www.editage.com/insights/the-icmje-recommendations-on-ai-advice-for-authors-and-peer-reviewers)).
- Journal AI policies (secondary; conflicting on placement):
  - **NEJM:** disclosure in cover letter and manuscript; one source places it in Methods, another in Acknowledgements; AI-created figures not permitted. Source: [Manusights, NEJM AI policy](https://manusights.com/blog/nejm-ai-policy)
  - **Lancet:** AI allowed for readability/language; disclosure in Methods per one source, Acknowledgements per another; AI images prohibited. Source: [Manusights, Lancet AI policy](https://manusights.com/blog/lancet-ai-policy); [Editage, 2026 Lancet AI guidelines](https://www.editage.com/insights/2026-lancet-ai-guidelines)
  - **JAMA:** all AI use disclosed in Methods; AI cannot be co-author. Source: [Manusights, JAMA AI policy](https://manusights.com/blog/jama-ai-policy)
  - **Nature Medicine:** follows Springer Nature rules, enforced more strictly for clinical content; AI authorship prohibited; disclosure in Methods; one 2026 guide lists copy-editing as permitted with disclosure, so the copy-editing rule is unclear. Source: [Manusights, Nature Medicine AI policy](https://manusights.com/blog/nature-medicine-ai-policy)
  - **Common ground across sources:** AI cannot be an author; AI use must be disclosed; AI-generated images generally barred.
- Comparison table of AI disclosure across Nature, Cell, NEJM, JAMA and BMJ: [CASRAI](https://casrai.org/news/ai-disclosure-comparing-nature-cell-nejm-jama-bmj); also [AI Cards, Göttingen](https://aicards.uni-goettingen.de/ai-disclosure-policies-by-journal/)
- Trial-level declarations (ethics/IRB statement, registration ID, funding, competing interests, data availability) are listed as submission requirements in Duke research-support slides, 2019 updated 2021: [Duke GR slides](https://dcricollab.dcri.duke.edu/sites/NIHKR/KR/GR-Slides-07-12-19.pdf)

### Inferences
- The AI-use disclosure should be written to satisfy the strictest placement in the target list (Methods for any analytic or figure-generating use, Acknowledgements for language help), and repeated in the cover letter.
- Data and code availability statements are standard but their exact form (repository, DOI, restricted access) was not confirmed for any target journal. For patient-level data, expect a restricted-access statement with a data-access route.

### Gaps
- Primary author-instruction text for all seven named journals was not retrieved. The conflicting placement claims above must be resolved from each journal's own page.
- Funding and COI disclosure format per journal (ICMJE form, ICMJE disclosure forms, journal-specific forms) not verified.
- Data and code availability wording requirements per journal not verified.

## 3. Registration, protocol and SAP publication

### Takeaway
Prospective interventional studies need an ICMJE-recognised registry ID in the manuscript. Protocol and SAP publication is a common expectation for clinical prediction and trial-derived papers, but no source retrieved confirmed a journal-wide mandate for SAP posting.

### Cited Findings
- medRxiv expects clinical trial registration information and ethics oversight; secondary CASRAI guides describe a trial ID from an ICMJE-recognised registry as the requirement, and that screening checks for ethics and registration. Sources: [CASRAI, medRxiv submission clinical](https://casrai.org/news/medrxiv-submission-clinical); [CASRAI, medRxiv explained](https://casrai.org/guides/medrxiv-explained). The official medRxiv page retrieved is older and does not state a registration mandate directly: [medRxiv node/24](https://medrxiv.org/node/24)
- Duke slides: study registration in ClinicalTrials.gov or another ICMJE-approved registry for trials, PROSPERO for systematic reviews, and/or a link to the protocol. Source: [Duke GR slides](https://dcricollab.dcri.duke.edu/sites/NIHKR/KR/GR-Slides-07-12-19.pdf)
- University library guidance (Pitt): any clinical trial described should be registered with an internationally recognised registry and the trial ID included in the manuscript. Source: [Pitt HSLS update](https://info.hsls.pitt.edu/updatereport/?p=12183)
- TRIPOD+AI, the current reporting guideline for clinical prediction models using regression or ML, includes study registration and sample size justification as items practitioners often omit (secondary blog claim). TRIPOD+AI supersedes TRIPOD 2015. Source: [TRIPOD+AI, PMC11025451](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11025451/); blog summary: [Journal Metrics, TRIPOD+AI](https://www.journalmetrics.org/blog/tripod-ai-clinical-prediction-model-reporting)

### Inferences
- For a prediction-model paper built on existing patient data, a trial registry ID is usually not applicable; a pre-specified protocol (e.g. OSF or a TRIPOD+AI-referenced analysis plan) is the closest expected step. Confirm with the target journal.
- For any interventional component, register before enrolment and cite the ID in the abstract and Methods.

### Gaps
- Journal-specific rules on protocol and SAP publication (e.g. whether an SAP must be posted or supplied) not retrieved.
- Results-posting deadlines on ClinicalTrials.gov not verified in this session (background knowledge says results posting is due within 1 year of primary completion for applicable trials; **unverified**).

## 4. Patient and public involvement (PPI) reporting: GRIPP2

### Takeaway
GRIPP2 is the international reporting guideline for PPI, with a long form (primary focus on PPI) and a short form (PPI secondary). BMJ asks authors to consider GRIPP2 and requires a PPI statement in Methods for research articles (secondary report).

### Cited Findings
- GRIPP2 short form is for studies where PPI is a secondary or tertiary focus; the long form (34 items) is for studies centred on PPI. Developed via EQUATOR methods with a three-round Delphi (143 / 123 / 112 respondents) and a face-to-face consensus meeting. Sources: [PMC secondary summary, GRIPP2 table](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6196421/table/Tab1); [NIHR ARC NWC GRIPP2 form and guidance](https://arc-nwc.nihr.ac.uk/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/ARC-NWC-GRIPP2-Form-and-Guidance_24.08.23-1.pdf)
- The checklists were published in BMJ 2017 (Staniszewska et al., BMJ 2017;358:j3453). Source: [BMJ, GRIPP2 article](https://www.bmj.com/node/948931) (secondary listing)
- BMJ was reported as the first major journal (2015) to require a PPI statement in Methods for all research articles; the journal advises authors to consider GRIPP2. Source: [NIHR ARC NWC guidance](https://arc-nwc.nihr.ac.uk/wp-content/uploads/2023/08/ARC-NWC-GRIPP2-Form-and-Guidance_24.08.23-1.pdf) and [Reporting involvement toolkit](https://involvementtoolkit.clinicaltrialsalliance.org.au/toolkit/evaluating/reporting-involvement/)
- Some journals decline to make GRIPP2 items mandatory and only encourage authors to consider them (example cited: Research Involvement and Engagement). Source: [BioMed Central blog, New guidance for reporting PPI](https://blogs.biomedcentral.com/on-medicine/2017/08/02/new-guidance-for-reporting-patient-and-public-involvement-in-research/)

### Inferences
- For a patient-level ML or clinical paper, the short form is the usual fit. The PPI section should state who was involved, when in the research cycle, how, and what changed as a result.

### Gaps
- Current BMJ and other target-journal PPI wording not retrieved. Whether GRIPP2 is mandatory at each journal is unconfirmed.

## 5. Preprint policies (medRxiv, bioRxiv, arXiv)

### Takeaway
medRxiv screens for ethics and trial registration; arXiv tightened some rules in late 2025 to early 2026. Preprint policy for a clinical paper must be checked against the target journal, since some journals restrict prior posting.

### Cited Findings
- medRxiv: trial registration and ethics checks at screening (secondary, see Section 3).
- arXiv (secondary, CASRAI 2026 editorial): between October 2025 and February 2026, arXiv required documented prior peer review for Computer Science surveys and position papers, stopped accepting endorsement based only on an institutional email, and required a full English version (machine translation permitted). The peer-review rule reportedly applies to the CS category; cs.CY was reported as not affected. Source: [CASRAI, arXiv 2026 endorsement changes](https://casrai.org/news/arxiv-2026-endorsement-english-ai-policy-changes)
- NeurIPS 2026 Position Paper Track requires substantially human-written text (AI only for copy-editing). Relevant only if a conference submission is planned. Source: [NeurIPS blog](https://blog.neurips.cc/author/jeankossaifi/) (secondary listing)

### Inferences
- A preprint of a patient-level paper on medRxiv is the usual route for clinical content; arXiv is more relevant for ML method papers. Verify each server's current policy on its own site before posting.

### Gaps
- Official arXiv and bioRxiv policy pages were not retrieved. Whether preprint posting prevents submission at specific journals (NEJM, Lancet, BMJ) was not verified.

## 6. Reporting checklists, structured abstracts, word and figure limits

### Takeaway
TRIPOD+AI is the core checklist for clinical prediction models using regression or ML and has a separate 13-item abstract checklist. Word, figure and reference limits were not retrieved for any journal.

### Cited Findings
- TRIPOD+AI: 27-item checklist, expanded checklist with per-item detail, and a 13-item TRIPOD+AI for Abstracts checklist; supersedes TRIPOD 2015. Source: [TRIPOD+AI, PMC11025451](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11025451/); [Journal Metrics blog](https://www.journalmetrics.org/blog/tripod-ai-clinical-prediction-model-reporting)
- Other reporting guidelines commonly required for trials and studies (CONSORT, SPIRIT, STROBE, CONSORT-AI, SPIRIT-AI) are not verified in this session.

### Inferences
- Submit the TRIPOD+AI checklist with the manuscript, and fill the abstract checklist for the structured abstract.

### Gaps
- Word limits, abstract word caps, figure and table limits, and reference limits per journal: not retrieved.

## 8. Common causes of desk rejection and major revision (clinical and ML-on-patient-data papers)

### Takeaway
No documented rejection-rate dataset was found. The strongest sourced signal is incomplete reporting against TRIPOD+AI; the rest is inference from the checklist scope.

### Cited Findings
- The BMJ authors argue that incomplete reporting can mask design, data-collection or conduct flaws that could cause harm if a model is deployed. Source: [TRIPOD+AI, BMJ 2024;385:e078378 (PMC11025451)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11025451/)
- A practitioner blog says the most-omitted items are study registration, sample size justification and fairness analysis. Its quantitative adherence figure (about 28% for ML papers) had no verifiable citation and should not be used. Source: [Journal Metrics blog](https://www.journalmetrics.org/blog/tripod-ai-clinical-prediction-model-reporting)
- A systematic review of orthopaedic AI papers reportedly found no study fully met abstract reporting requirements (secondary report, blog).

### Inferences (my inference, not from sources)
- Likely desk-rejection triggers: no TRIPOD+AI checklist; abstract not meeting the abstract checklist; no sample-size justification; no protocol or registration where applicable; no fairness or subgroup analysis; unclear handling of leakage, tuning and missing data; no calibration or external validation; code, data or model specification unavailable; AI use undisclosed or disclosed in the wrong section; missing ethics or consent statement.

### Gaps
- No primary editorial or study quantifying desk-rejection reasons for clinical AI papers was found. The list above must be validated against editorials from the target journals.

## Cross-cutting flags: where policies differ
- AI disclosure placement: Methods (JAMA, Lancet per one source, NEJM per one source, Nature Medicine) vs Acknowledgements (Lancet and NEJM per other sources). Sources conflict; check each journal.
- Copy-editing with AI: Nature Medicine allowed-with-disclosure per one 2026 guide, prohibited per another. Unclear.
- PPI: BMJ asks for a PPI statement; some journals only encourage GRIPP2.
- arXiv: CS category requires prior peer review for surveys and position papers; cs.CY reportedly exempt.
