# Build the evidence before filing in India

An Indian application for this federated-learning autonomous-vehicle (FL-AV) framework needs five forms at minimum (Form 1, Form 2, Form 3, Form 5 and Form 18). It also needs a Form 2 specification with drawings, at most 10 claims and an abstract of no more than 150 words. IP India never asks for a working prototype or source code at filing. What decides the application is evidence that has to be written into the specification. Section 10(4) of the Patents Act requires a description full enough, with the best method and a working example, that a skilled engineer can reproduce the invention. Section 3(k), applied through the CRI Guidelines 2025, requires a technical effect that can be demonstrated. In *Kroll* (2025) the Delhi High Court refused a network-software invention because it lacked "demonstrable technical advancement", and in *Caleb Suresh Motupalli* (2025) the Madras High Court refused an AI application that had no working examples. The repository already gives a reproducible implementation and an exact byte count: an **88.3% cut in uplink-plus-downlink traffic per round against standard FedAvg (8.71 vs 74.56 MB)**. It is short of what an examiner will test in four ways. Every result comes from **one seed**. **V2I airtime and latency are computed but thrown away**. There is **no hardware embodiment and there are no patent drawings**. The candidate claim with the best chance of novelty, quality-weighted secure aggregation that keeps each vehicle's quality score hidden, is **not implemented yet**. Three code defects would also make a specification written from the README over-claim. So the build work runs in a fixed order: disclosure and inventorship records first, then the defect fixes, then latency and multi-seed evidence, then the two claimable mechanisms, then drawings and the specification package. The provisional specification should be filed only once the claimed mechanisms work and have been measured. India gives no grace period for a preprint or a public GitHub repository, so nothing should be published before that filing date. *This is research synthesis, not legal advice. Most legal sources were read through search summaries because the IP India pages could not be opened. Fees and the final CRI 2025 wording must be checked against the primary texts on the day of filing.*

## Five forms, a 12-month clock and about INR 8,100 in official fees

The filing package is short and has fixed deadlines. Form 1 is the application. Form 2 holds the specification, whether provisional or complete; Rule 13(1) says "every specification... shall be made in Form 2" ([IP India Rule 13](https://ipindia.gov.in/writereaddata/Portal/ev/rules/pr13.html)). Form 5 names the true and first inventors. Form 3 is the statement and undertaking about foreign filings. Since the **Patents (Amendment) Rules 2024 took effect on 15 March 2024**, Form 3 may be filed with the application or within six months of it, and an updated Form 3 is due within three months of the First Examination Report ([Conventus on the 2024 Rules](https://conventuslaw.com/report/india-announces-progressive-changes-in-patents-rules-2003/)). The same Rules cut the deadline for the Form 18 request for examination **from 48 to 31 months** from the priority date ([Banana IP](https://www.bananaip.com/intellepedia/patent-amendment-rules-2024-key-changes-india/)). If it is missed, the application is deemed withdrawn. The **complete specification must follow a provisional within 12 months**, or the application is treated as abandoned ([IP India s.9](https://ipindia.gov.in/writereaddata/Portal/ev/sections/ps9.html)).

Two inventors filing as natural persons, with at most 10 claims and 30 pages, pay about **INR 8,100 in official fees up to examination**: INR 1,600 + 2,500 + 4,000. Agent fees are extra. Expedited examination under Form 18A is open only on Rule 24C grounds: a startup, a small entity, at least one female natural-person applicant, a government entity, or India chosen as the PCT search authority ([IP India Rule 24C](https://www.ipindia.gov.in/acts/patent-rules-2003/rule-24c)). That matters because the average reported time to a First Examination Report is about **5.7 months expedited against 24.7 months ordinary**. Inventors must be natural persons. The Indian Patent Office refused the DABUS application on 15 April 2026 because an AI system cannot be an inventor ([SpicyIP](https://spicyip.com/2026/04/the-inventor-is-still-human-indian-patent-offices-dabus-refusal.html)). That decision bears directly on this repository: every commit is authored by "Claude", the requirements document behind the design is not in the repository, and the repository owner account is not the same as the commit email's user. Inventors in India must also file there first. Under s.39 they cannot file abroad until they have filed in India and waited six weeks without a secrecy direction, or obtained Form 25 permission. The order that keeps novelty intact is: settle inventorship and ownership; file the Indian provisional; only then post a preprint or open the repository; within 12 months, file the complete specification (with Form 18 at the same time, to stay clear of the 31-month deadline) and any PCT application.

## The specification must carry the prototype, because the prototype never reaches the examiner

Section 10(3) allows the Controller to *ask* for a model or sample, but that model "shall not be deemed to form part of the specification". Rule 16 limits such requests to cases where the Controller requires one ([IP India s.10](https://ipindia.gov.in/writereaddata/Portal/ev/sections/ps10.html)). So no code is filed. The risk lies elsewhere. In IPA 1451/MUM/2013 the Controller asked for experimental data or a working model, none was supplied, and the application was refused ([Mondaq on models and samples](https://webiis08.mondaq.com/india/patent/1136566/explaining-inventions-better-models-and-samples-in-indian-patent-applications)). Section 10(4) requires a full and particular description, the **best method known to the applicant**, and claims that are fairly based on the disclosure. The Delhi High Court treats each of these requirements as mandatory. The IPAB has accepted one clearly described way of working the invention together with the best method ([Mondaq on sufficiency](https://webiis08.mondaq.com/india/patent/1139820/meeting-sufficiency-of-disclosure-requirements-under-the-indian-patent-act-1970)). For AI inventions, commentators reading the CRI Guidelines 2025 expect disclosure of the **model architecture, the characteristics of the training data, the pre-processing, the training parameters, and validation results that demonstrate the claimed effect**. A specification that says what the model does without saying how is "unlikely to clear examination" on sufficiency ([Intepat](https://www.intepat.com/blog/intellectual-property/artificial-intelligence-ipr/)). Industry bodies lobbied for disclosure of data *characteristics* rather than the datasets themselves ([BananaIP](https://www.bananaip.com/intellepedia/comments-on-draft-cri-guidelines-ai-ml-blockchain/)). That suits this project, whose synthetic fleet can be described fully by its generator parameters: Dirichlet α = 0.4, region-by-weather skew, and about 12% of vehicles de-calibrated with 25–45% label corruption.

The complete Form 2 runs in this order: title, field, background, objects, summary, brief description of drawings, detailed description with the best method, claims under "We claim:", and a separate abstract. The abstract must start with the title, stay within **150 words**, and name a reference figure with its reference signs (Rule 13(7)) ([Banana IP abstract guidelines](https://www.bananaip.com/intellepedia/indian-patent-office-abstract-writing-guidelines-technical-requirements/)). Rule 15 sets the drawing format: A4 sheets with **margins of at least 4 cm at the top and left and 3 cm at the bottom and right**, no dimensions, consecutive sheet numbers, and the applicant's name at the top left ([IP India Rule 15](https://ipindia.gov.in/writereaddata/portal/ev/rules/pr15.html)). Descriptive text is not allowed on drawings except in flow diagrams, and reference numerals must tie each element to the text ([iPleaders](https://blog.ipleaders.in/patent-drawing-importance/)). The 14 figures in `docs/figures/` are colour validation plots, and the README has an ASCII architecture sketch. Neither can be filed. Both have to be redrawn in black and white with reference numerals. The figure set should also include what the repository lacks entirely: hardware block diagrams of the vehicle's on-board unit (OBU) and of the roadside unit (RSU). Because novel hardware is no longer required, these units can be conventional. They still need to be recited as the technical means on which the effect is measured.

One timing point decides the build order. **A claim not fairly based on the provisional takes the later date**, and results added after the provisional cannot reach back to support claims filed earlier. The provisional should therefore be written as fully as a complete specification. It should include at least preliminary measured results for every mechanism it claims. A mechanism that exists only as an idea when the provisional is filed gains no priority date from it.

## Section 3(k) rewards measured network effects, not accuracy points

The CRI Guidelines 2025 were finalised on **29 July 2025** and replace the 2017 version ([IP India public notice](https://ipindia.gov.in/uploads/52c49fb5-31ee-4630-bb7d-52bddc4fb827_Public%20Notice.pdf)). They drop the old "novel hardware" requirement, following *Raytheon* (Delhi HC, 2023), and ask instead for a technical solution to a technical problem, achieved by technical means ([Lexology](https://www.lexology.com/library/detail.aspx?g=3c84cc89-5947-4dc8-be2b-762d2d6c9603)). They list recognised technical effects, including **improved transmission or reception of signals, higher processing speed, reduced memory access, real-time monitoring and control of a device, and enhanced security in a computer network** ([Bar & Bench](https://www.barandbench.com/law-firms/view-point/revised-cri-guidelines-2025)). They also warn that if any claim category (method, system or computer-readable medium) falls within the exclusion, the subject matter fails ([Intepat](https://www.intepat.com/blog/cri-guidelines-2025-patent-india-3k)). The courts split on the facts of each case. *Ericsson v Lava* (2024) upheld claims whose algorithms enhance how a system functions ([BananaIP](https://bananaip.com/?p=101685)). *Microsoft* (Madras HC, 2024) and *Comviva* (Delhi HC, 2024) treated network and authentication security as a technical contribution ([SpicyIP](https://spicyip.com/2024/07/madras-hc-on-computer-program-and-3k-following-the-dhc-way.html)). Against those, *Kroll* (Delhi HC, 1 July 2025) refused a peer-to-peer networking system built on standard components because it showed no "demonstrable technical advancement" ([Verdictum](https://www.verdictum.in/court-updates/high-courts/delhi-high-court/kroll-information-assurance-llc-v-the-controller-general-of-patents-designs-and-trademarks-2025-dhc-5096-1583129)). *Caleb Suresh Motupalli* (Madras HC, 29 January 2025) upheld a refusal of an AI application whose specification had no working examples, with s.3(k), s.10(4) and s.10(5) all in play ([LiveLaw](https://www.livelaw.in/amp/tech-law/madras-high-court-upholds-rejection-of-ai-human-integration-patent-claim-283918)). *Kroll* is the closest risk for this invention. It too is a network data-processing scheme on ordinary hardware, so the measured effect on the network has to be the contribution the application argues. Business framing should be avoided entirely, such as fleet cost savings or a data-sharing service. *OpenTV* treats the business-method bar as absolute ([AsiaIPLaw](https://asiaiplaw.com/sector/patents/india-the-delhi-high-courts-decision-in-opentv)).

That fixes which numbers the repository has to produce. Accuracy is the weakest argument: **82.17% against 81.50% best-round**, which the README itself calls within noise. The worst-served vehicle (40.8% against 32.7%) reversed direction between runs. The effects an examiner will accept are those on the system, each set against standard FedAvg with the same seeds:

- **Uplink bytes per round, and bytes needed to reach a target accuracy.** This maps to improved signal transmission. The repository measures it exactly.
- **V2I airtime and round completion time under a standard channel model.** This maps to reduced latency. The repository computes it in `SecureChannel._transfer` but discards it at `fedav/sim/runner.py:204`. The `round_seconds` column records host CPU time, not latency.
- **Robustness of the aggregate as the share of degraded-sensor vehicles rises.** This maps to reliability and efficient signal processing.
- **No raw-data egress, plus differential privacy and secure aggregation.** This maps to enhanced network security.

General ML practice calls for several seeds with reported variance ([arXiv 2406.14325](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.14325)). Federated-learning papers commonly run 3–9 seeds with bootstrap confidence intervals. No Indian source sets a minimum number of seeds; the multi-seed requirement comes from credibility, not statute. A standards-based channel model is cheap and reads as technical to an examiner. 3GPP TR 37.885 gives V2X path loss for highway line-of-sight as PL = 32.4 + 20 log10(d) + 20 log10(fc) dB with 3 dB shadowing ([TR 37.885 §6.2.1](https://www.itecspec.com/3gpp/37.885/s/6.2.1)), and the same model is implemented in ns-3 ([ns-3 ThreeGppV2vPropagationLossModel](https://www.nsnam.org/docs/release/3.42/doxygen/d8/de1/three-gpp-v2v-propagation-loss-model_8cc_source.html)). It turns the byte saving into seconds per round and a deadline-hit rate in pure Python. A real-image run would answer the "synthetic toy" objection. GTSRB is the cheapest option: 43 classes, a torchvision loader, and reported as CC0 ([Armory](https://armory.readthedocs.io/en/latest/dataset_licensing/)). The India Driving Dataset would be the most persuasive for an Indian filing, but it is about 22.8 GB and its licence is unconfirmed ([Dataset Ninja](https://datasetninja.com/idd-detection)).

## Prior art leaves room only for mechanism-level claims the code does not yet fully implement

Federated learning across vehicles is already patented. The closest single reference is **Toyota US 12,258,045 B2** (filed 5 February 2021, granted 25 March 2025). In it, vehicles exchange sensor-quality metadata that decides which of them join training. A quoted dependent claim covers "quality of the specific available sensors" ([Google Patents](https://patents.google.com/patent/US12258045)). **US 2022/0414464 A1** weights updates by a data-quality index for each source ([Google Patents](https://patents.google.com/patent/US20220414464A1/en)). Selecting vehicles by predicted dwell time within an RSU's range has been published ([arXiv 2401.07224](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.07224)). Budgeted top-k sparsification ([Sahu et al. 2021](https://mcanini.github.io/papers/rethink-gs.neurips21.pdf)), pairwise-mask secure aggregation ([Bonawitz 2017](https://eprint.iacr.org/2017/281)) and DP-FedAvg with capped weights ([McMahan 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.06963)) are all textbook. A claim to any one ingredient, or to "machine learning on driving data", fails on novelty, obviousness or s.3(k).

The snippet-level search found two combinations in no single reference. **K1, quality-weighted secure aggregation that never reveals q_k.** Each vehicle computes its quality score on board, scales its clipped update by that score, and appends the score as an extra coordinate before masking. The RSU recovers only Σq_kΔ_k and Σq_k, and DP noise is calibrated to a cap on q_k. **K2, budget allocation driven by link uptime, with error feedback that survives failed uploads.** Vehicles are admitted when their predicted uptime covers training plus upload time. The per-round byte budget is shared out in proportion to predicted capacity × q_k. On a CRC failure or timeout the vehicle keeps its whole residual for the next round. Both rest on snippet-level searching. A registered agent must read the Toyota claims (including US 12,337,867 on communication-aware FL) and the MERL claims (US 12,369,186) before drafting ([Google Patents](https://patents.google.com/patent/US12369186)).

The repository does not yet support either claim as built. **K1 is not implemented.** The RSU computes the weights α_k in plaintext, and `_secure_sum` masks α_kΔ_k with seeds taken from the public config seed (`secret=self.cfg.seed`). **K2 is partial.** The bandwidth controller never fired in the committed runs: MB per round stayed between 7.35 and 8.92, against thresholds of 9.0 and 5.4. Three defects documented in the earlier audit are still present. Each would make a specification drafted from the README over-claim, which conflicts with the s.10(4) duty to describe the best method that actually works:

- **FedNova normalisation is silently overwritten whenever secure aggregation is on.** `runner.py` calls `combine()` and then replaces its output with `_secure_sum`, which averages the un-normalised deltas. The proposed arm runs with secure aggregation on.
- **The "fairness guard" does not keep its stated bound of one selection every 12 rounds.** 28 of 50 vehicles waited longer than that.
- **The "uniform-random" FedAvg baseline is in fact biased towards vehicles with good links.**

The claim set should be built on K1 and K2. It should have single-actor families (an RSU method, a vehicle OBU method and a system) that each recite the V2I transceiver, processor and memory, and an effect clause such as "such that the encoded payload does not exceed the uplink budget allocated to the vehicle". It should fit within 10 claims. A computer-readable-medium claim goes last or is left out. Under the 2025 Guidelines a weak claim of any one category can sink the whole subject matter.

## Conclusion

The Indian route is cheap in fees and strict about evidence, and the evidence has to be fixed in the provisional specification before any public disclosure. That reverses the usual research habit of publishing first and patenting later. In practice it shifts effort away from writing forms and towards a few weeks of engineering. The forms are routine. What decides grant is whether the provisional already holds a working, measured description of a mechanism that Toyota, Bonawitz and Sahu do not cover. Today the repository's strongest number, the 88% byte saving, comes largely from fp16 encoding and multicast accounting against an uncompressed baseline. A transport-matched FedAvg baseline is what would separate the inventive contribution from known compression. The repository's best patent assets are K1 and K2, which a single focused build cycle could deliver. The current pipeline as a whole is a weaker asset. The open uncertainties are the exact final wording of the CRI 2025 AI chapter, whether results filed after the provisional can support an argument in a reply to the examination report, and full-claim prior art. A registered agent should settle all three before the complete specification is filed.
